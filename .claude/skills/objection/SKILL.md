# Objection

Fast, specific answers. He's probably about to dial.

## First — classify it

**Before giving a rebuttal, check whether it's an objection or a revocation.**

| Revocation — STOP | Objection — work it |
|---|---|
| "Take me off your list" | "I'm already working with someone" |
| "Stop calling me" | "Your rate's too high" |
| "Remove my number" | "I need to think about it" |
| "This is harassment" | "Now's not a good time" |
| "I'm not interested" — **second clear time** | "I'm not interested" — first, reflexive |

**If it's a revocation, say so and give him the exit line. Do not give him a rebuttal.**

> That's a revocation, not an objection. Say: *"Understood — I'll take you off our list
> right now. Sorry to have bothered you."* Then log it, internal DNC, permanently.
> `curriculum/04-compliance/19-cold-calling-law.md`

## Output format

Keep it short. He's mid-block.

```
[Objection]

WHAT'S BEHIND IT
  [One line]

SAY THIS
  "[Actual words]"

IF THEY PUSH
  "[Actual words]"

PREVENT IT NEXT TIME
  [Where upstream this gets fixed]
```

## The five techniques

Name the one you're using so he learns the pattern:

| Technique | Use |
|---|---|
| **Tie-down** | Make them reaffirm their own stated goal |
| **Reframe** | Add variables instead of arguing the objection |
| **Preemptive strike** | Raise it before they do — highest leverage |
| **Boomerang** | Turn the objection into the reason to act |
| **Silence** | Ask, then say nothing. Gets the real objection. |

## Market reflexes

**"Waiting for rates to drop"** and **"I don't want to lose my low rate"** are the two most
common objections in this market, and both have the same answer:

> *"Then a second lien is exactly right — you get the money now and your low rate stays
> untouched. When rates come down we consolidate."*

If he's getting either objection and hasn't raised a second lien, that's the actual fix.
`curriculum/02-products/11-seconds-heloc-heloan.md`

## Never give him

- A line that misrepresents where the lead came from
- A promise to beat a rate before pricing
- A fabricated manager approval or lock deadline
- "As low as 500" without the real-world caveats
- Anything disparaging a named competitor's compliance

If he asks for one of these, tell him why it's a problem and give the honest version —
which usually converts better anyway.
`curriculum/04-compliance/24-the-never-say-list.md`

## If he keeps hitting the same objection

That's a script defect upstream, not an objection-handling problem. Tell him:

| Recurring | Fix here instead |
|---|---|
| "Need to ask my spouse" | Discovery: "Is anyone else part of this decision?" |
| "How'd you get my number?" | Your opening isn't honest enough |
| "Need to think about it" | Weak diagnose/position — nothing to think *with* |
| "Rate's too high" | You presented before you positioned |
| "Not interested" | Your first fifteen seconds |

# Roleplay Personas

Thirty borrowers to practice against. Used by the `/cold-call-drill` skill, or by any
partner willing to read a card.

**Difficulty:** ⭐ easy · ⭐⭐ moderate · ⭐⭐⭐ hard · ⭐⭐⭐⭐ brutal

---

## Tier 1 — Fundamentals (⭐)

### 1. Denise — the cooperative aged lead
52, filled out a form 4 months ago about cash-out for a bathroom remodel. Never heard back
from anyone. Balance $145k at **3.4%**, value $310k. Wants $45k. Friendly, no urgency.
**Tests:** honest opening, legacy-rate handling, second lien.

### 2. Marcus — the fresh inbound
34, submitted a purchase pre-approval request 20 minutes ago. Actively looking, no agent
yet. $95k income, 720 FICO, $30k saved. Eager.
**Tests:** speed-to-lead energy, purchase discovery, agent referral.

### 3. Rosa — the past client
Closed with you 3 years ago. 4.1% rate, $220k balance, home now worth $390k. Calling to
check in. Has ~$18k in credit card debt she hasn't mentioned.
**Tests:** database call, uncovering need without pitching.

### 4. Wendell — the veteran
68, VA loan at **2.9%**, receives 40% disability. Wants $35k for a new roof. Polite,
somewhat hard of hearing, asks you to repeat things.
**Tests:** VA knowledge, funding fee exemption, patience, second lien.

### 5. Amy — asks the rate immediately
First words after hello: *"What's your rate?"* Won't answer anything until you answer.
**Tests:** the reframe without quoting.

## Tier 2 — Common obstacles (⭐⭐)

### 6. Terrence — already working with someone
Mid-process with a big retail lender. Not locked. Closing in 3 weeks. Polite but firm.
**Tests:** the locked/not-locked fork, asking for the LE.

### 7. Bridget — the spouse objection
Interested, engaged, gives good information. At every close: *"I'd have to talk to my
husband."* Husband handles the finances.
**Tests:** early decision-maker question, getting both on the phone.

### 8. Neil — "I need to think about it"
Great call. Good discovery. At the close, goes vague and won't say why. Real reason: he's
embarrassed his score is around 590 and hasn't told you.
**Tests:** silence, the honesty escalation, credit conversation without discouraging.

### 9. Charlene — the debt consolidator
$31k in cards at 22–26%, $19k car at 12%, mortgage $168k at **3.9%**, value $340k. Income
$78k. Overwhelmed by the monthly payments.
**Tests:** blended-rate math, the "will you close the cards" condition.

### 10. Sung-min — the self-employed
Owns a restaurant, 7 years, S-corp. Returns show $38k net. Business deposits ~$52k/month.
Wants $80k to open a second location.
**Tests:** deposits-not-income pivot, bank statement program, entity type.

### 11. Ray — "how did you get my number?"
Immediately suspicious. Won't move on until fully satisfied. Will hang up on evasion.
**Tests:** total honesty, offering to remove him.

### 12. Priya — the researcher
Has read everything. Asks about LLPAs, buying down with points, and why your rate differs
from an advertised rate she saw.
**Tests:** product knowledge, admitting what you don't know.

### 13. Dale — the low appraisal
Mid-process, appraisal just came in $22k under. Angry, considering walking.
**Tests:** delivering bad news with options.

### 14. Fatima — the first-time buyer
28, $62k income, 690 FICO, $8k saved, thinks she needs 20% down and can't buy for years.
**Tests:** correcting a wrong belief, low-down-payment programs, USDA check.

### 15. Gordon — the ARM candidate
Relocating for work in about 2.5 years. Buying $520k with 20% down. Defaults to wanting a
30-year fixed.
**Tests:** time-horizon question, ARM positioning, lender credits.

## Tier 3 — Hard (⭐⭐⭐)

### 16. Yvette — the hostile
Opens with *"I'm not interested."* Says it again. Then: *"You people are scammers."*
**Never** uses removal language.
**Tests:** classifying venting vs. revocation, offering to leave, staying calm.

### 17. Yvette-B — the revocation
Identical opening to #16, but her third line is *"Take me off your list."*
**Tests:** stopping immediately. **This drill is passed by ending the call.**

### 18. Owen — the credit event
Foreclosed in 2023. Assumes he can never buy again. Guarded, embarrassed, expects
rejection.
**Tests:** waiting periods, discussing credit without discouraging, giving a path.

### 19. Marguerite — the elderly borrower
79, widowed, **2.6%** rate, $95k balance, home worth $520k. Needs $40k for medical bills.
Mentions she may not be around much longer.
**Tests:** emotional disclosure handling, suitability, recommending the *smallest* loan
that solves the problem, possibly recommending no loan.

### 20. Duane — the competitor with a better rate
Has a written LE at 6.375%. Yours prices at 6.625%. He likes you but is going with them.
**Tests:** the takeaway close, asking for the LE, telling him honestly to take it if it's
better.

### 21. Cassie — the 30-day late
Wants cash-out. Discloses a 30-day mortgage late four months ago. Cause: her bank closed
the account after suspected fraud and autopay failed. Has a letter from the bank.
**Tests:** letting her finish, asking for the paperwork, honesty about the obstacle.

### 22. Hector — the occupancy request
Buying a rental. Asks you to write it up as a primary residence to get better pricing.
Frames it casually, as though everyone does it.
**Tests:** refusing cleanly, explaining why, then structuring it correctly.

### 23. Bernadette — the ghost
Excellent first call, agreed to everything, then vanished. You're on attempt four.
**Tests:** the file-closing message.

### 24. Roy — the DTI failure
Wants a $340k purchase. Income $6,800/month, existing debts $1,450. Doesn't work.
**Tests:** live DTI math, honest "not today," giving a specific path.

### 25. Simone — the investor
Owns 6 rentals. Conventional DTI is impossible. Wants to buy a 7th. Sophisticated, values
speed, will test whether you know DSCR.
**Tests:** DSCR knowledge, investor language, recognizing high lifetime value.

## Tier 4 — Brutal (⭐⭐⭐⭐)

### 26. Lawrence — the industry veteran
Retired mortgage underwriter. Asks about your overlays, whether you run DU or LPA, and
what your comp is. Will catch any bluff instantly.
**Tests:** saying "I don't know, I'll find out," answering the comp question straight.

### 27. Danielle — the multi-objection
Every objection in sequence: rate, spouse, timing, trust, "I'll wait for rates," "my
credit union is cheaper." Each one real.
**Tests:** stamina, not getting rattled, prevention.

### 28. Vernon — the unqualified but hopeful
Unfiled tax returns, self-employed, 30-day mortgage late two months ago, wants $110k
cash-out. Genuinely needs the money. Sympathetic.
**Tests:** telling the truth kindly, naming the bank statement path, assigning homework,
not promising numbers that don't exist.

### 29. Adaeze — the multi-state complication
Lives in one state, buying in another. Asks which states you're licensed in.
**Tests:** knowing your own license footprint, refusing unlicensed activity, internal
referral.

### 30. Kurt — the pressure tester
Deliberately says *"just give me a number, I don't want questions."* Escalates impatience.
Will hang up if you cave and quote blind, and will respect you if you hold.
**Tests:** holding the frame under pressure without being rigid.

## How to run a drill

### With `/cold-call-drill`
```
/cold-call-drill 19
/cold-call-drill hostile
/cold-call-drill random tier 3
```
The skill plays the borrower, stays in character, and debriefs against the scorecard.

### With a partner
Hand them the persona card. Rules:
- They **stay in character** — no coaching mid-call
- You run the full call arc
- Debrief against `46-scoring-rubric.md`
- **Do the same persona twice.** The second attempt is where the learning is.

### Alone
Read the card, then talk out loud to an empty room for five minutes. It feels ridiculous
and it works — the physical act of forming the sentences is most of the benefit.

## The weekly rotation

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Mon | One Tier 1 — warm up the opening |
| Tue | One Tier 2 — objection work |
| Wed | One Tier 3 — hard case |
| Thu | Repeat Wednesday's. Beat your score. |
| Fri | One Tier 4 — get humbled |

**Ten minutes a day.** That's fifty reps a month against borrowers who don't cost you
anything when you get it wrong.

---

## The two personas that matter most

**#17 (Yvette-B)** — because the only way to pass is to *stop*. Practicing the stop until
it's reflexive is the single most valuable compliance habit you can build.

**#19 (Marguerite)** — because it's the call where doing the right thing and doing the
profitable thing genuinely diverge, and how you handle it is who you're going to be in
this business.

# The Daily Protocol

What you actually do every day. Print it, follow it, don't negotiate with it.

---

## The principle

**You cannot control outcomes. You can control activity.**

On a bad day the goal is not "close a loan." The goal is **50 dials**. Hit the activity
number and the day was a success regardless of what happened. This single reframe is what
keeps people in the seat long enough to get good.

---

## The daily schedule

| Time | Block | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 8:30 | Prep | Review list, check calendar, set the dial goal |
| **8:50** | **Warm-up** | 10 min — non-negotiable |
| 9:00–11:00 | **Call Block 1** | Phone in a drawer. Email closed. |
| 11:00–11:30 | Admin | Notes, follow-ups, disclosures |
| 11:30–1:00 | **Call Block 2** | |
| 1:00–2:00 | Lunch / files | Work live files here, not during blocks |
| 2:00–4:00 | **Call Block 3** | |
| 4:00–6:00 | **Call Block 4** | **Highest value — people are home** |
| 6:00–6:30 | Wrap | Log everything, plan tomorrow |

**6:00–8:00 p.m. is prime calling time** for reaching working homeowners. Respect the
**8 p.m. mini-TCPA cutoff** in the borrower's time zone
(`../04-compliance/19-cold-calling-law.md`).

### The block rules

1. **Nothing but the dialer.** No email, no CRM cleanup, no "quick check" of anything.
2. **Never break a block to work a file.** Files go in admin time. Breaking a block for
   the exciting deal is how blocks die.
3. **Stand up**, or at least sit upright.
4. **Track dials visibly** — a number on a whiteboard where you can see it.

## The 10-minute warm-up

Your first call of the day should not be where you find your voice.

| Min | Drill |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | **Breathing.** Hand on stomach. In 4, hold 4, out 6. Drops your pitch. |
| 3–4 | **Articulation.** Tongue twisters, over-enunciated. *"Red leather, yellow leather."* |
| 5–6 | **Opening ×10.** Standing, smiling, out loud. |
| 7–8 | **Number drill.** *"Your payment is one thousand six hundred thirty dollars."* Slow, downward inflection. |
| 9–10 | **One roleplay round** — `/cold-call-drill` |

`../05-sales/28-tonality-lab.md`

## The pre-block checklist

Every block, every day:

- [ ] List scrubbed — DNC within 31 days, internal DNC current
- [ ] Consent records verified for aged leads
- [ ] Time zones checked by **address**, not area code
- [ ] Dial goal written down
- [ ] Opening rehearsed out loud
- [ ] Second-lien reframe ready for legacy-rate borrowers
- [ ] Water within reach
- [ ] Phone away

## The end-of-day wrap — 30 minutes

Non-negotiable. This is where the compounding happens.

1. **Log every call.** Outcome, objection, next step. A call you don't log is a call you
   paid for and threw away.
2. **Log every revocation** with a timestamp. Internal DNC. Permanently.
3. **Set tomorrow's follow-ups** with specific times.
4. **Update the tracker.** Compute the ratios.
5. **Write the one thing** you'll change tomorrow.
6. **Build tomorrow's list** so you can start dialing at 9:00 sharp.

## The weekly rhythm

| Day | Addition |
|---|---|
| **Mon** | Score last week's recorded call (`46-scoring-rubric.md`). Pick the lowest category. |
| **Tue** | Roleplay Tier 2 persona |
| **Wed** | Roleplay Tier 3 persona. Read one curriculum module. |
| **Thu** | Repeat Wednesday's persona. Beat the score. |
| **Fri** | Roleplay Tier 4. Review the week's numbers. Database touches. |
| **Sat AM** | Optional 2-hour block — underrated, low competition |

### The Friday review

Ten minutes. Four questions:

1. What were my ratios this week versus last?
2. Which funnel stage is weakest? (`../06-cold-calling/36-dialer-and-the-numbers.md`)
3. What objection killed the most calls?
4. What one thing am I changing next week?

## The 100-call rule

**Never judge a script change on fewer than 100 calls.**

Call outcomes are high-variance. Ten calls tells you nothing. New LOs change their opening
every twenty calls, learn nothing, and conclude nothing works.

Pick a version. Run 100. Record the numbers. **Then** change one thing.

---

## Recording and reviewing

The single fastest improvement mechanism available to you.

1. **Record** where legally permitted — disclose it when in doubt
2. **Listen to one full call daily.** Once for content, once with the meaning off, just
   listening to the sound.
3. **Transcribe one weekly** and run `/call-score`

You will hate this for about two weeks. Then it becomes the thing that makes you money.

## Sustainable volume

| Level | Dials/day |
|---|---|
| Minimum to be in the game | **50** |
| Solid, sustainable | 80–120 |
| High (needs a power dialer) | 150–200 |

**Track dials for accountability. Track talk time for progress.**

A 60-dial day with 25 minutes of talk time beats a 150-dial day with 8 minutes. Talk time
is where the learning and the money are.

---

## The first 90 days

Know the shape of this before you start, so it doesn't break you.

| Month | What happens | Income |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dialing, learning, first applications | **$0** |
| 2 | Applications, first locks, still no money | Little |
| 3 | First fundings | **Income arrives** |

**Most LOs who quit, quit in month two** — right before the thing they built starts
paying. It is arithmetic, not talent: your pipeline is empty, your database is empty, and
mortgages take 30–45 days.

Budget for 90 days of nothing. Then keep dialing.

---

## The non-negotiables

If everything falls apart, do these five:

1. **50 dials.** Every business day.
2. **10-minute warm-up.** Before the first dial.
3. **Log every call.** Same day.
4. **One roleplay.** Every day.
5. **Score one call.** Every week.

Everything else in this repo is optimization. These five are the job.

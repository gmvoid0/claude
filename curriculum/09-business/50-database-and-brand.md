# Database and Brand

The two assets that make year five easier than year one.

---

# The database

## Why it's the whole business

Post-trigger-lead-ban, with internet lead costs up **~45% year over year**, the database
is the most valuable asset in mortgage — and it's the only one that appreciates.

**The arithmetic:**

An LO with 300 past clients touched quarterly generates meaningful inbound refinance and
referral volume with **zero dialing**. An LO with 300 past clients they never contact
generates approximately none.

Identical inputs. The difference is entirely follow-up discipline, and it compounds every
year.

## Who goes in

**Everyone who ever gives you their phone number.** Including — especially — the ones who
said no.

| Segment | Cadence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Funded clients** | Quarterly, minimum | Refis, next purchase, referrals |
| **Applied, didn't close** | Monthly ×6, then quarterly | Circumstances change |
| **Quoted, said no** | Quarterly | Their reason expires |
| **Wrong timing** | At the stated time, then quarterly | **Best-converting segment** |
| **Closed elsewhere** | Quarterly | Their LO won't call them |
| **Rate-drop list** | **When rates move** | See below |
| **Revoked / DNC** | **Never** | Suppression. Permanent. Non-negotiable. |

That last row is a hard stop. Revocation does not decay
(`../04-compliance/19-cold-calling-law.md`).

## The rate-drop trigger list

**The highest-value list you will build**, and it costs nothing.

Every borrower who says *"I'll wait for rates to drop"* goes on it, with their current
rate and their stated target.

When the market moves, you call everyone in one afternoon:

> *"Michael, you told me in August you'd move if you saw a six and a quarter. It's there
> today. Want me to lock it?"*

Easiest call in the business. And in a market projected to reach **6.2–6.5% by year end
2026**, this list is going to pay.

**A "no" today is inventory. Treat it that way.**

## Data hygiene

For every record:
- Name, phone, email, property address
- Current rate, balance, loan type, servicer
- **What they wanted and why**
- What stopped it
- Their stated trigger ("call me at 6.25")
- Consent status and source
- Every contact, dated

**A CRM you don't update is a spreadsheet you're paying for.** The 30-minute daily wrap
(`../08-drills/44-daily-protocol.md`) is what keeps it alive.

## The touch rule

**Every touch carries something.** "Just checking in!" is worthless and signals you have
nothing to offer.

- A market move
- A guideline change
- A comp in their neighborhood
- A PMI cancellation opportunity
- A question only they can answer

**The PMI call is the best one:**

> *"You put 5% down three years ago and prices in your area are up substantially. You may
> already be under 80% and paying mortgage insurance you don't owe. Call your servicer and
> ask what it takes to remove it — might be $180 a month, costs you nothing but an
> appraisal fee."*

You made no money. You made a client for life, and they'll tell people.

## Ownership

**Read your employment agreement.** Know whether the CRM is yours or theirs, and what you
can take with you (`../04-compliance/23-your-license-and-career.md`).

Then keep your own records of what you're legally entitled to keep. Find this out in week
one, before you've built something worth taking.

# Brand

Slowest to build, cannot be banned, cannot be outbid.

## The realistic version

You are not going to become an influencer, and you shouldn't try. What you're building is
narrower and more useful:

> **When someone in your world thinks "mortgage," they think of you.**

That's it. It doesn't require a large audience — it requires being visible and useful to
the few hundred people who might actually transact or refer.

## What actually works for a new LO

### 1. Be findable and verifiable
- Complete LinkedIn with your **NMLS ID**
- Google Business profile
- Reviews from every closed client — **ask at closing, when they're happiest**

**Reviews matter more than content.** A borrower deciding whether to trust you looks for
proof other people did.

### 2. Teach one thing repeatedly
Pick the thing you know best and become the person who explains it.

In this market the obvious choice: **"how to get cash out without giving up your 3%
rate."** Almost nobody is explaining second liens well, and it's the question millions of
homeowners actually have.

### 3. Show up where the referral partners are
Open houses, brokerage meetings, local business groups. In-person beats posting for a new
LO, by a wide margin.

### 4. Be useful in writing
A short monthly email to your database. Not a newsletter — a note.

> *Rates came down about a quarter this week. Not enough to matter if you're sitting on a
> 3%, but second-lien pricing improved too. No action needed, just keeping you posted.*

**"No action needed" is why people keep opening it.**

## Compliance for anything public

**Everything you post is advertising**, and it's regulated
(`../04-compliance/23-your-license-and-career.md`).

- **NMLS ID** on marketing materials
- Company name as licensed
- **No unsubstantiated rate claims.** "Lowest rates in town" is a Reg N / MAP Rule problem.
- Any specific rate needs the required disclosures
- **Get company approval** before publishing anything — most shops require it
- Equal Housing Opportunity logo where required
- Never imply government affiliation

**Advertising violations are one of the top causes of MLO discipline**, and social media
is where new LOs get caught. When in doubt, ask compliance. It costs you a day.

## What not to do

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| Post rates without disclosures | Advertising violation |
| Claim to be the cheapest | Unsubstantiated, and false |
| Use client stories without written permission | Privacy, and possibly UDAAP |
| Buy followers | Everyone can tell |
| Post political content | Cuts your addressable market for nothing |
| Trash competitors | Reads as insecurity — see Call 3 |

## The long arc

| Year | Where business comes from |
|---|---|
| 1 | 70% cold, 20% database/referral, 10% inbound |
| 2 | 40% cold, 45% database/referral, 15% inbound |
| 3+ | 15% cold, 65% database/referral, 20% inbound |

**Every cold call you make in year one is an investment in year three** — not because of
the deal, but because of the database entry.

That reframe is worth holding onto on a bad day. **The call that didn't close still
produced an asset**, as long as you logged it.

---

## What you must be able to do

- Explain why the database is the most valuable asset in this business
- Segment a database and set cadences
- Maintain a rate-drop trigger list
- Make a value touch that isn't a check-in
- Ask for reviews at closing
- Know the advertising rules before you post anything

**Self-check:** You've been at this six months, 180 people in the CRM, no referral
partners, all business from purchased leads. What's the single highest-leverage thing to
do this month?

> *Call all 180. Every one. Segment them as you go: who closed elsewhere (refi and referral
> list), who never closed (still live), who said "call me when rates drop" (trigger list).
> Ask everyone who closed elsewhere for a referral. That's maybe two days of dialing
> against a list you already paid for, and it will produce more than a month of new cold
> leads — while building the asset that eventually replaces them.*

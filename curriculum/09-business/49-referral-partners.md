# Referral Partners

How you stop cold calling. Slower to build than dialing, and it compounds.

**Read `../04-compliance/22-respa-ecoa-udaap-fair-lending.md` before you act on any of
this.** RESPA Section 8 governs every relationship in this document and carries criminal
penalties.

---

## The RESPA line — first, because it matters most

**You may not give anything of value in exchange for referrals.**

| ❌ Never | ✅ Fine |
|---|---|
| Pay per referral | Buy coffee occasionally (nominal, untied) |
| Pay their marketing costs | **Co-marketing** where each pays fair market value |
| Give free leads, CRM, or admin help | Genuine co-branded material, proportional split |
| Sponsor their client event outright | Pay fair market value to exhibit |
| Below-market desk rent | **Fair market** desk rent, documented |

**The test:** *am I paying for something of real, independent value at a fair price — or
am I paying for the referrals?* If the payment stops being worth it the moment referrals
stop, it's a kickback.

**Marketing Services Agreements** are legal in principle and heavily scrutinized in
practice. **Never enter one without compliance review.**

## Why referrals beat cold calls

| | Cold lead | Referral |
|---|---|---|
| Trust at first contact | Zero | **Inherited** |
| Conversion | ~1–2% of dials | Often 50%+ |
| Cost | Rising ~45% YoY | Free |
| Repeatability | One at a time | **Ongoing flow** |
| Competition | 10 other callers | Usually none |

One good agent relationship can produce 2–3 deals a month indefinitely. That's the
equivalent of a large amount of dialing, permanently.

**But it takes 3–6 months to build.** Which is exactly why you cold call in the meantime —
cold calling funds the runway that referral building requires.

## Real estate agents

Your largest source.

### The reality

The average agent does very few transactions a year. **The top 10% do most of the volume.**
Chasing every agent is a waste; target deliberately.

### How to find the right ones

- **MLS data** — who actually has listings and closings in your area
- **Your own closings** — the agent on your last purchase already knows you
- **Open houses** — go on Sunday. Genuinely effective and almost nobody does it.
- **Brokerage meetings** — ask to present something useful

### What agents actually want

Not your rate sheet. Ask any of them:

1. **Deals that close on time** — their commission depends on it
2. **Pre-approvals they can trust** — a blown pre-approval wastes weeks of their work
3. **Answers on nights and weekends** — that's when offers happen
4. **Communication** — they hate being surprised
5. **Someone who makes them look good** to their client

**Rate is maybe fifth.** New LOs lead with rate and get ignored.

### The approach that works

❌ *"Hi, I'm a loan officer, I'd love to be on your preferred lender list."*

✅ Lead with something genuinely useful:

> *"I'm [Name], I'm a broker with [Company]. I'm not going to pitch you — I know you've
> got lenders. I wanted to show you one thing that's winning offers right now. Sellers are
> getting asked for price reductions, and most of them don't know a seller-paid buydown
> costs them the same and is worth far more to the buyer. I've got a one-page breakdown
> that shows the math. Want me to send it? If it's useful, use it. If not, no harm."*

**You gave them a tool to close more deals.** That's how the relationship starts.

### The buydown pitch — your best agent tool right now

> *"Instead of your buyer asking for $15,000 off, have them ask for a $15,000 rate
> buydown. Costs the seller exactly the same. But a price cut saves the buyer about $90 a
> month — the buydown saves them $400 a month for the first two years, right when money's
> tightest after moving in. It's an easier ask, and it closes deals that would otherwise
> stall on payment."*

`../02-products/12-arms-buydowns-points.md`

### Keeping them

- **Answer your phone.** Nights, weekends, always. This alone beats most competitors.
- **Pre-approve properly.** Pull credit, verify income. A pre-approval that falls apart
  destroys the relationship permanently.
- **Update proactively** — the agent should never have to ask
- **Never surprise them.** Bad news immediately, with options.
- **Send them business.** Buyers without an agent, referred honestly. This is the single
  strongest thing you can do — and it must be a genuine referral, not a paid arrangement.

## Other referral sources

Underused, less competitive, and often higher quality.

### CPAs and tax preparers
- Know exactly who's self-employed with strong cash flow
- Understand the bank statement borrower problem intimately
- **The approach:** teach them about bank statement programs. Most CPAs don't know these
  exist, and they have clients being told no by banks every year.

### Financial planners
- Know clients with assets and liquidity needs
- Understand asset depletion and debt consolidation math
- Care about suitability, so they respond well to an advisor posture

### Divorce and estate attorneys
- Constant refinance need — buyouts, equity splits, property transfers
- Emotionally difficult transactions that require patience
- Low competition

### Insurance agents
- Talk to homeowners constantly
- Natural reciprocal referral relationship
- Especially valuable in markets where insurance is a deal obstacle

### Builders
- Consistent volume
- Often want a preferred lender relationship
- **Watch RESPA carefully** here — builder arrangements get scrutinized

### Home improvement contractors
- Their customers frequently need financing
- Direct fit for HELOCs and fixed seconds
- Almost nobody works this channel

## Your past clients — the highest-yield source

Better than any agent, and you already have them.

**The EBR from a transaction runs 18 months**
(`../04-compliance/20-the-trigger-lead-ban.md`), and the relationship runs forever.

### The quarterly call

> *"Michael, it's [Name] — I did your loan back in [year]. How's the house treating you?"*
>
> *[genuine conversation]*
>
> *"Reason I'm calling — I go through my past clients every quarter and check whether
> anything's changed I should be telling them about. Any big expenses coming? Anything
> building up on credit cards? Still planning to be there another five years?"*

**No pitch.** The service is the pitch.

### The referral ask

The best moment is **right after closing**, when they're happiest:

> *"Glad this went smoothly. One favor — most of my business comes from people like you
> rather than advertising. If anyone you know is buying or refinancing, I'd love the
> introduction. And put my number in your phone, because when rates come down I'll be
> calling you before your servicer does."*

**Also ask people who closed elsewhere.** Someone who just went through a mortgage has
been talking about mortgages with everyone they know for two months. It's the
highest-yield referral moment available and almost nobody asks
(`../06-cold-calling/35-aged-lead-playbook.md`).

## The 90-day build

| Weeks | Action |
|---|---|
| **1–4** | Identify 20 target agents from MLS data. Attend 2 open houses/week. Build the buydown one-pager. |
| **5–8** | First contact with 20 agents, leading with the tool. Follow up with the 5 who engage. |
| **9–12** | Regular value contact with the 5. Attend one brokerage meeting. Ask for one opportunity. |

**Expect 2–3 real relationships from 20 contacts.** That's a good outcome, and it's worth
more than a month of dialing.

---

## The mix shift

| Source | Month 3 | Month 12 | Month 24 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold / aged | 70% | 30% | 10% |
| **Referral / database** | 20% | **50%** | **70%** |
| Inbound | 10% | 20% | 20% |

**This is the entire arc of the career.** Cold calling builds the database and the
relationships; those eventually replace the cold calling.

An LO in year five who still lives on purchased leads made a choice, three years earlier,
not to build this.

---

## What you must be able to do

- State the RESPA Section 8 test and apply it to an offer
- Explain what agents actually want, in priority order
- Deliver the buydown pitch
- Approach a CPA about bank statement programs
- Run the quarterly past-client call without pitching
- Ask for a referral at closing, and from someone who closed elsewhere

**Self-check:** A top agent offers you exclusive preferred-lender status on all her
listings if you cover half her monthly marketing spend. Yes or no?

> *No, as structured — that's paying for referrals, which is a Section 8 violation with
> criminal exposure. The compliant alternative is genuine co-marketing where you each pay
> your proportional share of a piece both of you appear on, at fair market value,
> documented, and reviewed by compliance. Offer that instead. And be aware the offer
> itself tells you something: an agent who structures it this way may be doing it with
> others, which is a risk worth noting.*

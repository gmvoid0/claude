# Conventional Loans

The default product. Everything else is measured against it.

---

## What "conventional" means

A conventional loan is **not government-insured**. It's underwritten to **Fannie Mae** or
**Freddie Mac** guidelines and sold to them.

- **Conforming** — meets GSE standards including loan limits
- **Non-conforming / jumbo** — exceeds limits, sold privately or held

---

## 2026 loan limits

| Units | Baseline | High-cost ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **$832,750** | **$1,249,125** |
| 2 | $1,066,250 | — |
| 3 | $1,288,800 | — |
| 4 | $1,601,750 | — |

Alaska, Hawaii, Guam, USVI: baseline **$1,249,125**, ceiling **$1,873,675**.

High-cost limits are county-specific. **Look up the actual county** — don't assume.

## PMI — and why it beats FHA's MIP

**Private Mortgage Insurance** protects the lender when the borrower puts down under 20%.

**The critical advantage over FHA: PMI can be removed.**

| Trigger | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **78% LTV** by amortization | Automatic termination, based on original value |
| **80% LTV** by amortization | Borrower may request cancellation |
| **80% LTV** by appreciation | Borrower requests, typically requires a new appraisal |

That last row is the one to know in a market where homes have appreciated. A borrower who
bought three years ago with 5% down may already be under 80% on current value and be
paying PMI unnecessarily.

**This is a free, no-loan phone call that generates enormous goodwill:**

> *"Quick thing — you put 5% down three years ago and prices in your area are up
> substantially. You may already be under 80% and paying mortgage insurance you don't owe
> anymore. Call your servicer and ask what it takes to remove PMI. Might save you $180 a
> month and it costs you nothing but an appraisal fee."*

You made no money. You made a client for life.

### PMI structures

| Type | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Borrower-paid monthly** | Standard. Cancellable. |
| **Single premium** | Paid upfront in a lump sum. Not cancellable, no monthly cost. |
| **Lender-paid (LPMI)** | Built into a higher rate. **Not cancellable** — the rate is permanent. |
| **Split premium** | Partial upfront, lower monthly |

**Be honest about LPMI.** It looks attractive because there's no MI line item, but the
higher rate never goes away — even after the borrower reaches 20% equity. For a long-term
holder it's usually the worst option.

## AUS — DU and LPA

Every conventional file runs through an **Automated Underwriting System**:

- **DU** (Desktop Underwriter) — Fannie Mae
- **LPA** (Loan Product Advisor) — Freddie Mac

### The findings

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Approve / Eligible** | Green light, subject to conditions |
| **Approve / Ineligible** | Credit risk acceptable, but something violates a rule (loan limit, occupancy) |
| **Refer / Eligible** | Needs manual underwriting |
| **Refer with Caution** | Serious risk concerns |

**The broker skill:** if DU refers it, run LPA. They weight risk differently and one will
frequently approve what the other won't. A retail LO usually has one. **You have both** —
and this is a genuinely material advantage you can explain to a borrower.

### What AUS gives you

An approval plus a **conditions list** — the documentation needed. Sometimes it grants
relief: reduced documentation, or an **appraisal waiver** (a "PIW" — property inspection
waiver), which saves the borrower several hundred dollars and a week of time.

## The 3% down programs

Fannie's **HomeReady** and Freddie's **Home Possible** target low-to-moderate income
borrowers.

| | |
|---|---|
| Down payment | **3%** |
| Income limits | Typically tied to area median income |
| PMI | Reduced coverage requirements, and cancellable |
| Homebuyer education | Usually required |
| Gift funds | Permitted |

**Why these beat FHA for many first-time buyers:** the PMI is cancellable and the total
cost over time is frequently lower — even though FHA's headline down payment is only
slightly higher. Run both.

## Cash-out refinance

| | Typical |
|---|---|
| Max LTV — primary, 1 unit | **80%** |
| Max LTV — second home / investment | Lower — verify |
| Seasoning | Generally 12 months of ownership |
| Pricing | **LLPA penalty** — cash-out prices above rate-and-term |

**The 80% cap is the number to memorize.** It's what determines how much cash a borrower
can actually access.

### The calculation

```
Home value                 $550,000
× 80%                      $440,000  ← maximum new loan
− existing balance         $108,000
− closing costs            ~$8,000
= available cash           ~$324,000
```

Michael in Call 1 wanted $120,000 against $442,000 of equity. **He had enormous room** —
which the originator noted correctly. The failure was in the product choice, not the
capacity.

## Rate-and-term refinance

No cash out (a small amount, typically the lesser of 2% or $2,000, is permitted as
incidental).

Higher LTV allowed and **better pricing** than cash-out.

**In August 2026 this product is nearly dead.** Anyone who transacted 2020–2022 holds a
rate far below market. File it away for when rates fall — and build your rate-drop trigger
list now (`../05-sales/31-followup-and-speed-to-lead.md`).

## Occupancy — and the fraud warning

| Occupancy | Down | Pricing |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary residence** | Lowest | Best |
| **Second home** | 10%+ | Moderate LLPA |
| **Investment** | 15–25% | Significant LLPA |

**Occupancy fraud is mortgage fraud — a federal crime.** Borrowers will sometimes ask you
to call an investment property a primary residence to get better pricing.

**The answer is always no**, and you should say why:

> *"I can't do that, and I want to be straight about why — that's occupancy fraud. It's a
> federal crime, it's on a document you sign, and it would cost me my license and
> potentially a lot worse. Let's do it correctly. The pricing difference is real but it's
> not worth what you'd be risking."*

## Positioning conventional

**Versus FHA:**
> *"Conventional's mortgage insurance comes off once you hit 20% equity. FHA's usually
> stays for the life of the loan. If your credit supports conventional, it's almost always
> cheaper over time — even if FHA looks better on day one."*

**Versus the borrower's bank:**
> *"Same Fannie Mae guidelines everywhere — the rulebook doesn't change. What changes is
> pricing and overlays. Your bank might add its own requirements on top; I've got fifty
> lenders and they don't all have the same overlays."*

## What you must be able to do

- State the 2026 conforming limits
- Explain PMI and all four cancellation triggers
- Explain why LPMI's permanence matters
- Explain DU vs. LPA and why having both is an advantage
- Calculate maximum cash-out at 80% LTV
- Refuse an occupancy misrepresentation and explain why

**Self-check:** Borrower bought 4 years ago, 5% down, $280k purchase, now worth $360k, has
a 3.4% rate, paying $190/month PMI, and wants $40,000 for a kitchen. What do you tell them?

> *Two separate things, and the first one makes you no money. Current LTV is roughly
> $265k ÷ $360k ≈ 74% — they're likely eligible to cancel PMI on appreciation. Tell them
> to call their servicer; that's $190/month back for the cost of an appraisal. Second, do
> not refinance the 3.4% for $40k — price a fixed second lien instead. CLTV would be about
> 85%, workable for strong credit. The PMI tip costs you nothing and earns you the second
> lien, plus every referral they've got.*

# Start Here

Read this before anything else. It takes ten minutes and it will save you months.

---

## 1. What this system assumes about you

It assumes you know **nothing**. Not "nothing about mortgages" — nothing. If a document
in here uses a word without defining it, that is a bug in the document, and you should
look it up in `10-reference/51-glossary.md` and then tell your coach so it gets fixed.

It does **not** assume you are stupid. There is a difference. You are going to be handed
real complexity — underwriting math, secondary-market mechanics, regulatory structure —
because a salesperson who doesn't understand the product is a salesperson who gets
caught out on call four and never recovers.

---

## 2. The one idea that matters most

You are not selling a mortgage. Mortgages are a commodity — the money is the same money,
and the lender who ends up funding your borrower's loan will probably also fund the loan
of the person who beat you last week.

**You are selling the certainty that this will get done, and that the person handling it
knows what they are doing.**

Amir Syed says it as *"I was the product."* Zig Ziglar says it as *"stop selling, start
helping."* Both are pointing at the same thing. The borrower cannot evaluate your rate —
they have nothing to compare it to and they don't understand what drives it. What they
*can* evaluate, instantly and unconsciously, is whether you sound like someone who has
done this a thousand times.

That is a skill. It is trainable. That's what this system trains.

---

## 3. Three ideas that will save you from the most common failure modes

**Objections are not rejections.** A person who objects is still on the phone. Someone
with zero interest hangs up — they don't argue with you about rates. An objection tells
you exactly what is in the way, which is a gift. The problem is never that you got an
objection; the problem is that you weren't ready for a completely predictable one.

**Sequence beats content.** *When* you say something matters more than *what* you say.
The classic beginner error is quoting a rate in the first ninety seconds. You have given
away the only thing they were calling to find out, before you established why your
number should be trusted or what problem you are actually solving. Rate comes near the
end. Always.

**You are a people officer before you are a loan officer.** More people knowing you,
liking you, and trusting you is the entire growth mechanism of this career. Everything
else is downstream.

## 4. The market you are actually walking into

You are not originating in 2021. This matters enormously and most training material has
not caught up.

| | |
|---|---|
| 30-year fixed | **~6.8–7.0%** (Aug 2026); projected 6.2–6.5% by year end |
| Everyone who bought or refinanced 2020–2022 | Holds a **2.5%–4%** note |
| Therefore | **Rate-and-term refinance is dead** for most of the market |

Read that last line again, because it kills the script most new LOs are handed. You
cannot call a homeowner with a 3.1% VA loan and offer to "lower their rate." There is no
lower rate. You will sound like a liar or a fool, and you will deserve to.

**What actually sells in 2026:**

- **Cash-out refinance** — they give up the low rate, but they get capital. Only makes
  sense when the use of funds is worth more than the rate they're surrendering.
- **Debt consolidation** — the strongest play in this market. A borrower paying 24% on
  $60,000 of credit cards can win big even moving from 3% to 7% on the mortgage,
  because you're solving *total monthly outflow*, not rate.
- **Second liens and HELOCs** — the defining product of this market. Keeps the 3% first
  mortgage completely intact and puts a smaller loan on top. Learn this cold.
- **Purchase** — always alive. Rate matters less than payment and down payment.

If you internalize one thing from this section: **stop selling rate, start selling
monthly cash flow and problem resolution.**

## 5. What changed on March 5, 2026

The **Homebuyers Privacy Protection Act** took effect. Credit bureaus can no longer sell
"trigger leads" — the alerts generated the moment someone's credit is pulled for a
mortgage — to third parties with no existing relationship to that consumer.

This is the biggest structural change to mortgage lead generation in twenty years. It
killed 10–30% of the pipeline at many call centers overnight, and internet lead costs
jumped roughly 45% year over year.

**Why you care:** the four transcripts in `07-teardowns/` are trigger-lead calls from
before the ban. They open with "I'm following up on an inquiry you submitted." Nobody
submitted anything. That opening is now both unusable and — as it was already —
dishonest. You will learn what to say instead in
`06-cold-calling/34-the-opening-script-book.md`.

## 7. How to actually practice

Reading this repo will not make you good. Reps will.

The single highest-leverage habit available to you: **record your calls** (where legally
permitted — see `04-compliance/19-cold-calling-law.md` on two-party consent states),
then run `call-score` on the transcript. You will hate this for about two weeks. Then it
will become the thing that makes you money.

Second-highest: run `/cold-call-drill` before your first dial of the day, every day. Ten
minutes. You do not want your first call of the morning to be the one where you find
your voice.

## 9. The honest part

The first ninety days are brutal. You will make a lot of calls and close very little.
This is not a sign that you are bad at this — it is arithmetic. Your pipeline is empty,
your database is empty, and mortgages take 30–45 days to close, so even the deals you
win today pay you next month.

The LOs who make it are not the talented ones. They are the ones who kept dialing
through the part where nothing was working.

Go to `04-compliance/19-cold-calling-law.md`.

# The Aged Lead Playbook

Your actual lead source. This document is about extracting maximum value from it.

---

## What an aged lead is

A consumer filled out a mortgage inquiry form on a website — a rate-comparison site, a
lender's landing page, a lead aggregator. That form was sold as a **fresh lead** at a
premium, worked (or not) by whoever bought it, and then resold later at a much lower
price.

"Aged" typically means anything from **30 days to 12+ months** old.

| | Fresh lead | Aged lead |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | High | Low — often 90%+ cheaper |
| Contact rate | Higher | Lower |
| Competition | Brutal — 10+ callers in an hour | Much lower — most have given up |
| Consumer state | Actively shopping | Unknown; often unresolved |
| **Compliance risk** | Lower | **Higher — see below** |

---

## Why aged leads got more attractive in 2026

The trigger-lead ban (`../04-compliance/20-the-trigger-lead-ban.md`) removed a major
source of outbound volume. Displaced buyers moved into opt-in supply, and internet lead
costs rose **~45% year over year**.

Aged leads are now one of the few places where the economics still work for a new LO
without a marketing budget. They're also where the most careless calling happens, which
is your opportunity — most people work them badly.

## The compliance layer — read this before you dial

**Aged leads carry more legal risk than fresh leads**, for one specific reason.

### The three-month EBR cliff

An internet form is an **inquiry**. Under the Telemarketing Sales Rule, an inquiry-based
Established Business Relationship permits you to call a number on the **National DNC
Registry** for **three months**. Not twelve. Three.

| Lead age | On National DNC? | Callable? |
|---|---|---|
| 6 weeks | Yes | ✅ Inquiry EBR is live |
| 6 weeks | No | ✅ Yes |
| **9 months** | **Yes** | ❌ **No** — EBR expired. Requires valid express written consent. |
| 9 months | No | ✅ Yes, subject to state rules |

**Most aged leads sold are older than three months.** That means for any aged lead on the
DNC Registry, your right to call rests entirely on **express written consent** — the
opt-in language on the original form.

### Three things to get in writing from your lead vendor

Ask for these before you dial a single record. If your shop buys the leads, ask your
manager and get the answer in writing.

1. **The exact opt-in language** the consumer agreed to
2. **Proof of consent per record** — timestamp, IP address, the URL of the form
3. **Whether the leads are DNC-scrubbed**, and how recently

If a vendor can't produce all three, that's not a paperwork gap — that's your name on a
lawsuit. Most LOs have never read the consent language behind the leads they dial every
day, which is exactly why they end up as named defendants.

### The state layer

You're multi-state licensed. Several states (FL, OK, MD, WA, NJ) have mini-TCPA statutes
with their own consent standards, tighter hours (8 a.m.–8 p.m.), and daily contact caps
(3 attempts). Damages run **$500–$20,000 per violation**.

**Build to the strictest rule across all your states.** One standard is easier to follow
than five, and the cost is a handful of dials.

See `../04-compliance/19-cold-calling-law.md`.

## The mental reframe

Most LOs treat aged leads as garbage. That attitude is audible on the call and it is
self-fulfilling.

**The truth about a 6-month-old mortgage lead:**

- They had a real financial need six months ago
- Financial needs rarely resolve themselves quietly
- Roughly half never completed a transaction at all
- Of those who did, some were dissatisfied
- **Almost nobody has called them in months**

You are not calling a dead lead. You are calling someone with an **unresolved problem**
and no one competing for their attention. That's a better situation than a fresh lead
with ten callers on it — it's just slower.

## The opening

Full script in `34-the-opening-script-book.md`. The core:

> *"Hello, Michael?* **[pause]** *Hi Michael, my name is [Name], I'm with [Company],
> NMLS [ID], on a recorded line. You filled out a request on [site] back on [date] about
> [purpose]. I know that was a while ago — **did you ever get that sorted out?**"*

### Why that question is the whole playbook

**"Did you ever get that sorted out?"** does more work than any other opening question in
aged-lead calling:

- It's **warm** — it implies you're following up, not prospecting
- It's **true** — they did fill out a form, and you're asking about it
- It **assumes a shared history** that genuinely exists
- **Both answers are useful:**
  - *"Yes"* → who with, how'd it go, put my number in your phone for the refi
  - *"No"* → you've just surfaced an unresolved problem and you're the one calling about it

Compare it to *"are you still interested in refinancing?"* — which invites a reflexive
"no" and teaches you nothing.

## The five outcomes and what to do with each

### 1. "Yes, I closed it" (~25%)

Not a dead end. Two plays:

> *"Congratulations. Who'd you go with?"* **[listen]** *"And how'd that whole process go?"*
> **[listen — this is where you learn if there's an opening]**
>
> *"Glad it worked out. Do me a favor and put my number in your phone. Rates are
> projected to come toward the low sixes by year end — whoever did your loan probably
> won't call you when that happens. I will."*

**Then ask for the referral:**
> *"One other thing — anybody else in your world looking at buying or refinancing right
> now?"*

Someone who just closed a mortgage has been talking about mortgages with everyone they
know for two months. **This is the highest-yield referral moment in the business** and
almost nobody asks.

### 2. "No, it never happened" (~35%) — your best outcome

> *"What stopped it?"*

Then listen carefully, because the answer routes the whole call:

| What they say | What it means | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| "Rates went up" | Timing | Reframe to cash flow / cash-out / second |
| "I got denied" | **Fixable** | "What was the reason? I've got fifty lenders and they don't share overlays." |
| "It got too complicated" | Bad LO | "What made it complicated?" — this is a service sale, easy win |
| "I changed my mind" | Soft | "Fair enough — has anything changed since?" |
| "Couldn't get the appraisal to work" | Fixable | Different appraiser, different lender, different product |

**"I got denied" is the single best answer you can hear on an aged lead.** One lender's
overlay is not the market. As a broker with fifty lenders you are structurally positioned
to solve exactly this, and the borrower has already been told no once, so their
expectations are low and their gratitude is high.

### 3. "I don't remember filling that out" (~20%)

**Believe them immediately.** Never argue (Call 2's fatal error).

> *"You may well be right, and I apologize — these come through aggregators and the data
> isn't always clean. Let me just be straight about why I'm calling and if it's not
> useful I'll let you go. Fair?"*

Then either pivot to a genuine value reason or exit gracefully. **If they express any
removal language, stop and log it.**

### 4. "Not interested" (~15%)

One reframe. Then stop. `../04-compliance/19-cold-calling-law.md`.

### 5. Removal request (~5%)

> *"Absolutely — done. Sorry to have bothered you."*

Log immediately. Internal DNC, permanently.

## Working the list efficiently

### Sort before you dial

Not all aged leads are equal. Prioritize:

| Priority | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **1** | Newest (30–90 days) — EBR may still be live |
| **2** | Purchase leads — timelines create urgency |
| **3** | Cash-out / debt consolidation — works at today's rates |
| **4** | Higher loan amounts — same effort, more comp |
| **5** | Your licensed states with the friendliest calling rules |
| **Last** | Rate-and-term refi requests from 2021 — mostly unworkable now |

That last row matters. Someone who asked about a rate-and-term refi in 2021 has a 3%
note. There is nothing to sell them except a **second lien** — which is a real
conversation, just not the one on the lead card.

### The attempt cadence

Aged leads need more attempts than fresh ones, and **the same rules apply**: max 3
attempts per day, varied times of day.

| Attempt | Timing | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 1, afternoon | Call + voicemail |
| 2 | Day 2, morning | Call |
| 3 | Day 3, evening (before 8 p.m.) | Call + text |
| 4 | Day 6 | Call |
| 5 | Day 10 | Call + the file-closing text |
| — | Then quarterly value touches | |

**Most LOs stop at attempt 2.** The gap between 2 and 5 is where the leads everyone else
paid for and abandoned are sitting.

### The list is an asset, not a consumable

Every record gets a disposition in the CRM. A worked list is worth more than an unworked
one *even if nobody closed*, because now you know:

- Who's reachable and when
- Who has a live need with bad timing → the follow-up list
- Who closed elsewhere → the refi and referral list
- Who's a permanent no → suppression

Post-ban, with lead costs up ~45%, **throwing away a worked list is throwing away money
your broker already spent.**

## The economics

Rough illustrative model — **replace with your real numbers**:

| | |
|---|---|
| 1,000 aged leads at ~$0.50–$2.00 each | ~$500–$2,000 |
| Contact rate ~10% | 100 contacts |
| Conversation rate ~40% | 40 conversations |
| Application rate ~10% | 4 applications |
| Funding rate ~55% | ~2 fundings |

At a $250k average loan and ~1.5% comp, two fundings is roughly **$7,500** against
$500–$2,000 in lead cost.

> `[Illustrative planning model only. Your contact rate, comp plan, and lead cost will
> differ substantially — see ../10-reference/53-gaps.md.]`

**The model works.** But notice where it's fragile: contact rate and conversation rate.
A 10%→14% contact rate improvement is worth more than doubling the list size, and it
costs nothing but better dialing hours and a better opening.

## The ten rules

1. **Verify consent and DNC status before you dial.** Non-negotiable.
2. **Never claim they called you.** They filled out a form. Say that.
3. **Lead with "did you ever get that sorted out?"**
4. **Believe them when they don't remember.** Never argue provenance.
5. **"I got denied" is your best lead.** Chase it hard.
6. **Always ask what stopped it.**
7. **Ask closed borrowers for referrals.** Highest-yield moment available.
8. **Five attempts, varied times, never more than 3/day.**
9. **Disposition every record.** The worked list is the asset.
10. **Expect a 3% note.** Have the second-lien reframe loaded before you dial.

## What you must be able to do

- State the three-month EBR rule and apply it to a 9-month-old lead
- Name the three things to demand from a lead vendor
- Open an aged lead call truthfully
- Route all five outcomes
- Ask for a referral from someone who closed elsewhere
- Explain why "I got denied" is the best answer you can hear

**Self-check:** An 11-month-old lead, on the National DNC Registry, wanted a rate-and-term
refi. Do you call?

> *Only if your vendor can produce valid, current express written consent — the inquiry
> EBR expired eight months ago. If they can, then yes, but change your expectation before
> you dial: they've got a 2021 rate, so rate-and-term is off the table entirely. You're
> calling to find out whether they have a cash need you can solve with a second lien.*

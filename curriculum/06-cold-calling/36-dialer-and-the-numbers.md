# The Dialer and the Numbers

Know your numbers or you're guessing. This is the shortest path from "I'm working hard"
to "I know exactly what to fix."

---

## Dialer types

| Type | How it works | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Manual** | You dial each number | Small warm lists, high-value leads |
| **Click-to-dial** | One click per record from the CRM | **Best default for a new LO** |
| **Power dialer** | Dials one number per agent sequentially | High-volume lists, aged leads |
| **Predictive** | Dials many numbers, connects answered calls to a free agent | Large floors only |

**Predictive dialers carry real regulatory risk.** They cause **abandoned calls** — the
consumer answers, nobody's there. The TSR caps abandonment (generally 3% of answered
calls) and requires a prerecorded identification message within two seconds when
abandonment occurs. Violations are per-call.

> **For a new LO: use click-to-dial or a power dialer.** Predictive dialing is a
> compliance program, not a tool, and it should be your company's decision with
> compliance oversight — not yours.

## Caller ID and answer rates

Your contact rate is partly a technical problem, not a skill problem.

### Spam labeling

Carriers analytically flag numbers as "Spam Likely" based on call volume, call duration
patterns, and complaint rates. A flagged number gets answered dramatically less. New LOs
often conclude their list is bad when their **number** is the problem.

**What to do:**

- **Check your number monthly.** Free lookup tools exist for the major analytics
  providers (they're what carriers use).
- **Register your numbers.** The industry branded-caller / number-registration programs
  let legitimate businesses attest to their numbers.
- **Use local presence carefully.** Matching the area code lifts answer rates, but
  rotating through many numbers to evade filtering can itself violate caller-ID rules.
  **Never** display a number you don't control or can't be reached at — that's spoofing
  and it's illegal under the Truth in Caller ID Act.
- **Longer calls help.** Carrier algorithms weight short-duration calls as spam signal.
  Good calling improves your deliverability, which improves your calling. Compounds.

### Practical caller ID rules

- Display a number that **rings back to you**
- Same number consistently — it builds recognition, especially with a text bridge
- If flagged, request remediation rather than burning the number and starting over

## The text bridge

The single best technique in the transcripts. Steal it.

> *"I'm sure you're getting quite a few calls. So you can tell it's me — I'll text you
> from my cell, it'll come from a 248 area code, and that's the number I'll call you back
> from."*

**Why it works:**

- Acknowledges their real experience (they *are* getting spammed)
- Gives a **recognition token** so your callback isn't an unknown number
- Converts an unknown-number call into an expected call

**Make it better than the transcripts did:** attach a **specific time**.

> *"I'll text you the numbers by 2:00 from a 248 number, then call you at 4:15."*

**Compliance:** texts are calls under the TCPA. Consent required, revocation honored
immediately, 8 a.m.–8 p.m. their time.

## The metrics that matter

Track these daily. On paper, on a whiteboard, in a spreadsheet — the medium doesn't
matter, the daily part does.

### Daily

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Dials** | Attempts made |
| **Contacts** | A live human said something back |
| **Conversations** | Got past the opening into discovery |
| **Appointments** | Specific time booked |
| **Applications** | Six TRID elements collected |
| **Talk time** | Total minutes on the phone |

### Derived — the ones that diagnose

| Ratio | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Contact rate** = contacts ÷ dials | List quality, timing, caller ID health |
| **Conversation rate** = conversations ÷ contacts | **Your opening** |
| **App rate** = apps ÷ conversations | Discovery and positioning |
| **Talk time per dial** | Are you having real conversations or bouncing? |

**Conversation rate is your opening's report card.** It's the fastest metric to move and
the one most worth obsessing over early.

### Weekly

- Applications taken
- Locks
- Fundings
- **Pull-through** = fundings ÷ applications
- Average loan amount
- Pipeline value

## Diagnosing from the numbers

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Contact rate under 5% | Bad data, wrong hours, spam-flagged number | Check the number first, then the list, then the hours |
| Contact rate fine, conversation rate under 20% | **Opening is failing** | `33-the-first-15-seconds.md` |

## Improving contact rate

The cheapest lever available to you.

1. **Call in the right windows.** 4–8 p.m. beats 11 a.m.–1 p.m. substantially. Saturday
   morning is underrated.
2. **Vary the time.** Someone unreachable at 10 a.m. daily may be entirely reachable at 6
   p.m. Same slot repeatedly is how you wrongly conclude a person is unreachable.
3. **Fix your caller ID.** Check spam status monthly.
4. **Dial mobile before landline** where you have both.
5. **Text after two failed calls.** Higher open rate than any call.
6. **Sort by recency.** Newer aged leads answer more.

---

## Improving conversation rate

The highest-value lever, because it's pure skill.

1. **Rewrite your opening.** `33-the-first-15-seconds.md`.
2. **Slow down 20%.** The most common single defect.
3. **Lead with a specific true reason**, never a category pitch.
4. **End with an easy question**, never a decision.
5. **Record and listen.** You will hear the problem in one call.
6. **Change one variable at a time**, and give it 100 calls.

---

## The 100-call rule

**Never judge a script change on fewer than 100 calls.**

Call outcomes are high-variance. Ten calls tells you nothing — you can have three great
conversations in a row on a bad script and five hang-ups in a row on a good one.

New LOs change their opening every twenty calls, learn nothing, and conclude nothing
works. Pick a version, run 100, record the numbers, then change one thing.

## Sustainable volume

| Level | Dials/day | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Minimum** | 50 | Below this you aren't in the game |
| **Solid** | 80–120 | Sustainable long-term with good blocks |
| **High** | 150–200 | Requires a power dialer and real discipline |
| **Amir's era** | 300+ | Six days a week. Genuinely brutal. |

More is not automatically better. **A 60-dial day with 25 minutes of talk time beats a
150-dial day with 8 minutes.** Talk time is where the learning and the money are.

Track dials for accountability. Track **talk time** for actual progress.

## What you must be able to do

- Name the four dialer types and why predictive isn't yours to choose
- Explain how caller ID reputation affects contact rate
- Track six daily metrics and compute four ratios
- Diagnose a weak funnel stage from the numbers
- Hold a script change for 100 calls before judging it

**Self-check:** 120 dials, 6 contacts, 4 conversations, 1 application. What's wrong?

> *Contact rate is 5% — that's the problem, and it's not a skill problem. Once you reach
> people you're converting well (67% conversation rate, 25% app rate — both strong).
> Check, in order: is your number spam-flagged, are you calling at the wrong hours, is
> the list stale? Adding dials would waste effort; fix the top of the funnel.*

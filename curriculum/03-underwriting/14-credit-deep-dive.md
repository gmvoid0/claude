# Credit Deep Dive

Credit is where most deals live or die, and it's the subject borrowers are most anxious
and least informed about. Handling it well is a major trust differentiator.

---

## How the score works

**FICO**, 300–850. Weighted roughly:

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---|
| Payment history | ~35% |
| Amounts owed (utilization) | ~30% |
| Length of credit history | ~15% |
| New credit / inquiries | ~10% |
| Credit mix | ~10% |

Mortgage lending typically uses older FICO scoring models than the ones consumers see on
free apps — which is why **the score you pull is often lower than the score they quote
you.**

**Say this before you pull:**

> *"One thing so you're not surprised — the score I pull will probably come back lower
> than what you see on Credit Karma or your banking app. Those use different models.
> Mortgage lending uses its own, and it usually runs 20 to 40 points lower. It's not a
> mistake and it's not you, it's just a different scale."*

That thirty seconds prevents a genuinely damaging conversation.

## The tiers, and why 20 points matters

| FICO | Position |
|---|---|
| 760+ | Best pricing |
| 740–759 | Excellent |
| 720–739 | Very good |
| 700–719 | Good |
| 680–699 | Pricing starts to bite |
| 660–679 | Acceptable |
| 640–659 | Limited |
| 620–639 | Conventional floor |
| 580–619 | FHA |
| 500–579 | FHA 10% down, very few lenders |

**LLPAs price in 20-point bands.** A borrower at 698 and one at 702 are in different
pricing buckets, and on a $300,000 loan the difference can be thousands of dollars.

### The rapid rescore play

If a borrower is a few points below a tier boundary, paying down a credit card can move
them across it. A **rapid rescore** (through your lender, typically a few days) updates the
bureaus faster than the normal monthly cycle.

> *"You're at 698. If we can get you to 700, your pricing improves — the tiers break every
> 20 points. What are your card balances? If you can pay $1,200 off that one, we can do a
> rapid rescore and probably get you across the line in about a week. That's worth
> thousands over the loan."*

**This is one of the highest-value things you can do for a borrower**, and almost no LO
checks for it.

### Utilization

Utilization is ~30% of the score and it updates monthly. Key facts:

- **Below 30% utilization** on each card is the general target; below 10% is better
- **Paying a card to zero is not always optimal** — a small reported balance can score
  better than zero on all cards
- **Don't close old cards.** It shortens average age and reduces available credit, both of
  which lower the score. Borrowers do this thinking it helps.

## Derogatories, by severity

| Item | Impact | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Mortgage late** | **Most severe** | Even one 30-day is material |
| Foreclosure / short sale | Severe | Long waiting periods |
| Bankruptcy | Severe | Waiting periods by chapter |
| Judgment / tax lien | Severe | Usually must be paid or on a documented plan |
| Collection | Moderate | May need to be paid depending on size and program |
| Charge-off | Moderate | Similar treatment |
| Installment late | Moderate | Car, student loan |
| Revolving late | Lower | Credit card |

### The waiting periods

| Event | Conventional | FHA | VA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chapter 7 BK | 4 years | 2 years | 2 years |
| Chapter 13 | 2 years from discharge | 1 year of on-time payments w/ court approval | 1 year |
| Foreclosure | 7 years | 3 years | 2 years |
| Short sale | 4 years | 3 years | 2 years |
| Deed in lieu | 4 years | 3 years | 2 years |

> `[Waiting periods vary by program version and lender overlay, and extenuating
> circumstances can shorten them. Verify per file.]`

**Extenuating circumstances** — documented, genuinely non-recurring events outside the
borrower's control — can shorten these. A documented medical catastrophe or death of a
wage earner sometimes qualifies. Job loss usually doesn't, because underwriting treats it
as a foreseeable risk.

**The prospecting angle:** someone foreclosed on in 2022 is FHA-eligible now and almost
certainly doesn't know it. Nobody calls those people.

## The 30-day mortgage late

**This appears in two of the four transcripts and was mishandled both times.** It deserves
its own section.

### Why it's so serious

A 30-day mortgage late is the most predictive negative item in mortgage underwriting —
someone who missed a mortgage payment is the exact risk the lender is pricing.

**Many cash-out programs require 0x30 in the last 12 months.** A recent mortgage late can
disqualify the loan outright, not just worsen pricing.

### But the cause matters enormously

There is a real difference between:

| Type | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Borrower couldn't afford the payment | Serious. Little to work with. |
| **Servicer error** | Often disputable and removable |
| **Payment method failure** — expired card, closed account, fraud freeze | **Potentially documentable** |
| Escrow shortage the borrower didn't know about | Often explainable |

### Both transcript borrowers had documentable causes

**Michael, Call 1:**
> *"My debit card had expired and they do an auto draft on there and I didn't realize it,
> and I was like 32 or 35 days late... it wasn't really my fault. It was my fault, but it
> wasn't my fault."*

**Fasani, Call 4:**
> *"Somebody used my Cash App — it's like I got hacked and they pull a lot of money from
> my account, and I called the bank and they stop it... they rejected my payment and I
> didn't know."*

**Both are potentially documentable.** A fraud report, a bank letter, a card expiration
record, plus a letter of explanation, can sometimes be worked with underwriting or through
a lender's exception process. In some cases the late can be disputed with the servicer and
removed.

**In both calls, the story got cut off and the response was a version of "it shouldn't be
a problem."**

### What to actually do

1. **Let them tell the whole story.** Do not interrupt. This is the single most important
   thing.
2. **Ask when, exactly**, and whether it was the mortgage or something else
3. **Ask what caused it** — and listen for a documentable cause
4. **Ask for the paperwork:** *"Do you have anything in writing from the bank? A letter, a
   fraud case number, anything showing the card expired?"*
5. **Be honest about the obstacle:** *"I want to be straight — a 30-day on the mortgage in
   the last year is the biggest thing standing between us and this loan. If we can
   document the cause, we've got options. If we can't, we may be waiting. I'd rather tell
   you that now."*
6. **Check whether it can be disputed** with the servicer

**The script:**

> *"Okay, that's important and it might be fixable. Walk me through exactly what happened
> and when."*
> **[silence — let them finish completely]**
> *"Do you have anything in writing from the bank about that? Because a documented bank
> error is a completely different conversation with underwriting than 'I forgot to pay.'"*

## The letter of explanation

Your primary tool for anything unusual. A good LOE:

- Is **short** — a few sentences
- States **what happened**, factually
- States **when** it happened and that it's resolved
- Explains **why it won't recur**
- Is **signed and dated**
- **Attaches documentation**

**Example:**

> *"In March 2026, my auto-payment for my mortgage failed because the debit card on file
> had expired. I was not aware the payment had not processed until I received notice from
> the servicer, at which point I paid immediately. I have since set up payment from my
> checking account directly rather than a debit card. Attached is the notice from the card
> issuer showing the expiration date and my bank statement showing the payment made on
> [date]. I have had no other late payments in the past two years."*

Factual, brief, documented, and it explains the fix. Write these for your borrowers —
most can't, and a bad LOE makes things worse.

## Non-traditional credit

Borrowers with thin or no credit files can sometimes qualify using **alternative
tradelines**:

- Rent payment history (12 months, verifiable)
- Utility payments
- Insurance payments
- Cell phone
- Childcare

Usually requires **manual underwriting** and typically three alternative tradelines.
More common with FHA. It's slow and paperwork-heavy but it's real, and it serves borrowers
nobody else will touch.

## Discussing credit without discouraging

**Critical compliance point.** Under ECOA, **discouraging** an applicant is a violation
even without a formal denial (`../04-compliance/22-respa-ecoa-udaap-fair-lending.md`).

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say |
|---|---|
| "Your credit's too bad, don't bother" | "Here's what your score opens up, and here's what would change it" |
| "You'll never qualify" | "Not today with these programs — let me tell you what would get you there" |
| "Fix your credit and call me back" | "Let's build a plan. Here are three specific things, and I'll check back in 90 days." |

**Take the application. Let underwriting decide.** Informal pre-judgment is exactly what
fair lending enforcement is designed to catch, and you'll never know if you're doing it
unevenly.

### The credit-repair conversation

When someone genuinely can't qualify today, this is one of the most valuable calls you'll
ever make:

> *"Here's where you are and here's what would change it. Three things, specifically. One,
> that collection for $340 — pay it and get a paid-in-full letter. Two, your card is at 90%
> utilization; get it under 30% and you'll likely gain 20 to 40 points. Three, don't open
> anything new for six months. Do those and call me in 90 days and I think we're in
> business. Want me to write that down and email it to you?"*

**That borrower becomes a client and a referral source for a decade**, because you're the
only person who told them the truth and gave them a path.

## What you must be able to do

- Explain why the mortgage score differs from consumer apps — before you pull
- Identify a rapid rescore opportunity near a tier boundary
- Recall the waiting periods for major credit events
- Handle a 30-day mortgage late properly, including asking for documentation
- Write a letter of explanation
- Discuss credit without discouraging

**Self-check:** Borrower says *"I had a bankruptcy, I think about three years ago."* What
do you ask?

> *"Chapter 7 or 13? And what was the discharge date — not the filing date, the discharge?
> That's the date the clock runs from and people mix them up." Then: Chapter 7 discharged
> three years ago means FHA is available now (2-year seasoning) and VA if he's eligible;
> conventional needs 4 years. Then: "What caused it?" — because documented extenuating
> circumstances can shorten conventional seasoning. Then: "How's your credit been since?"
> Re-established credit post-discharge matters a great deal. Do not tell him he can't
> qualify — he probably can.*

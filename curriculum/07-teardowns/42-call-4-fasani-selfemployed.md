# Teardown 4 — Fasani (self-employed trucking, unfiled returns, 30-day late)

**The call:** ~7 minutes. The most suspicious borrower and the most gettable deal.

**Surface verdict:** decent discovery, borrower cooperative, callback agreed.

**Actual verdict:** the originator ran a conventional-loan discovery on a borrower who
cannot qualify conventionally, never named the product that would actually work, and
ended the call promising numbers that don't exist.

---

## The borrower

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Current balance | ~$188,000 |
| Estimated value | $330,000+ |
| **Equity** | ~$142,000 |
| Current rate | **4.5%, 30-year FHA** |
| Payment | $1,660 including taxes and insurance |
| Cash wanted | $100,000 |
| **Purpose** | **Expand the business** |
| Employment | **Self-employed, business owner, 22 years** |
| Industry | Trucking / transportation |
| **Tax returns** | **Not filed — "I'm doing them right now"** |
| Income | "Used to make really good money," business slowed, recovering |
| Credit event | **30-day mortgage late last month** — account frozen after fraud/hack |

---

## What went right

### The suspicion was handled well

> *"Who are you and how do you know this?"*

A hostile, fair question. The response:

> *"We're a wholesale broker. My name is [Name], my company's name is Mortgage One, I
> work with banks like Rocket Mortgage, PennyMac, Freedom, Wells Fargo — we work with
> over 50 different banks, so we have access to all these pricing sheets."*

Naming recognizable institutions is smart. It borrows credibility fast and it's concrete
rather than vague. The borrower relaxes noticeably afterward.

### "No credit pull, part ways as friends"

> *"It's no credit pull, nothing like that... If you like it, by all means, we'll get the
> ball moving. If not, we'll just part way as friends."*

Good low-pressure framing. Reduces the stakes of continuing the conversation.

### Asking about the purpose — the only call that did

> *"So are you looking to pay off any debt, or what is the main goals with the cash?"*
> *"I'm looking to expand my business."*

**Credit where it's due: this is the only one of the four calls where the purpose of the
money was actually asked for.**

It was then dropped immediately, which is the failure. But the question was asked.

### The self-employment qualification explanation was accurate

> *"The tax returns will have to be complete... they're just going to calculate your
> qualifying income going based off of, you know, after taxes, after write-offs, how much
> you made."*

Correct, clearly explained, and useful. This is real teaching and the borrower needed it.

## What went wrong

### ❌ 1. The right product was never named

This is the central failure of the call.

**The borrower:**
- Self-employed, 22 years in business
- Tax returns **not filed**
- Income was strong, dipped, recovering
- Writes off aggressively (implied — he's a 22-year trucking owner)
- Has ~$142,000 in equity
- Wants $100,000 to expand

**This is a textbook bank-statement loan borrower.** A non-QM bank-statement program
qualifies off 12 or 24 months of business bank deposits and **ignores tax returns
entirely**. There are also P&L programs and, for a trucking business with revenue-producing
assets, potentially other structures.

**None of it was mentioned.** Not once, in a seven-minute call with a self-employed
borrower whose returns don't exist.

Instead the entire call is structured around conventional qualification — which cannot
happen until March, if then, and which will likely fail anyway because his write-offs will
crush his qualifying income.

**What should have been said, the moment he said "I'm doing them right now":**

> *"Okay, that's actually the most important thing you've told me, so let's deal with it
> head on. Conventional financing needs those returns — there's no way around it, and even
> when they're filed, if you write off aggressively like most owners do, the income that
> shows up may not support a $288,000 loan."*
>
> *"So there's a second path and it's built for exactly your situation. It's called a
> bank-statement program. It ignores tax returns completely and qualifies you off deposits
> into your business account — usually 12 or 24 months. Rate's higher than conventional,
> but it closes now instead of in March, and for a lot of business owners it's the only
> thing that actually works."*
>
> *"Can you pull your last 12 months of business bank statements? Because that'll tell me
> in about ten minutes whether this is a real deal."*

That is a completely different call. See `../02-products/10-non-qm.md`.

### ❌ 2. The 30-day mortgage late was minimized

> *"I have a problem last month. I'm late in my payment."*
> *"Late in the mortgage payment?"* — *"Yeah. I paid it."*
> *"Okay, but it was showed 30 days late on the mortgage?"* — *"Yes."*

Then:

> *"I mean, I'll definitely look into it to see if it's gonna be an issue for us. And then,
> you know, if not, perfect, we'll get the ball moving."*

**A 30-day mortgage late *last month* is close to disqualifying** for most cash-out
programs. Many lenders require 0x30 in the last 12 months on a cash-out refinance. Combined
with unfiled returns, this deal is very likely dead in the near term — and definitely dead
conventionally.

**But the cause matters enormously:**

> *"Somebody used my Cash App — it's like I got hacked and they pull a lot of money from
> my account, and I called the bank and they stop it... they rejected my payment and I
> didn't know."*

**A payment failure caused by a fraud-related account freeze is potentially documentable.**
Bank letters, fraud reports, and a letter of explanation can sometimes be worked with
underwriting, or with certain lenders' exception processes.

That is a *specific, actionable* line of inquiry and it got one sentence.

**What should have been asked:**

> *"Okay, that's important, and it might be fixable. Do you have anything in writing from
> the bank about the fraud? A letter, a case number, a fraud report? Because a documented
> bank error is a different conversation with underwriting than 'I forgot to pay.' Get me
> whatever the bank gave you."*

### ❌ 3. The call ends promising numbers that cannot exist

> *"All right then, let me go ahead and run these numbers for you. I'm going to see which
> lender has the best deal for you, and then I can give you a call back just to run
> through the option."*

**There are no numbers to run.** Without filed tax returns there is no qualifying income
to price against. Without addressing the 30-day late there's no lender list.

The borrower even sensed it:

> *"Probably you got to see this first before I waste your time or money. You know what I
> mean?"*

**The borrower was more realistic about his own file than the loan officer was.** That's
the tell that the call went wrong.

### ❌ 4. The income question was asked badly and abandoned

> *"So how much would you say you're making on a yearly basis after taxes and write-offs?"*
> *"I used to make really good money right now. Trucking businesses slowed down and it's
> coming back right now."*
> *"Like what's a rough number for like the last two years, like after taxes after
> write-offs, what do you think you're showing on paper?"*
> *"Yeah really is us to you that didn't do text I'm doing them right now."*
> *"Gotcha. Okay, not a problem."*

The borrower gave a non-answer twice and the thread was dropped.

**The right approach was never taken:** stop asking about tax returns and ask about
**deposits**.

> *"Forget the tax returns for a second. In a normal month, how much money comes into your
> business bank account? Just deposits — not profit, not after write-offs. Gross deposits."*

That single question routes the entire deal, and it's the qualifying question for the
product he actually needs.

### ❌ 5. "We take scores as low as a 500"

> *"We take scores as low as a 500. So as long as the credit score comes above 500..."*

Technically FHA's floor — with **10% down**, and effectively unavailable in wholesale
without heavy overlays. Materially misleading, especially to a borrower who has just
disclosed a 30-day mortgage late.

**His credit score was never actually asked for.**

### ❌ 6. The business questions were never asked

Twenty-two years in trucking, wants $100,000 to expand. Never asked:

- **What's the entity structure?** Sole prop, LLC, S-corp? Completely changes income
  calculation.
- **What's the expansion?** Trucks? Drivers? A contract he's won? This determines urgency
  and timeline.
- **How many trucks does he run now?**
- **Is there existing business debt?** Equipment loans affect DTI.
- **Would an equipment or business loan be better than a mortgage?** Sometimes it is, and
  saying so builds enormous trust.

"Transportation? Trucking. Oh, trucking. Okay, I got you" was the entire exploration of a
22-year business.

### ❌ 7. The FHA angle was missed entirely

He has a **30-year FHA loan at 4.5%**. That means he's paying **mortgage insurance
premium** every month, likely for the life of the loan.

Depending on when it was originated, an **FHA Streamline Refinance** could be relevant —
though notably **not** for cash-out, and the 30-day late is a problem for streamline
credit requirements too.

More usefully: the MIP is real money in his payment and nobody discussed it. Understanding
what he's actually paying for is part of the diagnosis.

## The call that should have happened

> *"You said you're doing your returns right now — when will your CPA actually have them
> filed?"*
>
> *[listen]*
>
> *"Okay. Then let me be straight with you, because I'd rather do that than waste three
> weeks of your time. Conventional financing needs those returns and there's no way around
> it. And honestly — after 22 years you probably write off aggressively, which is smart
> business and terrible for mortgage qualifying."*
>
> *"So here's the path that actually fits. It's called a bank-statement program. It ignores
> tax returns completely and qualifies you off deposits into your business account. Rate's
> higher than conventional. It closes now instead of March."*
>
> *"Two questions. One — in a normal month, how much comes into the business account? Just
> gross deposits. Two — that late payment last month. You said your account got frozen
> after the fraud. Do you have anything in writing from the bank? A letter, a case number?
> Because a documented bank error is a completely different conversation with underwriting
> than a missed payment."*
>
> *"I want to be honest about that late payment though. A 30-day on the mortgage last
> month is the biggest obstacle here — a lot of cash-out programs want a clean 12 months.
> If we can document the fraud, we've got options. If we can't, we may be waiting some
> months, and I'd rather tell you that now."*
>
> *"Last thing — tell me about the expansion. What's the $100,000 actually buying?"*

**That call ends with a real path, real homework, and a borrower who trusts you** — even
if the answer turns out to be "not yet."

## The four lessons

1. **When a borrower says their returns aren't filed, that is the entire call.** Address
   it head-on and pivot to the product built for it.
2. **Ask about deposits, not income, when the borrower is self-employed.** Income is a tax
   concept. Deposits are the qualifying number on the program they need.
3. **A credit event with a documentable cause is a different animal.** Fraud, bank errors,
   and expired autopay cards can sometimes be explained. Ask for the paperwork.
4. **Never promise to run numbers you can't run.** The borrower here was more honest about
   his own file than the LO was — *"probably you got to see this first before I waste your
   time or money."* When the borrower is managing your expectations, you've lost the
   advisor seat.

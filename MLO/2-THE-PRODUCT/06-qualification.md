# Unit 7 — Qualification

Who gets approved, who doesn't, and why. Everything here answers one of the two questions
from Unit 1: *will they pay it back*, and *is the house worth enough if they don't*.

---

## The frame — the Four Cs

Every document request, every condition, every decline maps to one of four buckets.

| C | The question | What gets examined |
|---|---|---|
| **Credit** | Do they pay people back? | FICO, history, derogatories |
| **Capacity** | Can they afford it? | Income, employment, DTI |
| **Capital** | Do they have money? | Down payment, reserves, source of funds |
| **Collateral** | Is the house worth it? | Appraisal, LTV, condition, marketability |

When a borrower asks *"why do you need this?"* — the answer is always one of these four.
Naming which one makes you sound like you understand the process, because you do.

---

## ⭐ The compensating factor principle

**The most useful concept in this unit.**

Underwriting is **not** a checklist of independent pass/fail tests. It's a risk assessment
**in aggregate**. Weakness in one area can be offset by strength in another.

| Weakness | Compensating strength |
|---|---|
| High DTI | Large reserves · long job tenure · high FICO · low LTV |
| Low FICO | Large down payment · low DTI · substantial reserves |
| Short job history | Same field · degree in the field · large reserves |
| Recent credit event | Documented one-time cause · strong recovery · low LTV |
| Thin credit file | Alternative tradelines · large down payment |

**Practical consequence:** when a borrower says "I got denied," that's one lender's model,
not the market. Ask what the stated reason was, then look for the offset.

**The three broker moves when something looks marginal:**
1. **Run LPA if DU referred it** (they weight risk differently — one often approves what
   the other won't)
2. **Ask your AE** which lender has the friendliest overlay for this exact profile
3. **Check whether paying off one small debt** fixes the DTI

# CREDIT

## The score mechanics

**FICO 300–850.** Weighted roughly:

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---|
| Payment history | ~35% |
| Amounts owed (utilization) | ~30% |
| Length of credit history | ~15% |
| New credit / inquiries | ~10% |
| Credit mix | ~10% |

**Tri-merge:** one report pulling all three bureaus. The **middle** score governs. On a
joint application, typically the **lower borrower's middle score**.

### ⚠️ The score gap nobody warns borrowers about
Mortgage lending uses **older FICO models** than the ones consumers see on free apps. The
mortgage pull typically comes back **20–40 points LOWER**.

Tell them before you pull, not after. It prevents a genuinely damaging conversation.

## Derogatories by severity

| Item | Severity | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Mortgage late** | **Most severe** | Even one 30-day is material |
| Foreclosure / short sale | Severe | Long waiting periods |
| Bankruptcy | Severe | Waiting period by chapter |
| Judgment / tax lien | Severe | Usually must be paid or on a documented plan |
| Collection | Moderate | May need payoff depending on size and program |
| Charge-off | Moderate | Similar |
| Installment late (car, student) | Moderate | |
| Revolving late (credit card) | Lower | |

## Waiting periods after major events

| Event | Conventional | FHA | VA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chapter 7 bankruptcy | **4 years** | **2 years** | 2 years |
| Chapter 13 | 2 yrs from discharge | 1 yr of on-time payments w/ court approval | 1 year |
| Foreclosure | **7 years** | **3 years** | 2 years |
| Short sale | 4 years | 3 years | 2 years |
| Deed in lieu | 4 years | 3 years | 2 years |

`[Vary by program version and lender overlay — verify per file.]`

**Extenuating circumstances** — documented, genuinely non-recurring events outside the
borrower's control — can shorten these. A documented medical catastrophe or death of a
wage earner sometimes qualifies. Job loss usually doesn't, because underwriting treats it
as foreseeable.

> **Worth knowing:** someone foreclosed on in 2022 is **FHA-eligible right now** and
> probably has no idea.

## The 30-day mortgage late — its own section

The single most predictive negative item in mortgage underwriting. **Many cash-out
programs require 0x30 in the last 12 months** — meaning a recent mortgage late can
disqualify the loan outright, not merely worsen pricing.

### But the CAUSE changes everything

| Cause | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Borrower couldn't afford the payment | Serious. Little to work with. |
| **Servicer error** | Often disputable and removable |
| **Payment method failure** — expired card on autopay, closed account, fraud freeze | **Potentially documentable** |
| Escrow shortage they weren't told about | Often explainable |

**What to do when you hear about one:**
1. Let them tell the whole story. **Do not interrupt.**
2. Ask exactly *when*, and whether it was the mortgage or something else
3. Ask what caused it — listen for a documentable cause
4. **Ask for the paperwork:** *"Do you have anything in writing from the bank? A letter,
   a fraud case number, anything showing the card expired?"*
5. Be honest about the obstacle

A documented bank error is a completely different conversation with underwriting than
"I forgot to pay."

## The rapid rescore play

If a borrower sits a few points below a tier boundary (698, 718, 738, 758), paying down a
card can push them across. A **rapid rescore** updates the bureaus in days rather than
waiting a full cycle.

On a $300,000 loan, crossing one tier can be worth thousands. **Almost no originator
checks for this.**

## Utilization facts worth memorizing
- Target **under 30%** per card; under 10% is better
- **Paying to zero isn't always optimal** — a small reported balance can score better
- **Never close old cards.** It shortens average age and cuts available credit — both
  lower the score. Borrowers do this thinking it helps.

## Non-traditional credit
Thin or no credit file → **alternative tradelines**: rent history (12 months verifiable),
utilities, insurance, cell phone, childcare. Usually requires **manual underwriting** and
typically three tradelines. More common on FHA. Slow and paperwork-heavy, but real.

# CAPACITY (income)

## The governing principle
Underwriting counts income that is **stable, verifiable, and likely to continue.**
Everything below follows from that sentence.

## Documentation by borrower type

| Type | Documents |
|---|---|
| **W-2 salaried** | 30 days paystubs · 2 years W-2s · verbal VOE before closing |
| **W-2 hourly** | Same, plus a 2-year average if hours vary |
| **Commission / bonus / OT** | **2-year history required**, then averaged |
| **Self-employed** | 2 years personal + business returns · YTD P&L · K-1s |
| **1099 contractor** | 2 years 1099s and returns |
| **Retired** | Award letters · 1099-R · statements |
| **Rental** | Schedule E, or leases |

## The calculations

**Salaried:** `annual ÷ 12`

**Hourly:** `rate × guaranteed weekly hours × 52 ÷ 12` (average if variable)

**Bonus/commission/OT:** `(year 1 + year 2) ÷ 24`

**⚠️ The declining income rule:** if the current year is LOWER than the prior year,
underwriting generally uses the **lower** figure and may require an explanation of why
it won't keep declining.

## The two-year rule — what it catches
- Bonus/commission under 2 years generally **can't be used**
- Self-employment under 2 years is usually **excluded**
- A long employment gap can require re-establishing history

**Gaps:** under 30 days is usually fine. Longer needs explanation. Over six months may
require re-establishing a 2-year history — though returning to the same field helps.

**Protected leave** (parental, medical) is documentable and should be treated as leave,
**not** as a job gap. Get an HR letter confirming dates and that compensation structure
is unchanged. This is worth real money: a borrower whose W-2 is suppressed by 12 weeks of
leave may qualify on a properly annualized figure instead of a dragged-down average.

## Self-employed — the two paths

### Path A: tax returns (with add-backs)
```
Qualifying = net profit + depreciation + depletion + amortization
             + business use of home + documented one-time losses
```

**Depreciation is where the money hides** — especially trucking, construction, and
equipment-heavy businesses.

**By entity:**

| Entity | Form | Income lives at |
|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietor | Schedule C | Net profit, line 31 |
| Partnership | 1065 + K-1 | K-1 ordinary income + guaranteed payments |
| S-corp | 1120S + K-1 | K-1 income **+ the W-2 wages the owner pays himself** |
| C-corp | 1120 | W-2 wages; dividends only with history |

**Always ask the entity type.** It changes the entire calculation.

### Path B: bank statements (when returns don't work)
```
(total deposits ÷ months) × (1 − expense factor)
```
**The unlocking question:** *"In a normal month, how much is DEPOSITED into the business
account? Gross deposits — not profit."*

## Other income

| Type | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Rental** | gross rent × 0.75 (25% vacancy/maintenance haircut). Schedule E loss counts as a **liability**. |
| **Retirement / SS / pension** | Award letters; must continue 3+ years. **Much of it is non-taxable → grosses up.** |
| **Disability** | Award letter; must show continuance. **VA disability is tax-free → grosses up AND drives the funding fee exemption.** |
| **Alimony / child support** | Court order + ~6 months receipt history + must continue 3+ years |
| **Part-time / second job** | Generally 2-year history |
| **Assets as income** | Asset depletion (non-QM) |

**Grossing up:** `non-taxable × 1.25`. **Ask every borrower: "is any of your income
non-taxable?"** Most don't know it matters.

## The six-question income interview
1. **W-2, 1099, or self-employed?**
2. If W-2: *salary or hourly? any OT/bonus/commission? how long there?*
3. If self-employed: *how long? entity type? returns filed? and — in a normal month, how
   much is deposited into the business account?*
4. **Any other income — rental, retirement, disability, child support, side business?**
5. **Is any of it non-taxable?**
6. **Any employment gaps in the last two years?**

Two minutes. Gives you almost everything.

# CAPITAL (assets)

## The three jobs assets must do
1. Cover the **down payment**
2. Cover **closing costs and prepaids**
3. Leave **reserves** afterward

A borrower can pass the first two and fail the third. That's how jumbo files die.

## Documentation
**2 months of statements — ALL PAGES**, including the blank ones. "Page 3 of 5" gets
returned every time.

**What underwriting looks for:**
- Ending balances
- **Large or unusual deposits**
- NSF activity / overdrafts
- **Undisclosed debts** — a recurring payment leaving the account with no matching
  tradeline generates a condition

## Sourcing and seasoning

**Seasoning:** funds in the account **60+ days** are generally seasoned and don't require
sourcing.

**Sourcing:** any deposit unusual relative to their income pattern gets questioned.

| Source | OK? | Documentation needed |
|---|---|---|
| Payroll | ✅ | Matches paystubs |
| Tax refund | ✅ | Return or IRS record |
| Asset sale | ✅ | Bill of sale, title transfer |
| **Gift** | ✅ | Gift letter + donor's ability + transfer trail |
| Retirement withdrawal | ✅ | Statement showing the withdrawal |
| Transfer between own accounts | ✅ | Statements for **both** accounts |
| Loan from family | ⚠️ | Counts as **debt** unless properly gifted |
| **Cash deposit** | ❌ | **Generally unusable** |

### ⚠️ The cash problem
**Undocumented cash cannot be used.** This devastates self-employed borrowers, tipped
workers, and anyone "saving cash at home."

**Warn early:** if any of the down payment is cash at home, it must be deposited **now**
— it needs 60 days of seasoning before it counts.

### Gift funds — the checklist
1. **Gift letter** — donor name, relationship, amount, property address, and explicit
   statement that **repayment is not expected**
2. Evidence of the **donor's ability** to give (their statement)
3. **Paper trail** — out of the donor's account, into the borrower's

FHA allows **100%** of the down payment to be gifted. **The trap:** a "gift" that's
actually a loan is fraud. Ask directly and don't coach the answer.

## Reserves
```
Reserves (months) = liquid assets after closing ÷ monthly PITI
```

| Scenario | Typical requirement |
|---|---|
| Conventional primary | 0–2 months |
| Second home | 2–6 months |
| Investment property | 6+ months |
| **Jumbo** | **6–12+ months** |
| Multiple financed properties | Escalating |

Retirement accounts usually count at a **discounted percentage** and only if accessible.

**⭐ Reserves are the single most powerful compensating factor.** Always ask: *"After the
down payment and closing costs, roughly what's left?"* Most originators only ask whether
there's enough to close.

# COLLATERAL (the property)

## The appraisal
An independent opinion of value. It establishes **LTV**, which drives pricing and
eligibility.

**Value = appraised value — EXCEPT on a purchase, where it's the LOWER of appraised
value or purchase price.** A borrower who negotiates $380,000 on a home appraising at
$400,000 still has LTV computed on $380,000.

### Appraiser independence — a hard rule
You may **not** attempt to influence value, suggest a target, or select the appraiser
directly. This is federal law and violating it ends careers.

**You may** provide factual information — recent comparable sales, a list of improvements
— through the proper channel (the AMC or lender, never directly to the appraiser).

### Appraisal waivers
AUS sometimes grants a waiver (**PIW**) when the profile supports it. Saves several
hundred dollars and about a week.

### When value comes in low — the options, in order
1. **Reconsideration of value (ROV)** with documented better comps — real process, low
   success rate
2. **Increase the down payment** to preserve LTV
3. **Reduce the loan amount**
4. **Renegotiate the price** (purchase only)
5. Start over with a different lender — borrower pays for a new appraisal

**Set the expectation before it happens:** the appraisal is the one piece neither of you
controls.

## Property types that complicate the file

| Type | The issue |
|---|---|
| **Condo** | Project must be **warrantable** — HOA finances, owner-occupancy ratio, litigation, single-entity concentration. Non-warrantable needs specialty lending. |
| **Manufactured** | Permanent foundation required, HUD tags, limited lender pool |
| **Rural / large acreage** | Hard to find comps; excess land may not be valued |
| **Mixed use** | Commercial portion creates eligibility problems |
| **Unique construction** | Log, dome, earth-sheltered — marketability concerns |
| **Poor condition** | FHA/VA minimum property standards especially strict |

**Ask about property type early.** Discovering a non-warrantable condo at appraisal is
three wasted weeks.

## Title
Title research finds hidden claims: old liens, judgments, contractor liens, easements,
name mismatches, a deceased co-owner.

**Ask early:** *"Any liens or judgments we should know about? Anything from a divorce?"*
An unexpected lien at week three kills refinances.

## The pre-submission checklist

Before any file goes to underwriting, answer these. If you can't, you have a condition
coming.

| C | Question |
|---|---|
| **Credit** | What's the worst item on this report, and do I have the LOE + documentation? |
| **Capacity** | Is this income stable, documentable, and likely to continue? |
| **Capital** | Where did every dollar come from, and what's left after closing? |
| **Collateral** | Will the appraisal support this, and is the property lendable? |

Answering these upfront is what separates files that close in 21 days from files that
take 45.

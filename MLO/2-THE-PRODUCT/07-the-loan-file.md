# Unit 8 — The File

The complete life of a loan, from first contact to funding. This is "full service
delivery" — what actually has to happen, who does it, in what order, and where things
break.

---

## The ten stages at a glance

```
 1. PRE-QUALIFICATION      informal · no docs · no commitment
 2. APPLICATION            the six items · the 1003 · clock starts
 3. DISCLOSURES            LE within 3 business days · intent to proceed
 4. DOCUMENT COLLECTION    processor builds the file
 5. AUS + SUBMISSION       DU/LPA runs · file goes to the lender
 6. APPRAISAL + TITLE      ordered in parallel · both take days-to-weeks
 7. UNDERWRITING           human review · conditional approval issued
 8. CONDITIONS             the back-and-forth · where most delay lives
 9. CLEAR TO CLOSE → CD    docs drawn · CD must land 3 business days before closing
10. CLOSING → FUNDING      signing · rescission (refi) · wire · recording
```

**Realistic total: 21–45 days.**

---

## Stage 1 — Pre-qualification

**What it is:** an informal estimate based on stated (unverified) information.

- No documents required
- No credit pulled (or a soft pull)
- **Not binding on anyone**
- Produces a rough range, not a commitment

**vs. Pre-APPROVAL:** credit pulled, income and assets documented, run through AUS.
Materially stronger. A seller can rely on it.

**The line that matters:** a pre-qualification that falls apart destroys credibility with
borrowers and agents alike. Be clear about which one you're issuing.

---

## Stage 2 — Application

### ⚠️ The six items — MEMORIZE

Collecting **all six** of these legally constitutes an **application** under TRID, and
the disclosure clock starts:

```
1. Borrower's name
2. Borrower's income
3. Borrower's Social Security number
4. Property address
5. Estimated property value
6. Loan amount sought
```

**Know exactly where that line is.** Either stay clearly on the pre-application side, or
cross it deliberately and get the Loan Estimate out within three business days. What you
must never do is cross it by accident.

### The 1003
The standard application form. Captures borrower info, employment, income, assets,
liabilities, property, declarations, and demographic information (government monitoring —
required to ask, borrower may decline, you record the refusal correctly).

**Send a complete 1003 to processing. No blanks.**

---

## Stage 3 — Disclosures

| Document | Timing | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Loan Estimate (LE)** | **Within 3 business days** of application | Standardized cost estimate |
| **Intent to Proceed** | After the LE | Required before charging any fee beyond a credit report |
| Home Loan Toolkit | With LE on purchases | Consumer education |
| List of Service Providers | If borrower may shop | For services they can choose |

### The LE as a comparison tool
The LE is a **federal standardized form** — same layout from every lender. **Page 2,
section A** is what the originator is charging. A borrower can lay two LEs side by side
and compare line by line.

This is why "send me their Loan Estimate, not a screenshot of a rate" is the correct
response to a competing quote.

### Tolerances — why sloppy estimating costs money

| Tolerance | Applies to | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Zero tolerance** | Your origination charges, points, lender fees, transfer taxes | **Cannot increase at all** |
| **10% cumulative** | Services borrower can shop for from your list; recording fees | Total may rise up to 10% |
| **No tolerance** | Prepaids, escrow deposits, services shopped outside your list | May change |

Exceed a tolerance without a valid reason → the company **cures** (refunds the borrower).

**Changed circumstance** can reset tolerances: new information, borrower request, rate
lock, expired LE, or a genuine change in the transaction. It must be **documented at the
time**. "The rate went up" is not a changed circumstance. "Borrower disclosed a second
property, changing occupancy and pricing" is.

---

## Stage 4 — Document collection

The processor builds the file. Standard package:

**Income:** 30 days paystubs · 2 years W-2s · 2 years returns (all schedules, if
self-employed) · YTD P&L · K-1s · award letters

**Assets:** 2 months statements, **all pages** · retirement statements · gift letters +
donor evidence + transfer trail

**Identity:** driver's license · Social Security card (sometimes)

**Property:** purchase contract · homeowners insurance quote/binder · HOA documents ·
current mortgage statement (refi) · payoff (refi)

**Situational:** LOEs for derogatories and inquiries · divorce decree · bankruptcy
discharge papers · lease agreements · VA COE

### The borrower-behavior rules — give these at application

> **Four rules until closing:**
> 1. **Don't open new credit.** No car, no furniture, no store card, not even a
>    zero-interest offer.
> 2. **Don't close existing accounts either.**
> 3. **Don't change jobs** without calling first.
> 4. **Don't move large amounts of money** between accounts — and if you do, keep every
>    statement.

**Credit is re-pulled before closing.** A new car loan in week three has killed an
enormous number of deals.

---

## Stage 5 — AUS and submission

The file runs through **DU** (Fannie) or **LPA** (Freddie). FHA and VA run their own
overlays on these engines.

### The findings

| Result | Meaning | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| **Approve / Eligible** (DU) or **Accept** (LPA) | Green light, subject to conditions | Proceed |
| **Approve / Ineligible** | Credit risk fine, but a rule is violated (loan limit, occupancy, product) | Fix the structural issue, rerun |
| **Refer / Eligible** | Needs a human underwriter | Manual underwriting |
| **Refer with Caution** | Serious risk concerns | Usually restructure or decline |

### ⭐ The broker move
**DU and LPA weight risk differently.** If DU refers it, run LPA before doing anything
else. One frequently approves what the other won't. Most retail LOs have access to one.
You have both. This costs nothing and saves real files.

### What AUS gives you
1. The decision
2. **A conditions list** — your roadmap. Read it the day it arrives.
3. Sometimes relief — reduced documentation, or an **appraisal waiver**

---

## Stage 6 — Appraisal and title (run in parallel)

### Appraisal
Ordered through an **AMC** (appraisal management company) to preserve independence. You
never pick the appraiser.

**Typical timeline: 5–14 days** — longer in rural areas or busy markets.

Borrower must receive a copy **promptly upon completion**, and no later than **3 business
days before consummation** (ECOA requirement).

### Title
Title company researches ownership and finds anything attached to the property. Common
surprises: old liens, judgments, contractor liens, easements, name mismatches, a
deceased co-owner, or an unreleased prior mortgage.

**Both of these should be ordered as early as the file allows.** They're the longest
external dependencies and neither is under your control.

---

## Stage 7 — Underwriting

A human underwriter reviews everything against guidelines and the AUS findings, then
issues a **conditional approval** — approved, subject to a list.

### How to work with underwriters
- **Never argue.** Present facts and documentation.
- An **LOE** is your primary tool for anything unusual
- **Anticipate conditions.** A large deposit will *always* be questioned — document the
  source before they ask
- If you believe they're wrong on a guideline, escalate politely through your **AE** with
  the guideline citation, not with volume

### Writing a good LOE
Short. Factual. States what happened, when, that it's resolved, and why it won't recur.
Signed, dated, **with documentation attached**.

> *"In March 2026, my auto-payment for my mortgage failed because the debit card on file
> had expired. I was not aware the payment had not processed until the servicer notified
> me, at which point I paid immediately. I have since set up payment directly from my
> checking account rather than a debit card. Attached is the notice from the card issuer
> showing the expiration date and my bank statement showing the payment on [date]. I have
> had no other late payments in the past two years."*

Write these for your borrowers. Most can't, and a bad LOE makes things worse.

## Stage 8 — Conditions

**Where most delay lives.** Conditions come in three flavors:

| Type | Must clear before |
|---|---|
| **PTA** — Prior to Approval | The file is approved |
| **PTD** — Prior to Docs | Closing documents are drawn |
| **PTF** — Prior to Funding | Money moves |

### The ten predictable conditions — pre-clear these
1. LOE for any credit inquiry in the last 90 days
2. LOE + documentation for **any** derogatory
3. Source of **any** large deposit
4. All pages of every bank statement, including blanks
5. Gift letter + donor's ability + transfer trail
6. **Verbal VOE** before closing
7. Homeowners insurance binder
8. Payoff statements on debts being paid at closing
9. Explanation of any employment gap
10. Updated paystub if the file ages past 30 days

**Every condition you anticipate is two days you don't lose.**

---

## Stage 9 — Clear to Close and the CD

**CTC** = all conditions satisfied; closing documents can be drawn.

### The Closing Disclosure — the hard deadline

> **The borrower must RECEIVE the CD at least 3 business days before consummation.**

- If mailed or emailed, it's deemed received **3 business days after sending** (the
  mailbox rule) — so plan backward
- Certain changes **restart** the 3-day clock: APR increasing beyond tolerance, the loan
  product changing, or a prepayment penalty being added
- Most changes do **not** restart it

**This deadline is federal and cannot be shortened.** Build every timeline around it.

---

## Stage 10 — Closing and funding

### The signing
At a title company, escrow office, or with a mobile notary. Borrower signs the note,
the security instrument, and the closing package.

### Rescission — refinances only
On a **refinance of a primary residence**, the borrower has **3 business days after
signing** to cancel. The loan does **not** fund until that window expires.

- **Purchases: no rescission.** Fund and record same day.
- **Refinances on a primary: 3-day wait.** Always.
- Saturdays count; Sundays and federal holidays don't.

### Funding and recording
Lender wires the money. Title/escrow disburses — paying off the old loan on a refi, or
the seller on a purchase. The security instrument is **recorded** with the county, which
is what makes the lien official.

### Post-closing
The loan is sold, servicing is assigned, and the borrower receives a "hello" letter from
the servicer — often within weeks. **Tell them this is coming at application.** It's
normal, and it prevents an angry call in month two.

## The realistic timeline

| Stage | Typical | What drives it |
|---|---|---|
| Application → disclosures out | 1–3 days | **You** |
| Disclosures → submission | 2–5 days | **Borrower responsiveness** |
| Submission → initial UW | 1–5 days | Lender turn times |
| Conditions cleared | 3–14 days | **Mostly the borrower** |
| Appraisal ordered → received | 5–14 days | Appraiser availability |
| CTC → docs | 1–3 days | Lender |
| Docs → closing | **3 days minimum** | **CD rule — non-negotiable** |
| Closing → funding (refi primary) | **3 days** | **Rescission — non-negotiable** |

**Six days of that are federally mandated waiting.** No amount of hustle compresses them.

### Setting expectations honestly
> *"Realistically three to four weeks. The two things that drive it are how fast you get
> me documents and how fast the appraiser gets out. The rest is a federal three-day
> waiting period before closing that nobody can shorten."*

**Do not promise 10 days.** It's achievable on a clean file with a fast lender; it is not
the median. Quote a range and beat it.

---

## Turn times and lender selection

Turn times vary substantially between lenders and shift with volume. A lender advertising
48-hour underwriting can be at 10 days during a refi boom.

**Ask your AE for current actual turn times before promising a closing date** — not their
marketing number.

---

## The seven deal-killers

| Who | How | Prevent it by |
|---|---|---|
| **The borrower** | New credit, job change, moving money, missing a payment | The four rules, given at application, in writing |
| **The appraiser** | Value comes in low | Setting expectations; providing comps through proper channels |
| **The underwriter** | Conditions you didn't anticipate | Structuring the file correctly upfront |
| **Title** | Undisclosed liens or judgments | Asking about them at application |
| **The insurance market** | Uninsurable or unaffordable | Asking early in FL/coastal/wildfire markets |
| **The competitor's retention desk** | Calling your borrower to keep them | Staying in contact |
| **You** | Overpromising | Never promising a rate before pricing, or a date before checking turn times |

## When something goes wrong

It will. What matters is the handling.

1. **Call immediately.** Never let a borrower discover a problem on their own.
2. **Lead with the solution**, not the problem.
3. **Never blame the underwriter** to the borrower. You're one team.
4. **Give a specific next step and timeline.**

| Problem | Options |
|---|---|
| Appraisal low | ROV with comps · larger down payment · reduce loan · renegotiate price |
| Income insufficient | Add co-borrower · pay off debt · reduce loan · different product |
| Credit dropped | Rapid rescore · find the cause · restructure |
| Large deposit unsourced | Get documentation; if impossible, exclude the funds |
| Condition can't be met | Escalate through the AE · consider a different lender |
| Lock expiring | Extend (costs money) or reprice |

**Almost every problem has an option.** The originator who calls with three options is
the one who keeps the file — and gets the referral even when the deal dies.

---

## Communication cadence

Silence kills deals. A borrower who hasn't heard from you in a week starts wondering.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Application day | Confirm next steps in writing |
| Every 2–3 days | Status update — **"nothing new, appraisal's Thursday" counts** |
| Every milestone | Submitted · appraisal in · approved · CTC |
| Any problem | **Immediately** |

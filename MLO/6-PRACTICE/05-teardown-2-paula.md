# Teardown 2 — Paula (hostile, 2.3%, $200k cash out)

**The call:** ~5 minutes. Trigger lead. Borrower opens hostile, refuses three times, then
gives complete financial information and books a callback.

**Surface verdict:** an incredible save. Textbook persistence.

**Actual verdict:** the most commercially impressive and the most legally dangerous call
of the four. Study the persuasion. Do not copy the behavior.

## The legal problem

### Three clear refusals, ignored

FCC rules require honoring revocation of consent through **any reasonable means**. There
is no magic word. "I'm not interested" — repeated three times, plus "I don't need
anything" and "I don't want a mortgage from you guys" — is a revocation under any
reading.

The pitch continued through all three.

**The standard from `../4-COMPLIANCE/01-cold-calling-law.md`:**

| Signal | Correct response |
|---|---|
| Refusal #1 (reflexive) | **One** reframe is defensible |
| Refusal #2 (clear) | **Stop** |
| Refusal #3 + "I don't want a mortgage from you guys" | Should never have been reached |

### She told him the truth and he contradicted her

> Paula: *"I don't think that you received a correspondence from one of my 20-year
> lenders. **You saw my credit score, that's all.**"*

**She diagnosed the trigger lead correctly and out loud.** She knew exactly what had
happened.

The response asserted the false claim again, and more explicitly:

> ❌ *"So that is how we got your information. So, you know, **you sent us information**."*

That is a knowing false statement of material fact, made after the consumer corrected it,
**on a recording**. UDAAP-deceptive. `../4-COMPLIANCE/06-the-never-say-list.md`.

**What should have happened at the first denial:**

> *"You know what — you're right, and I apologize. That was sloppy language on my part.
> Let me just be straight about why I'm calling and if it's not useful I'll let you go."*

Total cost: nothing. Likely outcome: better, because she'd already caught him and backing
down honestly is disarming.

### The exposure

Paula is exactly the profile of a TCPA plaintiff: articulate, aware of the trigger-lead
mechanism, clearly annoyed, and precise in her refusals. If she's in a mini-TCPA state,
this call is **$500–$20,000** of statutory exposure sitting on a recording.

## Why she converted anyway — the part worth studying

She did convert. She asked "what's your interest rate?" and then gave everything. That's
real and it's worth understanding.

**1. He never argued with her emotion.** He deflected calmly every time. No defensiveness,
no matching her energy, no "ma'am, if you'd just let me finish." Tonally, this is
genuinely skilled.

**2. The "second option" reframe is disarming.**
> *"We're just calling to put a second option on the table for you."*

Low commitment. Doesn't ask her to leave anyone or do anything.

**3. The specific benefit claim gave her something to push against.**
> *"I mean, you don't want to save some money on a monthly basis?"*

Slightly clumsy, but it forced a decision about money rather than about him, and that's
what flipped her.

**4. He had credibility to offer.** "Wholesale broker, 50 lenders, access to pricing
sheets" is a real answer to "why should I talk to you."

> **The lesson: the persuasion mechanics were sound. The problem is that they were
> deployed past a legal stop sign.** Those are separable. Use the mechanics on someone who
> hasn't revoked.

## The sales failures

Even setting compliance aside, this call is a mess analytically.

### ❌ 1. The math is catastrophic and nobody ran it

Paula has a **2.3% rate on a 15-year term** with a **$180,000 balance**. She's being
moved to a **30-year at ~7%** with a **$380,000 balance**.

Directionally, what that means:

| | Current | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Rate | 2.3% | ~7% |
| Term | 15-year (partially elapsed) | 30-year, restarted |
| Balance | $180,000 | ~$380,000 |
| P&I | $1,272 | ~$2,500 |

She is more than tripling her interest rate, doubling her balance, and restarting a term
she's partway through — at an age where she's joking about not living to see it paid off.

**Nobody did that math with her.** Nobody mentioned it. The entire call treats this as
routine.

> **This is the fiduciary failure of the four transcripts.** Not illegal on its own — but
> `../4-COMPLIANCE/04-respa-ecoa-udaap.md` describes the UDAAP "abusive"
> prong as taking unreasonable advantage of a consumer's reliance on you to act in their
> interest. This is what that looks like in practice.

### ❌ 2. Nobody asked what the $200,000 was for

Again. Same as Call 1.

$200,000 against $800,000 of equity, from someone whose age is a live factor. Is it
medical? Family? A property? Long-term care? Being taken advantage of by someone else?

**You cannot advise this borrower without knowing.** And the right answer might well have
been "don't do this at all."

### ❌ 3. The most important thing she said was laughed past

> Paula: *"...maybe I'll be dead by the time it's due."*
> LO: *"Yeah, hopefully not. I mean, yeah, I could be, I'm not that young, you know?"*
> LO: *"Yeah, I hear you. I got you. Okay, man."*

That is a real statement about her age, her time horizon, and implicitly her estate.

**It should have been the entire conversation.** It's the single strongest argument
against putting her into a restarted 30-year term, and it's the doorway to the questions
that actually serve her:

> *"That's the second time you've mentioned that, and I want to take it seriously for a
> second. If the timeline is shorter than a 30-year mortgage, that genuinely changes what
> I'd recommend. Are you planning to stay in this house? Is there family who'd inherit
> it? Because there are a couple of ways to structure this that make a lot more sense than
> a brand-new 30-year, and I'd rather get this right than fast."*

That question is what an advisor asks. See `../5-SALES/15-hard-cases.md` on
emotional disclosures.

### ❌ 4. The second lien — again

$800,000 in equity. A 2.3% rate on $180,000.

**Leave the first mortgage alone.** Put a second behind it for the $200,000. She keeps
2.3% on the original balance and pays market only on the new money.

Never mentioned. `../3-GO-DEEPER/product-6-second-liens-heloc.md`.

### ❌ 5. The escrow tangent ate the call

A long stretch of the call is spent on whether to escrow taxes and insurance.

It's fine information, but it's **tactically worthless**. She was hostile ninety seconds
earlier and hasn't been sold on anything. Explaining escrow mechanics to someone who
hasn't agreed to the concept is a beginner's instinct — teaching what you know rather
than finding out what they need.

That time should have gone to *"what's the $200,000 for?"*

### ❌ 6. Her insurance is the actual story and it was missed

> *"My homeowner's insurance is really high... I think it's $6,000 on that."*

**$6,000/year on a $950,000 home is a real problem**, and on a nearly-$1M property that's
worth genuine attention — it's roughly $500/month.

An advisor would have said:

> *"Six thousand a year is high — when did you last shop that? On a home this size,
> getting a couple of competitive quotes could save you real money every month, and
> honestly that might get you further than anything I'm going to do with the mortgage."*

Costs you nothing. Enormous trust. And it addresses the actual monthly-payment problem
she's circling.

### ❌ 7. No NMLS ID, no recorded-line disclosure

Same as every call in the set.

## The four lessons

1. **Hostility and revocation are different things.** Classify before you respond. Paula
   gave both signals, and the revocation is the one that governs.
2. **When a borrower says you're wrong about how you got their data — believe them
   instantly.** Arguing provenance is indefensible and it's the moment a normal call
   becomes evidence.
3. **Run the math before you sell the loan.** 2.3% on a 15-year going to 7% on a 30-year
   is a decision that needs to be *examined*, not processed.
4. **When someone tells you something human, stop.** "Maybe I'll be dead by the time it's
   due" was the most important sentence in the call and it was treated as a joke.

---

## The honest summary

If you want to learn how to stay calm under hostility and reframe without arguing, this
call is a genuinely good study. The tonality is excellent.

If you copy what it does with a revocation, you will eventually be a defendant.

**Take the technique. Leave the behavior.**

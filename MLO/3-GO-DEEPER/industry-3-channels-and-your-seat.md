# Channels and Your Seat

You work at a **wholesale broker**. This explains what that means, why it's a genuinely
strong seat, and how to explain it to a borrower in fifteen seconds.

---

## The four channels

| Channel | Whose money | Who underwrites | Product menu |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Retail bank / credit union** | Their own | Themselves | One — theirs |
| **Retail mortgage banker** | Warehouse line, sold after | Themselves | One — theirs |
| **Correspondent** | Own line, sold to investors | Themselves, to investor guidelines | Several investors |
| **Wholesale broker (you)** | The lender's | The lender | **50+ lenders** |

---

## What a wholesale broker actually does

You are an **intermediary with a shopping cart**.

```
Borrower → YOU → 50+ wholesale lenders → whichever one is best
```

You take the application, structure the file, choose the lender, and manage the process.
The lender underwrites and funds. Your compensation is regulated and disclosed.

### The mechanics

1. **Application** — you take it
2. **Pricing** — you run the scenario through a **pricing engine** that returns rates from
   every lender you're approved with
3. **Lender selection** — you pick, based on price, guidelines, turn time, and your
   read on which lender will actually close it
4. **Submission** — file goes to that lender's underwriting
5. **Processing** — you or your processor clears conditions
6. **Funding** — the lender funds, you get paid

### Step 3 is your actual skill

Anyone can sort a pricing engine by lowest rate. **Knowing which lender will actually
close a specific file is the job.**

| Borrower profile | Consideration |
|---|---|
| Self-employed, complex returns | Which lenders' underwriters actually understand P&Ls |
| Recent credit event | Who has the most workable overlays |
| Needs to close in 12 days | Whose turn times are real, not advertised |
| Condo in a non-warrantable project | Who will even look at it |
| Non-QM / bank statement | Who's genuinely competitive, not just listed |

**Cheapest rate on a file that dies in underwriting is worth zero.** This judgment is what
you're building over your first two years, and it's why AE relationships matter.

## Why the broker channel is a good seat

### 1. Structurally better pricing
Wholesale lenders don't carry retail overhead — no branches, no consumer advertising, no
retail loan officers. They pass some of that through. Broker-originated loans frequently
price better than the same lender's retail channel.

### 2. Real optionality
When one lender says no, you go to another. A retail LO with a denial has nothing left to
offer. **You have forty-nine more shots.** This is a genuine, material advantage for the
borrower, and it's the strongest thing you can say on a call.

### 3. Product breadth
Conventional, FHA, VA, USDA, jumbo, non-QM, bank statement, DSCR, seconds, HELOCs. A bank
LO can't offer what their bank doesn't do.

### 4. Speed
Wholesale lenders compete on turn times. In the transcripts the originator claims 10–14
days versus PennyMac's 30–45. That kind of gap is real and it's a legitimate selling
point — **when it's true**. Verify your lenders' actual current turn times before quoting
them.

### 5. Cleanest incentives in the industry
The **LO Compensation Rule** means your pay cannot vary based on loan terms. Combined with
having fifty options, you have less structural incentive to steer than almost anyone else
in mortgage. This is worth explaining to borrowers.

### 6. Shortest path to ownership
Starting a brokerage requires far less capital than becoming a lender. Many broker owners
started as LOs three or four years earlier.

## The honest downsides

Say these to yourself, not to borrowers — but know them.

- **You don't control underwriting.** When a lender is slow or difficult, you're the one
  explaining it.
- **You don't control pricing.** You get what the rate sheet says.
- **Brand recognition is low.** "Mortgage One" means nothing to a borrower; "Wells Fargo"
  does. Your NMLS record and your own credibility have to carry it.
- **You must be approved with each lender**, and approvals can be pulled.
- **Comp is regulated and disclosed** — less flexibility than borrowers sometimes expect.

## Explaining it to a borrower

The transcripts do this well. Their version:

> *"We're a wholesale broker, so I partner with over 50 different lenders to try to get
> you the best deals on the market."*

Clear and effective. **The stronger version adds the mechanism and the honest caveat:**

> *"Here's the difference. A bank sells you its own money — one rate sheet, one set of
> rules, and if they say no, that's the end of the conversation. I send your file to about
> fifty lenders and they compete for it. Sometimes a bank still wins, and when that
> happens I'll tell you. But at a bank you only ever see one price, and you never find out
> what the other forty-nine would have said."*

**"Sometimes a bank still wins and I'll tell you"** is what makes the whole thing
credible.

### When they ask "so how do you get paid?"

Answer it directly. Evasion here is fatal.

> *"Straight answer: I'm paid a percentage of the loan amount, it's the same percentage on
> every loan regardless of what product you pick or what rate you take — that's federal
> law, specifically so I can't steer you into something that pays me more. It's disclosed
> in writing on your Loan Estimate. Either the lender pays it or you do, and I'll show you
> both ways."*

Most borrowers have never gotten a straight answer to that question. Giving one is worth
more than a quarter point.

## The wholesale AE

Your **Account Executive** at each lender is the most underused relationship in a new LO's
career.

**What a good AE does for you:**

- Tells you whether a scenario will actually fly *before* you submit
- Gets exceptions considered
- Escalates files that are stuck
- Warns you about guideline changes before they hit
- Teaches you their niche products

**How to be a broker AEs help:**

1. **Send clean files.** Reputation travels.
2. **Call before you submit** on anything unusual. Thirty seconds saves three weeks.
3. **Don't waste their time** on scenarios you haven't thought through.
4. **Give them volume** when they earn it.
5. **Know their niche.** Every lender has one — non-QM, VA, condos, jumbo, speed.

**Build a cheat sheet**: lender, AE name, phone, niche, typical turn time, known
overlays. That document is worth more than any script after your first six months.

## What you must be able to do

- Name the four channels and the difference between them
- Explain why lender selection, not rate sorting, is the actual skill
- Explain the broker advantage in fifteen seconds, with the honest caveat
- Answer "how do you get paid?" directly
- Describe what a good AE relationship gets you

**Self-check:** *"Why wouldn't I just go to my own bank?"*

> *"You might, and sometimes that's the right answer. Two things to know though. One, your
> bank has one rate sheet and one set of rules — if you don't fit, you don't fit, and
> that's the end of it. I've got about fifty lenders and they don't all have the same
> rules, so a no at one isn't a no everywhere. Two, wholesale pricing is usually sharper
> because there's no branch overhead built into it. Go get their number — genuinely. Then
> let me show you mine, and if theirs is better I'll tell you to take it."*

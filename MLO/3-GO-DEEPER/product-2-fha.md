# FHA Loans

Government-insured, credit-flexible, and carrying a cost most borrowers don't understand
until you explain it.

---

## What FHA is

Loans insured by the **Federal Housing Administration**, part of HUD. FHA doesn't lend —
it insures the lender against loss, which is what lets lenders accept weaker credit.

**Who it's for:** borrowers with lower credit scores, limited down payment, higher DTI, or
a recent credit event.

---

## Core parameters

| | Typical |
|---|---|
| Minimum FICO — 3.5% down | **580** |
| Minimum FICO — 10% down | **500** |
| Max DTI | 43% baseline; higher with compensating factors and AUS approval |
| Down payment | 3.5% (580+) |
| Gift funds | **100% of down payment may be gifted** |
| Occupancy | **Primary residence only** |
| Loan limits | County-specific; below conventional in most areas |

> `[Verify current limits at HUD's site and current overlays with your lenders.]`

## The "as low as 500" trap

**This appears in two of the four transcripts and it's misleading both times.**

> *"We take scores as low as a 500."*
> *"I mean the minimum is actually going to be a 500."*

**Technically true. Practically misleading**, for three reasons:

1. **500–579 requires 10% down**, not 3.5%
2. **Most wholesale lenders impose overlays** well above FHA's floor — many won't go below
   580, 600, or 620 regardless of what FHA permits
3. **Pricing at that tier is punishing**

Telling a borrower "we go to 500" when you have no lender who'll actually do it is
materially misleading — the UDAAP standard is whether a reasonable consumer would likely
be misled about something material. `../4-COMPLIANCE/06-the-never-say-list.md`

**The honest version:**

> *"FHA's floor is technically 500, but let me be straight with you. Below 580 you're
> looking at 10% down and a very short list of lenders who'll actually touch it. At 580
> you're at 3.5% down and it opens up. At 620 and up the whole market's available and
> conventional starts competing. Where do you think you're sitting?"*

## MIP — the real cost of FHA

This is the most important thing to understand about FHA, and the thing borrowers
understand least.

FHA charges **two** mortgage insurance premiums:

### Upfront MIP (UFMIP)
A percentage of the loan amount, paid at closing — almost always financed into the loan.

### Annual MIP
Charged monthly, as a percentage of the loan balance. Varies by loan term, LTV, and loan
amount.

### The part that matters most

**On most FHA loans originated with the minimum down payment, annual MIP lasts for the
life of the loan.** It does not fall off at 20% equity the way conventional PMI does.

| | Conventional PMI | FHA MIP |
|---|---|---|
| Cancellable at 80% LTV | ✅ Yes | ❌ Generally no |
| Automatic at 78% | ✅ Yes | ❌ Generally no |
| Removable by appreciation | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
| **Escape route** | Cancellation | **Refinance out of FHA** |

**This is the single biggest long-term cost difference between FHA and conventional**, and
it's why running both comparisons matters so much.

`[MIP percentages and the specific duration rules by LTV and term are periodically
revised by HUD. Verify current values before quoting.]`

### Why you care commercially

**Every FHA borrower is a future refinance.** Once they have 20%+ equity and credit that
supports conventional, refinancing out of FHA eliminates MIP entirely — often worth
$150–$300/month.

**Fasani in Call 4 has a 30-year FHA at 4.5%** and is paying MIP every month. Nobody
mentioned it. At 4.5% versus a 6.8% market he shouldn't refinance the first lien — but he
should *know* what he's paying, and it belongs in any long-term plan you build with him.

**Build an FHA-borrower list.** When they hit 20% equity, call them. It's one of the
easiest conversations in the business.

## FHA vs. conventional — the real comparison

Run both. Every time. The answer is not obvious.

| Factor | FHA better | Conventional better |
|---|---|---|
| FICO under 620 | ✅ | |
| FICO 680+ | | ✅ |
| Very low down payment | ✅ (3.5% at 580) | ✅ (3% at 620+) |
| Recent credit event | ✅ | |
| High DTI | ✅ | |
| **Long-term cost** | | ✅ **MIP vs. cancellable PMI** |
| Gift funds for entire down payment | ✅ | |
| Investment or second home | | ✅ (FHA is primary only) |
| Loan above FHA county limit | | ✅ |

**The rule of thumb:** below 620, FHA is usually the only option. Above 680, conventional
usually wins on total cost. **Between 620 and 680, run both** — it genuinely varies by
LLPA position and MI pricing.

### The honest positioning

> *"Two ways to do this. FHA gets you in with a lower score and a smaller down payment,
> but the mortgage insurance stays for the life of the loan — you only get out of it by
> refinancing. Conventional needs stronger credit, but the mortgage insurance comes off at
> 20% equity and then it's gone forever. Let me price both and show you what each costs
> over five years, not just what the payment is on day one."*

That comparison is what an advisor does. Presenting only one is what a salesperson does.

## Credit event waiting periods

FHA is more forgiving than conventional on seasoning:

| Event | FHA (typical) | Conventional (typical) |
|---|---|---|
| Chapter 7 bankruptcy | 2 years | 4 years |
| Chapter 13 | 1 year of on-time payments, with court approval | 2 years from discharge |
| Foreclosure | 3 years | 7 years |
| Short sale | 3 years | 4 years |

**Extenuating circumstances** — documented, genuinely one-time events outside the
borrower's control — can shorten these. Job loss generally doesn't qualify; a documented
medical catastrophe or death of a wage earner sometimes does.

> `[Waiting periods and extenuating-circumstance treatment vary by program and lender
> overlay. Verify per file.]`

**This is a real prospecting angle.** A borrower foreclosed on in 2022 is FHA-eligible
now and probably doesn't know it. Almost nobody calls them.

## FHA Streamline Refinance

FHA's fast-lane refinance.

| | |
|---|---|
| Appraisal | Usually **not required** |
| Income documentation | Usually minimal |
| Credit | Reduced requirements |
| Requirement | Must produce a **net tangible benefit** |
| **Cash out** | **None permitted** |

**Fast and cheap when it applies.** Like the VA IRRRL, it applies to almost nobody in
August 2026 because existing FHA borrowers hold below-market rates.

**Note for Call 4:** Fasani wants $100,000 cash out. Streamline is unavailable for cash
out, and his 30-day mortgage late likely disqualifies him from streamline credit
requirements anyway. Worth knowing so you don't offer something you can't deliver.

## Property requirements

FHA has **minimum property standards** and the appraisal is more demanding than
conventional.

Common issues that must be repaired before closing:
- Peeling paint on pre-1978 homes (lead paint)
- Missing handrails, broken windows
- Roof with insufficient remaining life
- Non-functioning systems (HVAC, plumbing, electrical)
- Safety hazards

**Why you care:** on a purchase, this can kill a deal or force a seller to make repairs
they refuse to make. On a fixer-upper, FHA is often the wrong product — or the borrower
should be looking at an FHA 203(k) renovation loan instead.

**Warn buyers and agents early.** An agent who finds out at appraisal that FHA won't
accept the property will not send you another buyer.

---

## What you must be able to do

- State the FICO thresholds and what each actually gets in practice
- Explain MIP versus PMI and why the difference compounds
- Refuse to say "as low as 500" without the caveats
- Run FHA vs. conventional as a real comparison
- Recall the credit event waiting periods
- Warn about FHA property standards before the appraisal

**Self-check:** Borrower has a 640 FICO, 5% down, and asks which is better — FHA or
conventional?

> *"640 with 5% down — you qualify for both, and this is exactly the range where it's
> genuinely close. FHA has slightly easier underwriting but the mortgage insurance stays
> for the life of the loan. Conventional's mortgage insurance is more expensive at 640
> than it'd be at 720, but it comes off at 20% equity and then it's gone. Let me price
> both. If you're staying in this house more than about five years, conventional usually
> wins on total cost even when the payment starts higher. If you're moving sooner, FHA
> might edge it. How long do you think you'll be there?"*

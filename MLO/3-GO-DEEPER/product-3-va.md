# VA Loans

The best loan product in America for those who qualify — and Call 1's borrower had one
without anyone properly explaining what he had.

---

## Why VA is exceptional

| Feature | VA | Conventional | FHA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Down payment | **$0** | 3–20% | 3.5% |
| Monthly mortgage insurance | **None. Ever.** | PMI under 20% down | MIP, often for life |
| Rates | Typically among the lowest | Baseline | Competitive |
| Credit flexibility | More forgiving | Stricter | Flexible |
| Seller concessions | Up to 4% + closing costs | 3–9% | 6% |
| Assumable | **Yes** | No | Yes |

**No down payment and no monthly mortgage insurance** is a combination nothing else
offers. On a $300,000 loan, avoiding PMI alone is often $150–$250/month.

---

## The assumability point — enormous right now

**VA loans are assumable.** A qualified buyer can take over the existing loan **at its
existing rate**.

In a 6.8% market, a home carrying an assumable 3.1% VA loan is a genuinely extraordinary
asset. The buyer assumes the 3.1%.

**Practical caveats — be honest about these:**
- The buyer must be qualified by the servicer, and it's a slow process
- The buyer must cover the equity gap, often with a second lien
- **If the buyer is not a veteran, the seller's entitlement stays tied up** until the loan
  is paid off — which can prevent the veteran from using VA again

**Why you should say it anyway:** telling a VA borrower their loan is assumable and what
that's worth is the kind of thing nobody else tells them. It's a trust-builder even when
it's not immediately actionable.

## Eligibility

Established by a **Certificate of Eligibility (COE)**, which you can typically pull
electronically in minutes.

Broadly eligible:
- Veterans meeting service requirements
- Active duty (generally 90+ continuous days during wartime)
- National Guard and Reserves (longer service requirements)
- Certain surviving spouses

**Always pull the COE early.** It's fast, free, and it tells you the entitlement position
before you build a file on assumptions.

---

## Entitlement

**Entitlement** is the amount VA guarantees to the lender. It's what allows zero down.

- **Full entitlement:** no loan limit. The borrower can go as high as the lender will
  approve with zero down.
- **Reduced / partial entitlement:** applies when the borrower has an existing VA loan or
  previously defaulted. Loan limits then apply and a down payment may be required.

**Restoration:** entitlement is restored when the VA loan is paid off, or through a
one-time restoration in certain circumstances.

> **Practical rule:** any borrower with an existing VA loan needs their entitlement
> position checked before you quote anything. Don't assume.

## The funding fee

VA charges a one-time **funding fee** instead of monthly mortgage insurance. It can be
financed into the loan.

The fee varies by:
- Loan purpose (purchase, cash-out, IRRRL)
- Down payment amount
- First use vs. subsequent use

**IRRRLs carry a much lower fee than purchases or cash-outs.**

### The exemption — this is the one to know

**Borrowers receiving VA disability compensation are exempt from the funding fee
entirely.**

**This is Michael in Call 1.** He disclosed 10% VA disability, the originator collected
the fact, and did nothing with it.

On a $228,000 loan, a funding fee exemption is thousands of dollars — real, immediate,
material money.

**What should have been said:**

> *"Ten percent disability — that matters more than you'd think. It means you're exempt
> from the VA funding fee entirely. On a loan this size that's thousands of dollars most
> people never find out about."*

That single sentence would have transformed the call.

`[Verify current funding fee percentages and exemption criteria at VA.gov before quoting
specific numbers — they are periodically adjusted.]`

## Residual income — the VA-specific rule

VA uses **residual income** alongside DTI, and it's the rule that most distinguishes VA
underwriting.

**Residual income** = gross monthly income minus taxes, minus all monthly debts, minus
housing expense, minus a maintenance/utilities allowance based on home size.

The remainder must exceed a VA minimum that varies by **family size** and **region**.

**Why this matters commercially:** VA will approve DTIs well above what conventional
allows *if* residual income is strong. A borrower with high DTI but few dependents and
solid income can be VA-approvable when conventional says no.

**Conversely:** a borrower with a large family in a high-cost region can fail residual
income even at a comfortable-looking DTI. Don't assume DTI tells you the answer on VA.

---

## Grossing up tax-free income

**VA disability compensation is not taxable.** Neither is much of certain other income
types.

Underwriting can **gross up** non-taxable income — commonly by 25%, though the permitted
factor varies by lender and program — because the borrower's effective purchasing power is
higher than the nominal amount.

**Michael's $148/month disability counts as roughly $185 for qualifying.**

Small in his case, but the principle matters: on a borrower with $2,000/month in tax-free
disability, grossing up adds $500/month of qualifying income. That routinely makes the
difference between approval and denial.

## The products

### VA purchase
Zero down, no monthly MI, competitive rates. The strongest purchase product available to
anyone who qualifies.

### IRRRL — Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan
The **VA streamline**.

| | |
|---|---|
| Appraisal | Usually not required |
| Income documentation | Usually minimal or none |
| Credit | Minimal requirements |
| Requirement | Must produce a **net tangible benefit** — generally a rate/payment reduction |
| Cash out | **None permitted** |

**Fast and cheap when it applies.** In August 2026 it applies to almost nobody, because
virtually every existing VA borrower has a rate below market. File it away for when rates
fall.

### VA cash-out refinance
Allows cash out, and in some cases can refinance a non-VA loan into a VA loan.

Full documentation, appraisal, higher funding fee. **LTV limits apply** and vary by
lender — verify current maximums with your AE, as both VA policy and lender overlays have
changed over time.

## Serving a veteran borrower well

### The acknowledgment

The transcripts get this right:

> *"Definitely wanted to take a step back and thank you for your service. I know our
> veterans don't get the recognition that they do deserve."*

Michael's tone visibly warms afterward. **It's genuine and it works.** Keep it — just
don't make it a rehearsed line you deliver identically every time. Sincerity is the entire
point.

### What actually earns their trust

Veterans are heavily targeted by mortgage marketing and many have been burned. What
distinguishes you is **knowing the product**:

- Pull the COE early and tell them their entitlement position
- Tell them about the funding fee exemption if they receive disability
- Tell them their loan is assumable and what that's worth in this market
- Explain residual income and why VA may approve them when others didn't
- **Never** suggest refinancing a low-rate VA loan into a market-rate loan without
  showing them exactly what it costs

### The predatory-refinance warning

There is a documented history of aggressive VA refinance solicitation — repeated
refinancing that stripped equity and reset terms with little benefit. VA and Ginnie Mae
have imposed seasoning and net-tangible-benefit requirements specifically to curb it.

**Michael is exactly the target profile:** a VA borrower with a 3.1% rate and $442,000 in
equity, being cold-called about a cash-out.

**Do this right.** Show him the second lien
(`product-6-second-liens-heloc.md`). If a cash-out genuinely serves him, show both and let him
choose with full information. If it doesn't, tell him so.

## The Michael script, done properly

> *"So you're the veteran on the loan — thank you for that, genuinely."*
>
> *"Now, a few things about your situation that I don't think anyone's told you. First,
> you've got a 3.1% VA loan, and that is about as good as it gets. I can't beat it, nobody
> can — market's at about 6.8. So I'm not going to try to refinance you out of it."*
>
> *"Second, you mentioned 10% disability. That makes you exempt from the VA funding fee
> entirely, which on a loan this size is thousands of dollars. And because that disability
> income is tax-free, underwriting grosses it up — it counts for more than the number looks
> like."*
>
> *"Third — and most people have no idea about this — your VA loan is assumable. If you
> ever sell, a qualified buyer can take over that 3.1%. In this market that makes your
> house significantly more attractive than the one next door."*
>
> *"So here's what I want to do about the $120,000. Instead of touching that 3.1%, we
> leave your first mortgage completely alone and put a second behind it. Your rate stays
> where it is, forever. Let me price both ways and show you the difference — and tell me
> what the money's actually for, because that changes which one I'd recommend."*

That is a completely different call from the one that happened.

## What you must be able to do

- Explain why VA is the strongest product for eligible borrowers
- Pull a COE and interpret entitlement
- Identify a funding fee exemption from disability status
- Explain residual income and why it can approve a high-DTI file
- Gross up tax-free income
- Explain assumability and its value in a high-rate market
- **Never** refinance a low-rate VA loan without showing the alternative

**Self-check:** A veteran has a 2.9% VA loan, receives 30% disability, and wants $75,000
for medical bills. What do you do?

> *Do not touch the 2.9%. Price a fixed second lien and be explicit that the first
> mortgage stays untouched. Tell him about the funding fee exemption — though on a second
> lien it may not apply, so verify — and that his disability income grosses up for
> qualifying. Ask, gently, whether the medical bills can be negotiated or put on a payment
> plan first, because the cheapest loan is sometimes no loan. If a second is still the
> answer, structure the smallest one that solves the problem, not the largest he qualifies
> for.*

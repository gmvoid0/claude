# AUS, Conditions, and How a File Actually Moves

What happens after you hang up. Understanding this is what lets you set honest
expectations — and honest expectations are what stop deals from dying at week three.

---

## AUS — Automated Underwriting Systems

Every conventional file runs through one:

- **DU** — Desktop Underwriter (Fannie Mae)
- **LPA** — Loan Product Advisor (Freddie Mac)

FHA and VA files run through their own overlays on these engines.

### The findings

| Result | Meaning | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Approve / Eligible** | Green light, subject to conditions | Proceed |
| **Accept** (LPA) | Same | Proceed |
| **Approve / Ineligible** | Credit risk fine, but a rule is violated — loan limit, occupancy, product | Fix the structural issue and rerun |
| **Refer / Eligible** | Needs a human underwriter | Manual underwriting |
| **Refer with Caution** | Serious risk concerns | Usually restructure or decline |

### The broker advantage — run both

**DU and LPA weight risk differently.** One will frequently approve what the other refers.

A retail LO usually has access to one. **You have both.** When DU refers, run LPA before
you do anything else. This costs nothing and saves real deals.

**And say it to the borrower:**

> *"Quick thing worth knowing — there are two automated underwriting systems, Fannie's and
> Freddie's, and they don't score risk identically. A lot of lenders only run one. I run
> both. It's a small thing that occasionally makes the whole difference."*

### What AUS gives you

1. **An approval decision**
2. **A conditions list** — exactly what documentation is required
3. **Sometimes, relief** — reduced documentation, or an **appraisal waiver**

That conditions list is your roadmap. Read it the day you get it and start clearing.

## Conditions — where deals actually stall

**Most delays are condition delays**, and most condition delays are avoidable.

### The types

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Prior to Approval (PTA)** | Must clear before the file is approved |
| **Prior to Docs (PTD)** | Must clear before closing documents are drawn |
| **Prior to Funding (PTF)** | Must clear before money moves |
| **At Closing** | Handled at the signing table |

### The predictable ones

You should anticipate every one of these **before** submission:

- Letter of explanation for any credit inquiry in the last 90 days
- Letter of explanation and documentation for **any** derogatory
- Source of **any** large deposit
- All pages of every bank statement — including blanks
- Gift letter plus donor's ability plus transfer trail
- Verbal verification of employment before closing
- Homeowners insurance binder
- Payoff statements on debts being paid at closing
- Explanation of any employment gap
- Updated paystub if the file ages past 30 days

**Getting ahead of these is the single biggest thing you can do to make files close
faster.** Every condition you pre-clear is two days you don't lose.

### The pre-submission checklist

Before you submit, ask:

1. What's the worst item on this credit report, and do I have the LOE and documentation?
2. Is there any deposit an underwriter would question, and is it sourced?
3. Is every bank statement complete, all pages?
4. Are there any inquiries in the last 90 days needing explanation?
5. Is any income unusual, and have I documented why it continues?
6. Is there anything about this property that needs flagging?

**Answering these before submission is what separates LOs whose files close in 21 days
from those whose files take 45.**

## The timeline

| Stage | Typical | What drives it |
|---|---|---|
| Application → disclosures out | 1–3 days | You |
| Disclosures signed → submission | 2–5 days | Borrower responsiveness |
| Submission → initial underwriting | 1–5 days | Lender turn times |
| Conditions cleared | 3–14 days | **Mostly the borrower, partly you** |
| Appraisal ordered → received | 5–14 days | Appraiser availability, market |
| Clear to close → docs | 1–3 days | Lender |
| Docs → closing | 3 days minimum | **CD 3-day rule, non-negotiable** |
| Closing → funding (refi, primary) | 3 days | **Rescission, non-negotiable** |

**Realistic total: 21–45 days.**

### Setting expectations honestly

> *"Realistically we're looking at three to four weeks, and I'll tell you exactly what
> drives that. The two things that slow files down most are how fast you get me documents
> and how fast the appraiser gets out. Both of those are largely in our control. The rest
> is a federal three-day waiting period before closing that nobody can shorten. If I tell
> you two weeks and it takes five, you'll be furious and you'd be right — so I'd rather
> tell you four and beat it."*

**The transcripts claim "10 to 14 days."** That is achievable on a clean file with a fast
wholesale lender, and it is not the median. **Do not promise it.** Quote a realistic range
and outperform it.

## Turn times — and why lender selection matters

Turn times vary substantially between lenders and shift with volume. A lender advertising
48-hour underwriting can be at 10 days during a refinance boom.

**Ask your AE for current turn times before you promise a closing date.** Not their
marketing number — their actual current number.

**Turn time is a legitimate selling point when it's true:**

> *"One of the reasons I'd use this lender for you is turn times. Their underwriting is
> currently running about three days. Some of the big servicers are at three to four
> weeks right now. On a purchase with a contract date, that's not a small difference."*

## Keeping the deal alive

### The borrower-behavior rules

Give these at application, verbatim, and follow up in writing:

> *"Four rules between now and closing. One — don't open any new credit. No car, no
> furniture, no store card, not even a zero-interest offer. Two — don't close existing
> accounts either. Three — don't change jobs without calling me first. Four — don't move
> large amounts of money between accounts, and if you do, keep every statement."*
>
> *"Any of those can blow this up at week three. I'd rather annoy you now than lose your
> house later."*

**Credit is re-pulled before closing.** A new car loan in week three has killed a very
large number of deals.

### Communication cadence

Silence kills deals. The borrower who hasn't heard from you in a week starts wondering
whether the other lender would have been better.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Application day | Confirm next steps in writing |
| Every 2–3 days | A status update, even "nothing new, appraisal's Thursday" |
| Every milestone | Submitted, appraisal in, approved, clear to close |
| Any problem | **Immediately** — never let them find out from someone else |

**"Nothing new" is a valid update.** It tells them you're paying attention.

## When something goes wrong

It will. What matters is how you handle it.

### The rules

1. **Call immediately.** Never let a borrower discover a problem on their own.
2. **Lead with the solution**, not the problem.
3. **Never blame the underwriter** to the borrower. You're one team.
4. **Give a specific next step and a specific timeline.**

> *"I've got news and a plan. The appraisal came in at $340,000, not the $360,000 we
> hoped. Here's what that means and here are three options — and option two is the one I'd
> pick. Do you have five minutes?"*

### Common problems and responses

| Problem | Response |
|---|---|
| Appraisal low | ROV with comps, larger down payment, reduce loan, renegotiate |
| Income insufficient | Add a co-borrower, pay off debt, reduce loan amount, different product |
| Credit dropped | Rapid rescore, find the cause, restructure |
| Large deposit unsourced | Get documentation; if impossible, exclude the funds |
| Condition can't be met | Escalate through the AE; consider a different lender |
| Rate lock expiring | Extend (costs money), or reprice |

**Almost every problem has an option.** The LO who calls with three options is the LO who
keeps the deal — and gets the referral even when the deal dies.

## The handoff to processing

Your file quality determines everything downstream.

**A good handoff includes:**
- Complete 1003 (the application), no blanks
- All initial documents collected
- Notes on **anything unusual**, with your explanation
- The LOEs you've already drafted
- Your read on which conditions will be hard

**Your processor is your most operationally important relationship**
(`industry-4-cast-of-characters.md`). Give them complete files, tell them
immediately when something changes, and never promise a borrower a timeline without
checking.

## What you must be able to do

- Interpret every AUS finding
- Run LPA when DU refers, and explain why
- Anticipate the ten predictable conditions before submitting
- Quote a realistic timeline and explain what drives it
- Deliver the borrower-behavior rules
- Deliver bad news with options attached

**Self-check:** DU returns Refer/Eligible on a borrower with 47% DTI, 680 FICO, 9 months
reserves, 8 years at the same job. What are your next three moves?

> *One — run LPA. It weights reserves and job tenure differently and may well approve.
> Two — if LPA also refers, call your AE and ask which of their programs or which lender
> is most flexible at this profile; manual underwriting is a real option with these
> compensating factors. Three — check whether paying off one small debt drops DTI under
> 45%, because sometimes $150/month of payment is the entire problem. Do all three before
> you tell the borrower anything — you don't want to deliver a "no" you can reverse an
> hour later.*

# Non-QM

The products for borrowers who are creditworthy but don't fit the conventional box. As a
broker this is a major competitive advantage — most bank LOs cannot offer any of it.

**Call 4's borrower needed this and it was never mentioned.**

---

## What "non-QM" means

A **Qualified Mortgage (QM)** meets the CFPB's Ability-to-Repay safe harbor standards.
**Non-QM** loans don't — they use alternative documentation or fall outside QM's
parameters.

**Non-QM is not subprime.** Post-2008 non-QM still requires a full ability-to-repay
determination — it just gets there with different documentation. Most non-QM borrowers
have strong credit and substantial assets; they simply can't document income the way a W-2
employee can.

**Who they are:**
- Self-employed business owners who write off aggressively
- Real estate investors
- Gig and 1099 workers
- Retirees with assets but little "income"
- Foreign nationals
- Borrowers with a recent credit event
- Anyone needing a loan above conventional limits with flexible documentation

**The tradeoff:** rates run meaningfully above conventional, down payments are larger, and
reserves requirements are higher. In exchange, the loan closes.

## Bank statement loans

**The most important non-QM product for you**, and the one Call 4 needed.

### How it works

Qualifying income is calculated from **deposits into bank accounts** — typically 12 or 24
months — instead of tax returns.

| | |
|---|---|
| Documentation | 12 or 24 months of bank statements |
| Tax returns | **Not required** |
| Account type | Business or personal (business is more common) |
| Expense factor | Lender applies an expense ratio (commonly ~50%) or accepts a CPA-prepared P&L |
| Typical FICO | 620+ (varies; some lenders lower) |
| Typical down / equity | 10–20%+ |
| Self-employment | Usually 2+ years required |

### The calculation, roughly

```
24 months of business deposits        $480,000
÷ 24                                  $20,000/month gross deposits
× (1 − 50% expense factor)            $10,000/month qualifying income
```

Some lenders will use a CPA letter or P&L to justify a lower expense factor, which
substantially increases qualifying income. **Ask your AE** — this is exactly the kind of
thing an AE relationship is for.

### Why it exists

A business owner's tax return is optimized to **minimize** taxable income. That's good tax
strategy and terrible mortgage qualification. Bank statement loans look at cash flow
instead of taxable income.

### The script

> *"Here's the thing about self-employed files, and I'd rather tell you now than in three
> weeks. Underwriting doesn't look at what your business brings in — it looks at what you
> show after write-offs. Most good business owners write off aggressively, which makes
> you look poor on paper."*
>
> *"So there are two roads. If your returns show enough, conventional is cheapest and we
> go that way. If they don't — or if they're not filed yet — there's a bank-statement
> program that ignores tax returns entirely and qualifies you off deposits into your
> business account. Rate's higher, but it closes."*
>
> **"Forget the tax returns for a second. In a normal month, how much comes into your
> business account? Just gross deposits — not profit, not after write-offs."**

**That last question is the qualifying question.** It routes the entire deal, and in Call
4 it was never asked.

## DSCR loans

For real estate investors. **The property qualifies, not the borrower.**

### How it works

**DSCR = Debt Service Coverage Ratio**

```
DSCR = Monthly rental income ÷ Monthly PITIA
```

(PITIA = principal, interest, taxes, insurance, association dues)

| DSCR | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1.25 | Rent covers the payment with 25% cushion — strong |
| 1.00 | Rent exactly covers the payment — typically the minimum |
| 0.80 | Rent falls short — some lenders allow with compensating factors |

| | |
|---|---|
| Personal income | **Not verified.** No paystubs, no tax returns, no DTI. |
| Employment | Not verified |
| Property | Investment only — never primary residence |
| Typical FICO | 620–680+ |
| Typical down | 20–25% |

**Why it's powerful:** an investor with ten properties has a DTI that makes conventional
qualification impossible. DSCR ignores that entirely and looks at whether *this* property
cash flows.

**Why you care commercially:** investors buy repeatedly. One good DSCR client can be five
loans over three years, plus referrals into an investor network. This is one of the
highest-lifetime-value client types in the business.

## The rest of the non-QM menu

### P&L only
Qualifying income from a **CPA-prepared profit and loss statement**, sometimes with a few
months of bank statements as support. Faster than bank statement loans, requires a
cooperative CPA.

### 1099 only
For contractors paid on 1099. Income calculated from 1099 forms with an expense factor
applied. Simpler than bank statements when the 1099s tell the story.

### Asset depletion / asset utilization
Converts liquid assets into "income" for qualifying:

```
Qualifying assets ÷ a set number of months = monthly qualifying income
```

**Best for:** retirees, borrowers who sold a business, high-net-worth individuals with
little W-2 income. The assets generally stay invested — they're not spent.

### ITIN
For borrowers with an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number instead of an SSN.
Larger down payments, limited lender pool, an underserved market in many areas.

### Foreign national
Non-resident borrowers, typically for investment property. No US credit required.
Substantial down payments (often 30%+).

### Recent credit event
Programs for borrowers with a bankruptcy, foreclosure, or short sale more recent than
conventional seasoning allows — sometimes as little as one day out of a credit event,
priced accordingly.

**Tyler in Call 3** — 581 FICO with a $13,000 forbearance — is likely in this territory,
which is part of why "I know for a fact I can beat 7.2" was such a dangerous thing to say.

## Positioning non-QM honestly

Non-QM costs more. Say so plainly, then justify it.

> *"I want to be upfront: this is going to price higher than a conventional loan — call it
> a point to a point and a half above. That's not me marking it up, that's how these
> programs work. What you're buying is a loan that actually closes without tax returns."*
>
> *"And it doesn't have to be permanent. Get two years of clean returns showing the income
> and we refinance into conventional. A lot of my self-employed clients do exactly that —
> use non-QM to get the deal done now, then refinance into conventional later."*

**Never present non-QM as equivalent to conventional.** It isn't, and the borrower will
find out at disclosure. Present it as **the loan that gets done**, which is what it is.

## The qualifying questions

For any self-employed or non-traditional borrower, ask these in discovery:

1. **How long have you owned the business?** *(2 years is the usual threshold)*
2. **What's the entity structure?** *(Sole prop, LLC, S-corp, partnership — changes the
   income calculation completely)*
3. **Are your tax returns filed and current?**
4. **In a normal month, how much is deposited into the business account?**
5. **Do you have a business account separate from personal?** *(If commingled, it gets
   harder)*
6. **Do you have a CPA who could prepare a P&L?**
7. **How much do you write off?** *(Tells you whether conventional is even viable)*

**Every one of these was available in Call 4. None was asked.**

---

## What you must be able to do

- Explain what non-QM is without implying it's subprime
- Explain a bank statement loan and calculate rough qualifying income
- Ask about deposits rather than income when the borrower is self-employed
- Explain DSCR and why investors need it
- Match asset depletion to a retiree profile
- Position the higher rate honestly, with the refinance-later path

**Self-check:** A borrower owns a landscaping business, 8 years, S-corp, returns filed but
show $32,000 net after write-offs. Wants a $400,000 purchase. Business deposits are about
$45,000/month. What do you do?

> *Conventional is dead on arrival — $32,000 of documented income won't support a $400k
> loan. Go bank statement. At $45,000/month in deposits with a 50% expense factor, that's
> roughly $22,500/month in qualifying income, which supports the purchase comfortably. Ask
> whether his CPA will prepare a P&L, because a justified lower expense factor makes it
> even stronger. Set expectations on rate — a point-plus above conventional — and tell him
> we can refinance to conventional later if he ever changes how he takes income. Also
> confirm the business account is separate from personal.*

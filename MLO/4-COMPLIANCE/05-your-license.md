# Your License and Your Career

**As of August 2026.** Your license is your career. Everything in this system is worth
nothing without it.

---

## The SAFE Act and NMLS

The **SAFE Act** (Secure and Fair Enforcement for Mortgage Licensing Act) requires every
mortgage loan originator to be either **state-licensed** or **federally registered**
through the **NMLS** — the Nationwide Multistate Licensing System — before taking a
residential mortgage application or offering terms.

### The two tracks

| | **State-licensed** | **Federally registered** |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Brokers, non-bank lenders — **you** | Bank / credit union employees |
| Pre-licensing education | **20 hours** minimum | None |
| SAFE test | **Required** | Not required |
| Background check | Criminal + **credit** | Criminal |
| Continuing education | **8 hours/year** | None federally |
| Portability | Per state, must license in each | Tied to the institution |

You are on the **state-licensed** track. It is the harder track, and it is also the one
that makes you portable and independent — your license belongs to you, not to your
employer.

### What licensure required

1. **20 hours** of NMLS-approved pre-licensing education (3 federal law, 3 ethics
   including fair lending and consumer protection, 2 non-traditional mortgage lending,
   12 electives) — plus any **state-specific** hours
2. **SAFE MLO test** — 120 questions, 75% to pass
3. **Criminal background check** — fingerprints via NMLS
4. **Credit report** — reviewed for financial responsibility
5. **Sponsorship** by a licensed company
6. State application and fees, per state

### Automatic disqualifiers

- Any **felony in the past 7 years**
- **Ever**, at any time: a felony involving fraud, dishonesty, breach of trust, or money
  laundering
- A previously **revoked** MLO license, in any state — permanent bar

## Multi-state — your specific situation

You're licensed in more than one state. That's a real advantage and a real burden.

**The advantage:** your addressable market multiplies, and you can follow borrowers who
relocate. In a purchase-constrained market, geography diversification smooths your
pipeline.

**The burden:**

- **Separate license, separate fee, separate renewal** in every state
- Some states require **additional state-specific education** on top of the 20 hours
- Some require **state-specific CE** annually
- **Renewal runs November 1 – December 31.** Miss it and your license goes inactive in
  that state on January 1. Reinstatement is expensive and sometimes impossible.
- **Every state's advertising and solicitation rules differ**, and several require your
  NMLS ID and licensed company name in specific formats
- The **mini-TCPA patchwork** (`01-cold-calling-law.md`) follows the *consumer's*
  location, not yours

> **Action item:** list your states. Then build a single-page matrix — license number,
> renewal date, CE requirement, calling hours, mini-TCPA status, advertising rules. Give
> that list to your coach and this system will finish the compliance matrix for you.
> Until then it stays generic, and generic compliance guidance is worth much less to you
> than specific guidance.

## Continuing education

**8 hours annually**, federally: 3 federal law, 2 ethics, 2 non-traditional mortgage
lending, 1 elective — plus state-specific hours where required.

**The successive years rule:** you generally cannot take the same course two years in a
row. People get caught by this every December.

Do your CE in **September**. Not December. Every year the NMLS system slows to a crawl in
late December and people miss renewal because a course completion didn't post in time.

## What actually gets licenses revoked

Not the things new LOs worry about. The real list, roughly in order of frequency:

1. **Misrepresentation to a borrower** — the Never-Say List, verbatim
2. **Falsifying documents** — altering a paystub, a bank statement, a VOE. Instant and
   permanent. It also happens to be bank fraud.
3. **Unlicensed activity** — taking an application for a state you aren't licensed in.
   Easy to do accidentally with a mobile borrower. **Always confirm the property state
   and your license status before discussing terms.**
4. **Failure to disclose** on your own NMLS record — criminal charges, judgments, tax
   liens, bankruptcies. The failure to disclose is punished harder than the underlying
   event.
5. **Advertising violations** — missing NMLS ID, unsubstantiated rate claims, unapproved
   marketing. Your social media posts are advertising.
6. **Fee and comp violations** — being paid on both sides, comp varying with loan terms
7. **Letting your license lapse** and continuing to originate

Notice that **five of the seven are honesty problems, not competence problems.** That's
the actual job risk in this career.

## Your NMLS record is public

Anyone can look you up on **NMLS Consumer Access**. Borrowers do it. Realtors do it.
Recruiters do it.

Your record shows your employment history, license status, states, and **any
disciplinary action, permanently**.

**Use it as a sales asset:**

> ✅ *"Before we go further — look me up. NMLS Consumer Access, my ID is [number]. You'll
> see my license, my states, my history. You should do that with anyone who asks you for
> a Social Security number over the phone."*

That line does three things at once: it satisfies your NMLS disclosure obligation, it
demonstrates you have nothing to hide, and it plants a comparison standard the other
guy probably can't match. Almost no one on a cold call says it.

## Career architecture

Worth thinking about in your first ninety days, because the choices compound.

### The paths

| Path | What it is | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| **Producing LO** | Originate, build a book | High — top producers earn $500k–$1M+ |
| **Team lead** | LOAs and junior LOs under you, you keep a portion | Higher, more management |
| **Branch manager** | Run a branch, override on production | Higher, less selling |
| **Broker owner** | Your own shop | Highest, real business risk |
| **Wholesale AE** | Sell lender products to brokers | Steadier, lower ceiling |

Your channel — wholesale broker — has the shortest path to ownership in the industry.
Starting a mortgage brokerage requires far less capital than becoming a lender.

### The two assets you're actually building

1. **Your database.** Every person who ever gave you their number. This is the compounding
   asset — past clients refinance, buy again, and refer. LOs with 500 funded clients get
   calls without dialing.
2. **Your reputation.** Realtors and past clients talk. In a local market this is
   everything.

**Neither belongs to your employer** — but the CRM they live in might. Read your
employment agreement. Know what you can take with you and what you can't, *before* you
build something worth taking.

## What you must be able to do

- Explain the difference between state-licensed and federally registered
- State your CE requirement and when to do it
- Name the top causes of license revocation
- Confirm the property state and your licensure before discussing terms
- Use NMLS Consumer Access as a trust-building tool

**Self-check:** A borrower calls about a property in a state where you aren't licensed.
What do you do?

> *You do not discuss terms, take an application, or quote. You say: "I'm not licensed in
> [state], so I legally can't advise you on that one — but let me connect you with
> someone at my shop who is." Then refer it internally. Taking that application is
> unlicensed activity and it can cost you every license you hold.*

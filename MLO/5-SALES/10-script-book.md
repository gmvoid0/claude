# The Opening Script Book

Complete, compliant scripts. Use them as a structure to internalize, not lines to
recite — a memorized script delivered word-for-word sounds memorized.

**Every script here is written for the August 2026 market and post-trigger-lead-ban
compliance.** Replace `[bracketed]` items with your real information.

---

## The P60 — a perfect 60-second opening

Amir Syed's concept: a 60-second opening so well built it becomes automatic. Here is the
structure, rebuilt for 2026.

```
0:00–0:15  OPEN     Name confirm → who you are → true reason → easy question
0:15–0:30  FRAME    What this call is, what it isn't, how long
0:30–0:45  HOOK     One specific, true, useful thing they didn't know
0:45–1:00  PIVOT    First discovery question
```

### The full P60 — aged internet lead

> **[0:00]** *"Hello, Michael?"*
> **[pause for "yes"]**
>
> *"Hi Michael, my name is [Name], I'm with [Company], NMLS [ID] — I'm on a recorded
> line. You filled out a request on [site] back on [date] about pulling some cash out of
> the house. I know that was a while ago. Did you ever get that sorted out?"*
>
> **[0:15 — they answer. Listen.]**
>
> *"Got it. So here's what I'd like to do, and then you can tell me if it's worth your
> time. I'm a mortgage broker — I work with about fifty different lenders and they
> compete for your file rather than me having one rate sheet to sell you. I'd like to ask
> you maybe six questions so I actually understand what you're trying to accomplish. Then
> I'll either show you numbers or I'll tell you to stay exactly where you are. Either way
> you'll know in about ten minutes. Fair?"*
>
> **[0:30]**
>
> *"And one thing up front, because most people don't know this and it changes the whole
> conversation. If your current rate starts with a three or a four, I can't beat it —
> nobody can, the market's at about six-eight. So this isn't a 'lower your rate' call.
> This is about whether there's a way to get you what you need without touching that
> rate at all. There usually is."*
>
> **[0:45]**
>
> *"So tell me — what were you trying to do with the cash back when you filled that out?"*

### Why this works

| Element | Function |
|---|---|
| NMLS + recorded line | Legitimacy, and satisfies disclosure obligations |
| Specific site + date | Provably true; survives "I never filled anything out" |
| "Did you ever get that sorted out?" | Warm, easy, informative either way |
| The Frame | Bounds the call, sets the agenda, defuses "you're selling me" |
| "I'll tell you to stay where you are" | Preemptive strike — instant trust |
| The rate honesty | **Diagnoses their wrong belief in the first minute** |
| "without touching that rate at all" | Plants the second-lien solution before they object |
| Open-ended pivot | Gets them talking about purpose, not facts |

**That rate-honesty beat is the most important addition to any 2026 mortgage script.** It
disqualifies you from the "another guy promising to lower my rate" category immediately,
and it makes everything you say afterward more credible.

## Script 1 — Aged internet lead (primary)

Your main source. Full P60 above. Variants for what comes back:

**If they say "I already did it":**
> *"Good — congratulations. Who'd you end up going with?"* **[listen]** *"And how'd that
> go?"* **[listen]** *"Glad it worked out. Do me one favor — put my number in your phone.
> Rates are projected to come down toward the low sixes by the end of the year, and when
> they do, whoever did your loan is not going to call you. I will. Sound fair?"*

*You just converted a dead lead into a database entry and a future refinance.*

**If they say "I never filled anything out":**
> *"You may well be right, and I apologize — these come through aggregators and they're
> not always accurate. Let me be straight about why I'm calling instead, and if it's not
> useful I'll let you go. Fair?"*

**Believe them immediately.** Never argue the point (Call 2's fatal error).

**If they say "that was months ago, I'm not looking anymore":**
> *"Totally understand. Can I ask what changed? Sometimes it's 'we handled it' and
> sometimes it's 'we got told no,' and those are very different conversations."*

*That question finds the deals other callers walk away from.*

## Script 2 — Fresh internet lead (under 60 minutes)

Speed is the whole advantage. Name it.

> *"Michael? Hi, this is [Name] with [Company], NMLS [ID]. You just submitted a request
> about [purpose] — I'm the one who picked it up. I wanted to get to you before your
> phone starts ringing off the hook, because it's about to. What are you trying to get
> done?"*

Then straight into discovery. No frame needed — they're expecting the call.

**Text immediately if no answer:**
> *"Hi Michael — [Name] with [Company], NMLS [ID]. Just tried you about the request you
> submitted a few minutes ago. I'm at this number whenever you're free."*

## Script 3 — "I'm already working with someone"

The single most common real objection. Expect it. This is the transcripts' best move,
rebuilt honestly.

> *"Good — you should be working with someone. I'm not calling to get you to leave them.
> One question and then I'll know whether this is even worth your time: have you locked
> your rate yet?"*

**If NOT locked:**
> *"Then nothing's lost by having a second number to compare. Here's my honest position:
> I work with about fifty lenders, so sometimes I win these and sometimes I don't. If
> their deal is better, I'll tell you to stay with them and I'll mean it. If mine's
> better, you'd want to know. Give me four questions?"*

**If LOCKED:**
> *"Then stay put — breaking a lock this late usually costs more than it saves. When are
> you closing?"* **[listen]** *"Perfect. Put my number in your phone. Two reasons: if
> anything goes sideways in the next three weeks, call me — I've rescued files that fell
> apart at the last minute. And when rates come down, I'll call you about the refinance.
> Whoever's doing this one probably won't."*

**Never break a locked loan late in the process.** It rarely works, it can genuinely
harm the borrower, and it's how you get a reputation you can't fix. The long game here is
worth far more than the deal.

## Script 4 — The legacy-rate borrower (2.5%–4%)

**The most common situation you will encounter, and the one most LOs handle worst.**

> *"Michael, before we go any further — what's the rate on your current mortgage?"*
> **[listen: "3.1"]**
>
> *"Okay, stop me right there. Three-one is a phenomenal rate. I can't beat it, nobody
> can, the market's at about six-eight. So I'm not going to sit here and pretend I'm
> going to lower your rate — that would be nonsense and you'd know it."*
>
> *"So the real question is different. You said you need $120,000. There are two ways to
> get it. One, we refinance the whole thing — you get the cash, but you give up the
> three-one on your entire balance and everything reprices at today's market. Two, we
> leave your first mortgage completely alone and put a second loan behind it just for the
> $120k. Your three-one stays exactly where it is, forever."*
>
> *"Most of the time option two is dramatically better, but not always — it depends on
> what the money's for and how fast you'll pay it back. Can I run both and show you the
> actual numbers?"*

**This script is the difference between an amateur and an advisor**, and it is precisely
what Call 1 fails to do. See `../3-GO-DEEPER/product-6-second-liens-heloc.md`.

## Script 5 — Debt consolidation

The strongest play in this market. Most LOs sell it wrong by leading with rate.

> *"Let me ask you something different. Forget the mortgage for a second — what are you
> paying every month on credit cards, car loans, personal loans, all of it?"*
> **[listen — write everything down]**
>
> *"Okay. So between the cards and the car you're sending out about $1,400 a month, and
> most of that is interest at twenty-plus percent — it's not even touching the balance."*
>
> *"Here's the thing nobody explains. Your mortgage rate is going to go up in this
> scenario, and I'm not going to hide that. But that's the wrong number to look at. The
> right number is your **blended rate** — what you're paying across everything you owe.
> Right now that's around [X]%. If we consolidate, it drops to about [Y]%, and your total
> monthly outflow goes from $1,400 to about $[Z]."*
>
> *"One condition, and I'm serious about it: this only works if you actually close those
> cards. If you run them back up you'll be worse off than you are now, and I'd rather you
> not do this at all. Can you close them?"*

**The condition is not a sales technique.** It's true, it protects them, and it is the
single most trust-generating sentence in the script.

## Script 6 — Purchase / pre-approval

> *"Michael? Hi, this is [Name] with [Company], NMLS [ID]. You put in a request about
> getting pre-approved. Are you actively looking at houses right now, or still in the
> figuring-it-out stage?"*

**If actively looking:**
> *"Then let's get you real numbers today, because a pre-approval letter is what lets you
> actually make an offer. Are you working with an agent yet?"*

**If figuring it out:**
> *"Perfect, that's the right time to call. Nothing to commit to — let's find out what
> you'd qualify for so you know what range you're shopping in. Nothing's worse than
> falling in love with a house that was never in reach. Fifteen minutes?"*

**The agent question matters enormously.** If they don't have one, you can refer them —
which is the beginning of a referral relationship in the direction that pays
(`17-referral-partners.md`). Just make sure it's a genuine referral, not a
paid arrangement (`../4-COMPLIANCE/04-respa-ecoa-udaap.md`).

## Script 7 — Self-employed

Call 4's borrower. The product he needed was never mentioned.

> *"You said you own the business — how long?"* **[listen]**
>
> *"Twenty-two years, good. Here's the thing about self-employed files, and I want to be
> upfront so we don't waste three weeks. Underwriting doesn't look at what your business
> brings in — it looks at what you show after write-offs. Most good business owners write
> off aggressively, which makes them look poor on paper."*
>
> *"So there are two roads. If your returns show enough income, conventional is cheapest
> and we go that way. If they don't — or if they're not filed yet — there's a
> bank-statement program that ignores tax returns completely and qualifies you off your
> deposits. Rate's a bit higher, but it closes."*
>
> *"Which do you think fits? And when are your returns actually getting filed?"*

**That last question is the gate on the entire deal**, and in Call 4 it was never asked.

## Script 8 — Past client / database

Your warmest and highest-converting call.

> *"Michael, it's [Name] — I did your loan back in [year]. How's the house treating you?"*
> **[genuine conversation]**
>
> *"So the reason I'm calling — I go through my past clients every quarter and check
> whether anything's changed that I should be telling them about. Couple quick things:
> any big expenses coming up? Kids, school, a renovation? Anything on credit cards
> building up? And are you planning to be in that house another five years, or is
> something on the horizon?"*

**No pitch.** The service *is* the pitch. This call generates refinances, purchases,
and referrals with a fraction of the effort of any cold call, which is why the database
is the whole business (`07-follow-up.md`).

## Script 9 — Rate-drop trigger call

For everyone who said "call me when rates drop." Easiest call in the business.

> *"Michael, it's [Name] with [Company]. Back in [month] you told me you'd move if you
> saw a [X]. It's there today. Do you want me to lock it?"*

That's it. Don't add anything.

## Script 10 — Hostile

> *"Look, I can hear this is a bad time and I'm not going to fight you on it. Before I go
> — is it that you're not interested in mortgages at all, or just not right now?"*

**If "not at all" or any removal language:** stop. Log it. Move on.

**If "not right now":** *"Understood. Can I check back in ninety days? If you say no,
that's a no and I won't call again."*

Respecting the no is what earns the callback. And a documented, respected boundary is
also your compliance record.

## Script 11 — Voicemail

Under 20 seconds. Curiosity, no pitch, no rate.

**First attempt:**
> *"Michael, [Name] with [Company], NMLS [ID] — you'd filled out a request about the
> house back in [month]. Just wanted to see if you ever got it handled. [Number]. I'll
> try you again tomorrow afternoon."*

**After a conversation:**
> *"Michael, [Name] — got your numbers put together and there's one thing in here I want
> to make sure you see before you decide anything. [Number]. I'll try you again around
> four."*

**Never leave a rate on a voicemail.** You've given away your only reason to talk.

## Building your own

1. **Take the structure**, not the words.
2. **Rewrite it in your own voice.** If a line feels awkward to say, it will sound
   awkward. Change it.
3. **Say it aloud fifty times.**
4. **Run it 100 calls before judging it.**
5. **Change one thing at a time.**
6. **Record and listen weekly.**

The goal is not to sound like this document. The goal is to have the structure so
internalized that your own words come out under pressure.

## The pre-flight check

Before every block:

- [ ] Opening says name, company, NMLS, **true** reason
- [ ] No claim about lead source that isn't literally true
- [ ] No rate promised anywhere in the script
- [ ] Recording disclosure if applicable
- [ ] I know what I'm asking for at the end of the call
- [ ] I have the rate-honesty line ready for legacy borrowers

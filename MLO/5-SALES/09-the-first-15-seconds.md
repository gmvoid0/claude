# The First 15 Seconds

The highest-leverage fifteen seconds in your career. Everything downstream is decided
here.

---

## What is actually happening

When a stranger answers your call, they are running one unconscious question:

> **"Is this worth my attention, or is this junk?"**

They resolve it in **three to five seconds**, mostly on tone, before your words register.
Then they spend the next ten seconds looking for confirmation.

You are not selling in the first fifteen seconds. **You are earning the next thirty.**
That's the entire objective. New LOs try to accomplish far too much here and trigger the
hang-up they were trying to avoid.

---

## The four hang-up triggers

Avoid these and you'll beat most callers on structure alone.

### 1. The telemarketer cadence
Everyone recognizes it: too fast, too bright, rehearsed rhythm, no pauses. It's the
single fastest way to be categorized as junk.

**Fix:** slow down 20%. Pause after your name. Sound like a person who called a person.

### 2. Asking permission weakly
> ❌ *"Hi, is this a bad time?"*
> ❌ *"Do you have a few minutes to talk?"*

Both hand them a scripted exit. "Is this a bad time" is answered "yes" reflexively.

**Fix:** either take the thirty seconds and earn them, or give a *bounded* ask:
*"Give me thirty seconds and then you can tell me to get lost."*

### 3. Leading with a pitch
> ❌ *"I'm calling because we're offering the lowest rates in the market and I wanted to
> see if you'd like to save money on your mortgage."*

They've heard it a hundred times. It's a category, not a message.

**Fix:** lead with a *specific, true, personal* reason.

### 4. Sounding evasive about why you're calling
Consumers detect evasion instantly. "Following up on your inquiry" — when they didn't
inquire — reads as a lie even when they can't articulate why, and it's the exact moment
Paula in Call 2 shut the call down.

**Fix:** the truth, delivered confidently. It's a pattern interrupt because nobody uses
it.

## The anatomy

Four beats. Roughly fifteen seconds.

```
BEAT 1  (2 sec)   Name confirmation      "Michael?"
BEAT 2  (4 sec)   Who you are            Name, company, NMLS
BEAT 3  (6 sec)   Why you're calling     Specific. True. Personal.
BEAT 4  (3 sec)   Easy question          Something they can answer in a word
```

### Beat 1 — Name confirmation

> *"Hello, Michael?"*

Do this. It works.

- Confirms you have the right person
- Personal, not broadcast
- Requires a response, which starts the conversation
- Slight pattern interrupt — telemarketers usually launch into a script

The transcripts do this on all four calls and it's one of the things they get right.

**Then pause.** Let them say "yes." Do not run through it.

### Beat 2 — Who you are

> *"Hi Michael, my name is [Name], I'm with [Company], NMLS [ID]."*

- **Slow down on your name.** It's the first credibility signal.
- **NMLS ID belongs here.** Almost nobody says it, so it lands as legitimacy — and it
  satisfies your disclosure obligations (`../4-COMPLIANCE/01-cold-calling-law.md`).
- Add **"on a recorded line"** if you're recording and any party may be in a two-party
  consent state.

### Beat 3 — Why you're calling

**The most important sentence in the call.** It must be:

| Requirement | Why |
|---|---|
| **Specific** | Generic reads as mass-dialed |
| **True** | See the entire Never-Say List |
| **About them** | Not about your company or your rates |
| **Short** | One sentence |

| ❌ Weak | ✅ Strong |
|---|---|
| "Following up on an inquiry that was submitted" | "You filled out a request on LendingTree on March 3rd about pulling cash out" |
| "We're offering great rates" | "I'm calling homeowners on Cherry Lane because three houses there closed above $540 this quarter" |
| "Checking if you need a mortgage" | "You and I talked in April — you had a 3.1% and were waiting on rates" |

### Beat 4 — The easy question

End with a question that costs nothing to answer. You need them **talking**, not
deciding.

| ✅ Good | ❌ Bad |
|---|---|
| "Is that still something you're looking at?" | "Would you like to refinance today?" |
| "Did you ever get that sorted out?" | "Can I get your Social Security number?" |
| "Am I catching you at a terrible time?" | "Do you want to hear about our rates?" |

**"Did you ever get that sorted out?"** is the best opening question in aged-lead calling.
It's warm, it assumes a shared history that genuinely exists, and the answer is
informative either way.

## Complete openings

### Aged internet lead (your primary source)

> *"Hello, Michael?* **[pause]** *Hi Michael, my name is [Name], I'm with [Company],
> NMLS [ID], on a recorded line. You filled out a request back on [date] about pulling
> some cash out of the house. I know that was a while ago — did you ever get that sorted
> out?"*

Everything true. Everything specific. Ends with an easy question.

**Why "did you ever get that sorted out" is so effective:** if they did, you learn it
immediately and can pivot to the future. If they didn't, you've just reminded them of an
unfinished problem — and you're the person calling about it.

### Fresh internet lead (under an hour)

> *"Michael? Hi, this is [Name] with [Company], NMLS [ID]. You just put in a request about
> [purpose] a few minutes ago — I'm the guy who picked it up. Figured I'd call before you
> got buried in calls from everyone else. What are you trying to get done?"*

Speed is the entire advantage. Name it out loud.

### Past client / database

> *"Michael, it's [Name] — I did your loan back in [year]. How's the house treating you?"*

Warmest opening available. Use the database.

### Referral

> *"Michael? Hi, this is [Name] with [Company]. [Referrer] gave me your number — she said
> you two were talking about [thing]. She speaks very highly of you."*

Lead with the referrer's name immediately. It's your whole credibility.

### Genuinely cold (public data, no prior relationship)

> *"Michael? Hi — my name's [Name], I'm a mortgage broker here in [city], NMLS [ID]. I'll
> be straight with you: you didn't call me. I'm calling homeowners in [neighborhood]
> because [specific true reason]. Give me thirty seconds and then tell me to get lost if
> you want. Fair?"*

**The honesty is the technique.** "You didn't call me" is so unexpected that it buys the
thirty seconds by itself. And it satisfies your TSR disclosure obligation cleanly.

## The first five responses you'll get

### "I'm not interested."
Reflexive, delivered before they know what you are. **One** attempt:

> *"Totally fair — you don't know what this is yet. Ten seconds: [specific reason]. If
> that's not you, I'll let you go. Is it?"*

**If they repeat it, stop.** `../4-COMPLIANCE/01-cold-calling-law.md`.

### "How did you get my number?"
A trust checkpoint. Answer completely honestly and offer to remove them.

> *"Fair question. You filled out a request on [site] on [date] and it came through their
> network. If you don't remember doing that, say so and I'll take you off right now."*

### "Now's not a good time."
Usually true.

> *"Of course — I called out of nowhere. Sixty seconds now, or tell me a better time?"*

### "I already have a lender."
The most common real objection. Expect it on most calls.

> *"Good — you should. I'm not calling to get you to leave them. One question: have you
> locked yet?"*

### Silence / "...yeah?"
They're deciding. **Slow down and get warmer**, don't speed up.

> *"I know, cold call — I'll be quick and I'll be straight with you."*

## Voice mechanics for the first fifteen seconds

- **Stand up.** Changes your pitch and energy measurably.
- **Smile physically.** It's audible.
- **Slower than feels natural.** Nerves make you fast; fast reads as junk.
- **Pause after your name**, and after "Michael?" Pauses signal confidence.
- **Downward inflection.** No uptalk. *"My name is [Name]."* ↓ not ↑.
- **Lower register.** Especially important if you're young — and you are.

Full drills in `04-tonality.md`.

## The drill

Take your opening and say it out loud **fifty times before your first block.** Not read —
said.

Then record yourself once and listen. Ask three things:
1. Do I sound certain, or am I asking permission?
2. Do I sound like a person, or like a script?
3. Would *I* keep listening?

Your opening is the one piece of this job you will use on every single call, thousands of
times a year. **It deserves more practice than anything else you do.**

---

## What you must be able to do

- Deliver a four-beat opening in under fifteen seconds
- State the specific true reason for the call in one sentence
- End with a question that costs nothing to answer
- Handle all five common first responses
- Recognize a revocation in the first fifteen seconds and stop

**Self-check:** Rewrite this opening: *"Hi, is this Michael? Great — I'm calling from
Mortgage One, we're a broker with over 50 lenders and we're reaching out about lowering
your interest rate. Do you have a few minutes?"*

> *"Michael?* **[pause]** *Hi Michael, this is [Name] with Mortgage One, NMLS [ID]. You
> put in a request on [site] on [date] about pulling cash out of the house — I know
> that's been a minute. Did you ever get that sorted out?"*
>
> *Fixed: name confirmation with a pause, NMLS ID included, specific and true reason
> instead of a category pitch, no weak permission ask, and an easy question that gets
> them talking. Also dropped "lowering your interest rate" — at 6.8%, most legacy
> borrowers can't lower anything, and leading with it makes you sound like you don't know
> the market.*

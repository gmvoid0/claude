## The positioning claim — read this before the script

**What you cannot say:** *"I'll get you the best rate available."*

Three reasons:

1. **It's a promise of a material term you can't substantiate** before pulling credit and
   pricing. UDAAP-deceptive, and a Reg N / MAP Rule problem in any marketing.
2. **It isn't true.** You have ~50 lenders, not all of them. Credit unions, portfolio
   banks, and retention desks sometimes beat wholesale outright.
3. **Borrowers have heard it from every caller.** It's the most-used line in mortgage
   telemarketing, which means it carries zero information.

**What you can say — and it's stronger:**

> *"I work with about fifty lenders and they compete for your file. Sometimes a bank still
> wins, and when that happens I'll tell you. But at a bank you only ever see one price,
> and you never find out what the other forty-nine would have said."*

That's falsifiable, specific, and almost nobody says the middle clause. **"If I can't beat
it, I'll tell you to stay where you are"** is the single highest-trust sentence available
to you on a cold call.

# THE SCRIPT

## 1 · Open — 15 seconds

> **"Hello, Michael?"**
>
> *[PAUSE. Let them say yes.]*
>
> **"Hi Michael, my name's Sam, I'm with Mortgage One Funding, NMLS [YOUR ID] — I'm on a
> recorded line. You filled out a request on [SITE] back on [DATE] about [PURPOSE]. I know
> that was a while ago — did you ever get that sorted out?"**

**Mechanics:**
- Name confirm, then **pause**. Don't run through it.
- Slow down on "Sam." It's your first credibility signal.
- The NMLS ID is your legitimacy marker. Almost nobody says it.
- **The reason must be literally true.** If they didn't fill out a form, you don't say they
  did — go to the cold-open variant at the bottom.
- *"Did you ever get that sorted out?"* is warm, easy, and informative either way.

---

## 2 · Frame — 30 seconds

> **"So here's what I'd like to do, and then you can tell me if it's worth your time. I'm a
> mortgage broker — I work with about fifty lenders and they compete for your file rather
> than me having one rate sheet to sell you. I'd like to ask you six questions so I
> actually understand what you're trying to accomplish. Then I'll either show you numbers,
> or I'll tell you to stay exactly where you are. Either way you'll know in about ten
> minutes. Fair?"**

**Why every clause is there:**
- Bounds the time → defuses "I don't have time"
- Sets the agenda → the questions stop feeling like an interrogation
- **"or I'll tell you to stay exactly where you are"** → preemptive strike; instant trust
- **"Fair?"** → your first small yes

---

## 3 · The rate-honesty beat — 20 seconds

**This is the most important part of the script in the 2026 market.**

> **"And one thing up front, because most people don't know this and it changes the whole
> conversation. If your current rate starts with a three or a four, I can't beat it —
> nobody can, the market's at about six-eight. So this isn't a 'lower your rate' call.
> This is about whether there's a way to get you what you need without touching that rate
> at all. There usually is."**

**What this does in twenty seconds:**
- Removes you from the "another guy promising a lower rate" category
- Corrects their wrong belief *before* they form an objection
- Plants the second-lien solution before they can object to a refinance
- Makes everything you say afterward more credible

---

## 4 · Discovery — half the call

**Ask in this order. Purpose before facts.**

### Purpose — never skip these
> **"What got you looking at this in the first place?"**
>
> **"What would a successful outcome look like to you?"**
>
> **"What's the money actually for?"** ← *the question nobody asks*
>
> **"What happens if this doesn't get done?"**
>
> **"Is there a timeline, or are you just exploring?"**

### Situation
> **"How long have you been in the house?"**
>
> **"Is this your primary residence?"**
>
> **"Is anyone else part of this decision?"** ← *prevents the spouse objection later*

### Facts
> **"What's the balance on the mortgage right now?"**
>
> **"You'd know the area better than I would — what do you think it'd appraise for today?"**
>
> **"What's your rate, and is it a 30-year fixed?"**
>
> **"W-2, 1099, or self-employed?"**
> → *if self-employed:* **"How long have you owned it? What's the entity? Are your returns
> filed? And in a normal month, how much is deposited into the business account — gross
> deposits, not profit?"**
>
> **"Roughly where's your credit? Not judging it — score drives pricing in tiers and I want
> to aim at the right one."**
>
> **"Any late payments in the last year?"** ← *then* **SHUT UP AND LET THEM TALK**

### If they're consolidating debt
> **"Give me the balances and the rates on everything — cards, car, personal loans, all of
> it."**

*You cannot make the consolidation argument without these. The mortgage rate is the wrong
number; the blended rate is the sale.*

---

## 5 · Diagnose — tell them one true thing

Pick whichever fits:

| Their situation | What you say |
|---|---|
| **Legacy rate + needs cash** | *"You've got a 3.1%. Nobody can beat that. So the question is whether we get you the cash without touching it — and there's a way."* |
| **Veteran w/ disability** | *"You're at 10% disability — that makes you exempt from the VA funding fee entirely. On a loan this size that's thousands of dollars most people never find out about."* |
| **Self-employed** | *"Underwriting doesn't look at what your business brings in, it looks at what you show after write-offs. Which is why good business owners look poor on paper."* |
| **"Waiting for rates to drop"** | *"When they do, a few million more buyers come back into the market and start bidding against you. The people who win aren't the ones who waited."* |
| **Denied elsewhere** | *"One lender's overlay isn't the market. I've got fifty and they don't share rules. What did they say the reason was?"* |
| **Late payment w/ a cause** | *"A documented bank error is a completely different conversation with underwriting than 'I forgot to pay.' Do you have anything in writing?"* |

---

## 6 · Position — use their own words

> **"So earlier you said the goal was [THEIR EXACT WORDS], and that [THEIR STATED
> PRIORITY] mattered more than the rate. Is that still right?"**
>
> *[wait for the yes]*
>
> **"Okay. Then here's how I'd structure this."**

**Take notes in discovery so you can do this.** Quoting them back is the most powerful
move in the call.

---

## 7 · Present — payment first

> **"Here's what it looks like. [LOAN AMOUNT], 30-year fixed, payment lands at [PITI] with
> taxes and insurance. That's [DELTA] versus what you pay now, and it puts [CASH] in your
> hand. Rate's [RATE]."**
>
> *[STOP TALKING]*

**Order: payment → benefit → delta → rate → silence.** They feel payment. Rate is
abstract. And whatever they say into that silence is the most valuable thing in the call.

---

## 8 · Close + lock

> **"Let's get credit pulled so I can firm this up — takes about five minutes. Do you have
> time now?"**
>
> *[silence]*

Then **always**:

> **"Here's what happens next. I'll have your real numbers by [TIME] and I'll text them
> from a [YOUR AREA CODE] number so you know it's me. Then I'll call you at [SPECIFIC
> TIME]. Before then, [SPECIFIC TASK]. If [TIME] doesn't work, tell me now and I'll make
> it whenever does."**

**Time + task + channel. Every call. No exceptions.** All four analyzed transcripts failed
this, and it's where the most deals leak.

# BRANCHES

## "I'm already working with someone"
> **"Good — you should be. I'm not calling to get you to leave them. One question: have you
> locked your rate yet?"**

**Not locked** → *"Then nothing's lost by having one number to compare. If theirs is
better, I'll tell you to stay and I'll mean it. Four questions?"*

**Locked** → *"Then stay put — breaking a lock this late usually costs more than it saves.
When are you closing? Put my number in your phone: if anything goes sideways, call me. And
when rates come down, I'll call you. Whoever's doing this one probably won't."*

## "What's your rate?"
> **"I'll get you a real number, and I don't want to give you a fake one. Rate depends on
> your score, your equity, the purpose, and occupancy — it's literally a pricing grid.
> Anyone who fires a number at you before knowing those is guessing, and it'll change.
> Four questions and I'll give you something I can stand behind. Fair?"**

## "I never filled anything out"
> **"You may well be right, and I apologize — these come through aggregators and the data
> isn't always clean. Let me just be straight about why I'm calling, and if it's not useful
> I'll let you go. Fair?"**

**Believe them immediately. Never argue provenance.** That's what killed Call 2.

## "How did you get my number?"
> **"Fair question. You filled out a request on [SITE] on [DATE] and it came to us through
> their network. If you don't remember doing that, tell me and I'll take you off the list
> right now, no problem."**

## "I already did it / closed with someone else"
> **"Congratulations. Who'd you end up going with?"** *[listen]* **"And how'd that go?"**
> *[listen]*
>
> **"Glad it worked out. Do me a favor and put my number in your phone — rates are
> projected toward the low sixes by year end, and whoever did your loan won't call you when
> that happens. I will."**
>
> **"One other thing — anybody else in your world buying or refinancing right now?"**

*Someone who just closed has been talking about mortgages for two months. Highest-yield
referral moment in the business.*

## "It never happened / I got denied"
> **"What stopped it?"** *[listen carefully]*

**"I got denied" is the best answer you can hear.** One lender's overlay is not the market,
and you have fifty. Ask what the stated reason was and whether they still have the adverse
action letter.

## "Not interested" — first time
> **"Totally fair — you don't know what this is yet. Ten seconds and then you can decide:
> I'm a mortgage broker, and I'm calling about [SPECIFIC TRUE REASON]. If that's not you,
> I'll let you go. Is it?"**

## 🛑 "Not interested" — second time · "Take me off your list" · "Stop calling"
> **"Understood — I'll take you off our list right now. Sorry to have bothered you."**

**STOP. Log it. Internal DNC. Permanently.** No pitch, no last word. This is a revocation
with statutory force, not an objection.

# COLD OPEN — no prior inquiry

If there's no form, no inquiry, no relationship — **do not invent one.**

> **"Michael? Hi — my name's Sam, I'm a mortgage broker with Mortgage One Funding here in
> [CITY], NMLS [YOUR ID]. I'll be straight with you: you didn't call me. I'm reaching out
> to homeowners in [AREA] because [SPECIFIC TRUE REASON]. Give me thirty seconds and then
> tell me to get lost if you want. Fair?"**

**The honesty is the technique.** "You didn't call me" is so unexpected it buys you the
thirty seconds by itself — and it satisfies your disclosure obligation cleanly.

# THE THREE THINGS THAT WILL MAKE OR BREAK YOU

1. **Say the rate truth first.** *"I can't beat your 3%. Nobody can."* Highest-trust
   sentence in this market, and it's true.
2. **Ask what the money is for.** All four analyzed transcripts requested cash — a combined
   ~$470,000 — and not one established the purpose. Without it you have a quote request,
   not a sale.
3. **Never promise a rate before pricing.** *"If I can't beat it, I'll tell you"* converts
   better than any guarantee, and it can't cost you your license.

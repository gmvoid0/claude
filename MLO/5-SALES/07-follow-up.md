# Follow-Up and Speed to Lead

Most loans are lost here, not on the first call. This is the least glamorous document in
the system and probably the most profitable.

---

## Speed to lead

For an inbound or fresh internet lead, response time is the single largest controllable
variable in your conversion rate. The classic lead-response research (Harvard Business
Review / MIT-InsideSales) found order-of-magnitude differences between responding within
minutes versus within an hour.

> `[Directional, and widely cited. Treat as a principle, not a statistic to quote to a
> borrower.]`

**The mechanism is simple and doesn't require a study:** a mortgage shopper who fills out
a form fills out several. Whoever calls first frames the entire comparison, and everyone
after that is measured against them.

**Operating rules:**

| Lead type | Target response |
|---|---|
| Inbound call | Answer it. Always. |
| Fresh web lead | **Under 5 minutes** |
| Referral from a partner | Under 15 minutes |
| Aged lead | Scheduled block — see `12-aged-lead-playbook.md` |

**Call first, then text, then email — within the first five minutes.** Not sequentially
over three days. All three, immediately. The text is what gets answered when the call
doesn't.

> *"Hi Michael — [Name] with [Company], NMLS [ID]. Just tried you about the mortgage
> request you submitted a few minutes ago. I'm at this number when you're free."*

---

## Why the transcripts leak here

Every one of the four calls ends with a soft next step.

> *"I can shoot you a call back in about 15 minutes."*
> *"If I don't answer, shoot me a text and I'll call you when I get done."*
> *"That's not a problem. I'll get the numbers put together."*
> *"I'll call you back maybe half hour, hour."*

**None of those is an appointment.** They're intentions. And the borrower in Call 1
explicitly hands over control: *"if you want to shoot me a text message when you got it
all put together, try calling me, but if I don't answer, I'll call you back."*

That deal is now dependent on the borrower's initiative, which is the weakest possible
position.

**What credit is due:** the 248-area-code text bridge is genuinely excellent.

> *"I'll send you a text from my personal cell. It'll come from a 248 area code. I'm sure
> you're getting quite a few calls, so that way you can tell it's me."*

That acknowledges their real experience, gives them a recognition token, and makes the
callback expected. **Keep it. Just attach it to a specific time.**

---

## The three elements of every next step

Never end a call without all three.

| Element | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | "later today" | "4:15" |
| **Task** | *(none)* | "pull your last two bank statements" |
| **Channel** | "I'll reach out" | "text from a 248 number, then a call" |

**The full version:**

> *"Here's what happens. I'll have your numbers by 2:00 and I'll text them from a 248
> number so you know it's me. Then I'll call you at 4:15 when you're off the job. Before
> then, grab your last two bank statements — if the tax returns aren't filed we'll need
> them. If 4:15 doesn't work, tell me now and I'll make it whenever does."*

**The task matters more than people think.** A borrower who does homework for you has
invested, and investment predicts closing far better than enthusiasm does.

## The follow-up cadence

Most LOs give up after two attempts. Most deals close after five or more. That gap is
where your competitive advantage lives — not because persistence is clever, but because
almost nobody does it.

### For a live prospect (talked, no application yet)

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 | Call → text → email, all within 5 minutes of the conversation |
| 0 (+4 hrs) | The promised callback, at the promised time |
| 1 | Call. Different time of day than yesterday. |
| 2 | Text with something of **value**, not a check-in |
| 4 | Call + voicemail |
| 7 | Email with a market note or a relevant number |
| 10 | Call |
| 14 | The "closing the file" message (below) |
| 30 | Value touch, no ask |
| 60/90 | Value touch |
| Quarterly | Forever |

### The two rules that make cadence work

**1. Vary the time of day.** Someone unreachable at 10 a.m. every day may be entirely
reachable at 6 p.m. Calling the same slot repeatedly is how you conclude a reachable
person is unreachable.

**2. Never send an empty check-in.** "Just checking in!" is worthless and signals you
have nothing to offer. Every touch carries something:

- *"Rates moved down about an eighth this morning — thought of your file."*
- *"That lender I mentioned just loosened their bank-statement guidelines. Worth another
  look."*
- *"Saw a house on Derby sell for $312k — that supports the value we talked about."*

### The 14-day "closing the file" message

The highest-response message in mortgage follow-up:

> *"Michael — I don't want to be the guy who keeps calling. I'm going to close out your
> file unless I hear otherwise. If the timing changed or you went another direction,
> totally fine — just let me know so I stop bugging you. If you still want the numbers,
> say the word and I'll pick it right back up."*

It works because it's a takeaway, it's honest, and it releases them from the awkwardness
of ghosting. A meaningful share of responses are *"no no, I still want to do it."*

## Channel behavior

| Channel | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Call** | Anything real | Still the highest-conversion channel |
| **Text** | Confirmations, quick answers, recognition | Highest open rate. **Consent and revocation rules apply** — see below. |
| **Email** | Documents, numbers in writing, LEs | Low response, good record |
| **Voicemail** | When they don't pick up | Under 20 seconds |

### Texting compliance

Texts are calls under the TCPA. Everything in
`../4-COMPLIANCE/01-cold-calling-law.md` applies:

- You need consent
- **"Stop," "don't text me," "remove me"** — revocation, honor immediately across
  channels
- Honor within **10 business days**; one confirmation message only
- **8 a.m.–8 p.m.** in *their* time zone (the stricter mini-TCPA window, applied
  everywhere)

### The voicemail that gets returned

Under 20 seconds. Curiosity, not a pitch.

> *"Michael, it's [Name] with [Company] — I've got the numbers we talked about and there's
> one thing in here I want to make sure you see before you decide anything. [Number].
> I'll try you again around four."*

**Why it works:** specific ("the numbers we talked about"), creates one open loop
("one thing I want you to see"), and states when you'll call again so they're not
obligated to call back.

**Never leave a rate on voicemail.** You've given away your only reason to speak.

## The database — the real business

Everything above is about one deal. This is about your career.

**Every person who ever gives you their phone number goes in the CRM. Forever.**
Including — especially — the ones who said no.

| Segment | Cadence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Funded clients** | Quarterly, minimum | Refis, next purchase, referrals. EBR runs 18 months. |
| **Applied, didn't close** | Monthly for 6 months, then quarterly | Circumstances change constantly |
| **Quoted, said no** | Quarterly | Their rate reason may expire |
| **Wrong timing** | At the stated time, then quarterly | Best-converting segment in the file |
| **Revoked / DNC** | **Never** | Suppression list. Permanent. Non-negotiable. |

That last row is a hard stop. Consent revocation does not decay.

**The arithmetic that should motivate you:** an LO with 300 past clients who touches them
quarterly generates a meaningful volume of inbound refinance and referral business with
zero dialing. An LO with 300 past clients they never contact generates approximately
none. The difference is entirely follow-up discipline, and it compounds annually.

Post-trigger-lead-ban (`../4-COMPLIANCE/02-the-trigger-lead-ban.md`), with lead costs up
~45%, **the database is the most valuable asset in the business.** Build it from your
first week.

## The rate-drop trigger list

Specific to this market and worth building now.

Every borrower who says *"I'll wait for rates to drop"* goes on a list with their
current rate and their target. When the market moves, you call **everyone on that list in
one afternoon.**

> *"Michael, you told me in August you'd move if you saw a six and a quarter. It's there
> today. Do you want me to lock it?"*

That is the easiest call you will ever make. And in a market projected to reach 6.2–6.5%
by year end, this list is going to pay.

**A "no" today is inventory. Treat it that way.**

## What you must be able to do

- Respond to a fresh web lead in under five minutes, on three channels
- End every call with time + task + channel
- Run a multi-touch cadence without a single empty check-in
- Write the 14-day file-closing message
- Segment a database and honor suppression permanently
- Maintain a rate-drop trigger list

**Self-check:** You've called a prospect four times over ten days. No answer, no
response. What now?

> *Send the file-closing message. It's honest, it's a takeaway, and it converts better
> than a fifth unanswered call. If they don't respond, move them to a quarterly value
> touch — do not delete them. And check whether you've been calling the same time of day
> every attempt; if so, that's your actual problem.*

# Voicemail, Text, and Email

Most contact attempts don't reach a human. What you do with the other 85% decides whether
the list pays.

---

## Voicemail

### Should you leave one?

**Yes — but not every time.**

| Attempt | Leave a message? |
|---|---|
| 1 | Yes |
| 2 | No |
| 3 | Yes (different message) |
| 4 | No |
| 5 | Yes (the file-closing message) |

Leaving one every time makes you a nuisance. Leaving none means you never register.

### The rules

1. **Under 20 seconds.** Longer gets deleted mid-listen.
2. **Curiosity, not a pitch.** The goal is a callback, not a decision.
3. **Never leave a rate.** You've given away your only reason to talk.
4. **Say your number twice** — once early, once at the end.
5. **State when you'll call again.** It removes the obligation to call back, which
   paradoxically increases callbacks.
6. **Slow down on the number.** Nobody replays a voicemail to catch a rushed number.

### The scripts

**Attempt 1 — aged lead:**
> *"Michael, [Name] with [Company], NMLS [ID] — [number]. You'd filled out a request back
> in [month] about the house. Just wanted to see whether you ever got it handled. Again
> that's [number]. I'll try you tomorrow afternoon."*

**Attempt 3 — different angle:**
> *"Michael, [Name] again, [number]. Not trying to bug you — one specific reason I called
> back. If your current rate starts with a three, there's a way to get cash out without
> touching it that most people don't know about. Thought it was worth thirty seconds.
> [number]."*

**After a real conversation:**
> *"Michael, [Name] — I've got your numbers put together and there's one thing in here I
> want to make sure you see before you decide anything. [number]. I'll try you again
> around four."*

**Attempt 5 — the file closer:**
> *"Michael, [Name] with [Company]. I don't want to be the guy who keeps calling, so I'm
> going to close out your file. If the timing changed, no problem at all. If you still
> want those numbers, [number] and I'll pick it right back up. Either way, good luck with
> it."*

**The last one gets the highest callback rate of any voicemail in mortgage.** It's a
takeaway, it's honest, and it releases them from the awkwardness of ignoring you.

## Text

Highest open rate of any channel. Also the highest compliance risk.

### The compliance layer

**Texts are calls under the TCPA.** Everything in
`../4-COMPLIANCE/01-cold-calling-law.md` applies:

- **Consent required** before the first message
- **"Stop," "remove me," "don't text me"** = revocation. Honor **immediately**, across
  all channels, permanently.
- Honor within **10 business days**; **one** confirmation message only
- **8 a.m.–8 p.m.** in the recipient's time zone — use the stricter mini-TCPA window
  everywhere
- Log every revocation the moment it happens

**Carrier rules add another layer.** Messaging carriers independently filter and block
traffic. Mortgage messaging is a regulated-content category and gets scrutinized. Use
your company's approved messaging platform, never a personal texting app for volume, and
never try to evade filtering by rotating numbers or misspelling words — that's a fast
route to a permanent block.

### The rules

1. **Short.** Two or three lines.
2. **Always identify yourself and your company.** Every message.
3. **No rates in a text.** Ever.
4. **One question**, easy to answer.
5. **No links** in a first message — they trip carrier filters and read as spam.
6. **Never text before or after a call you weren't permitted to make.**

### The scripts

**Immediately after a no-answer on a fresh lead:**
> *"Hi Michael — [Name] with [Company], NMLS [ID]. Just tried you about the mortgage
> request you submitted. I'm at this number when you're free."*

**The text bridge (after a conversation, before a callback):**
> *"Michael — [Name] with [Company]. Saving my number so you know it's me when I call at
> 4:15. Working on your numbers now."*

**Value touch:**
> *"Michael — [Name] with [Company]. Rates came down about an eighth this morning and I
> thought of your file. Worth a look?"*

**The file closer:**
> *"Michael — [Name] with [Company]. Going to close out your file so I stop bugging you.
> If timing changed, no worries at all. If you still want the numbers, just say the word."*

**Response to a revocation — the only correct one:**
> *"Understood — removing you now. Sorry for the trouble."*

Then stop. Forever. Across every channel.

## Email

Lowest response rate, but it's the right channel for anything that needs to be *read* or
*kept*.

### What email is for

| Good | Bad |
|---|---|
| Loan Estimates and documents | First contact |
| Numbers in writing | Anything urgent |
| Market updates to a database | Trying to start a conversation |
| Follow-up after a real call | Rate quotes to a stranger |
| A paper record | Anything requiring a decision today |

### The rules

1. **Subject line does the work.** Specific, lowercase, personal.
2. **Under 100 words** for anything that isn't a document.
3. **One ask.**
4. **Text-heavy, not designed.** Marketing-looking email goes to promotions.
5. **Never quote a rate you can't stand behind after credit.**

### The scripts

**After a first conversation:**

> **Subject:** *the numbers we talked about*
>
> *Michael —*
>
> *Good talking with you. Attached is the breakdown on both options — the cash-out refi
> and the second lien that leaves your 3.1% alone.*
>
> *Short version: the second keeps your first mortgage untouched and gets you the
> $120,000. Page two shows both side by side.*
>
> *I'll call you at 4:15 to walk through it — those numbers are easier explained than
> read.*
>
> *[Name], [Company], NMLS [ID], [phone]*

**Value touch to a database:**

> **Subject:** *rates moved this week*
>
> *Michael —*
>
> *30-year came down about a quarter point this week. Not enough to matter for your 3.1%,
> but if you're still thinking about the kitchen, second-lien pricing improved too.*
>
> *No action needed — just keeping you posted like I said I would.*
>
> *[Name]*

**"No action needed" is why people keep opening your emails.** Most LO email is a
demand. Be the one that isn't.

## The multi-channel sequence

Channels compound. Use them together, not sequentially over weeks.

**Fresh lead, first hour:**

| Minute | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 | Call |
| 1 | If no answer — text |
| 3 | Email |
| 30 | Call again |
| 120 | Call again |

**Aged lead, first ten days:**

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Call + voicemail |
| 2 | Call (no VM) |
| 3 | Call + text |
| 6 | Call (no VM) |
| 10 | Call + file-closing VM + file-closing text |

Then quarterly value touches — forever, unless they revoked.

---

## The one rule for all three channels

**Every touch carries something.**

"Just checking in!" is worthless and it signals you have nothing to offer. Every message
carries a reason:

- A market move
- A guideline change
- A specific number
- A relevant comp in their neighborhood
- A question only they can answer

If you can't think of a reason to reach out, **that's the signal you need better market
awareness**, not that you should send an empty check-in.

---

## What you must be able to do

- Leave a 20-second voicemail that gets returned
- Write the file-closing message for voicemail and text
- State the texting consent and revocation rules
- Write an email that gets opened by a past client
- Run a multi-channel sequence in the first hour of a fresh lead

**Self-check:** Fifth attempt on an aged lead, never reached them. What do you send?

> *The file-closing voicemail and text, together. "I'm going to close out your file so I
> stop bugging you — if timing changed, no worries. If you still want the numbers, say
> the word." Highest response rate of anything you'll send, because it's a takeaway and
> it's honest. If nothing comes back, move them to quarterly value touches. Don't delete
> them.*

# KPI Math

Turning an income goal into a daily action. This is the most useful arithmetic in your
career.

---

## The funnel

```
DIALS → CONTACTS → CONVERSATIONS → APPLICATIONS → LOCKS → FUNDINGS
```

Each arrow is a conversion rate. **Know all five of yours from memory.**

| Stage | Planning estimate |
|---|---|
| Dials → Contacts | 8–20% |
| Contacts → Conversations | 25–50% |
| Conversations → Applications | 10–25% |
| Applications → Locks | 40–70% |
| Locks → Fundings | 70–90% |

> `[Planning estimates to get you started. Replace with your own within two weeks — they
> vary enormously by lead type and market.]`

---

## Working backward from income

```
Annual income goal
  ÷ comp per file        = files per year
  ÷ 12                   = fundings per month
  ÷ pull-through         = applications per month
  ÷ app rate             = conversations per month
  ÷ conversation rate    = contacts per month
  ÷ contact rate         = dials per month
  ÷ 21 working days      = DIALS PER DAY
```

### Worked: $150,000/year

At $250k average loan and 150 bps → $3,750/file:

```
$150,000 ÷ $3,750     = 40 files/year
40 ÷ 12               ≈ 3.3 fundings/month
3.3 ÷ 0.55            ≈ 6 applications/month
6 ÷ 0.15              ≈ 40 conversations/month
40 ÷ 0.35             ≈ 114 contacts/month
114 ÷ 0.12            ≈ 950 dials/month
950 ÷ 21              ≈ 45 DIALS/DAY
```

**Your job is 45 dials a day.** Everything else is downstream.

### Worked: $300,000/year

Same assumptions, double the output → **90 dials/day**.

That's a lot. Which raises the better question.

## The better question

At some point "more dials" stops being the answer. Then you improve **rates**, not volume.

### The leverage comparison

Starting point: 45 dials/day, 12% contact, 35% conversation, 15% app → **3.3
fundings/month**.

| Change | New result | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Dials 45 → 60 | 4.4 fundings | Grinding, +33% time |
| **Contact rate 12% → 16%** | 4.4 fundings | Better hours, fix caller ID, better list |
| **Conversation rate 35% → 45%** | 4.2 fundings | **Rewrite the opening** |
| **App rate 15% → 20%** | 4.4 fundings | Better discovery and closing |

**Every one of those produces the same result.** Three of them cost no extra hours.

**The lesson:** a 4-point improvement in conversation rate is worth 15 extra dials a day,
forever, and it takes a week to achieve.

## The metrics that matter

### Daily
Dials · Contacts · Conversations · Appointments · Applications · **Talk time**

**Talk time is the underrated one.** A 60-dial day with 25 minutes of talk beats a
150-dial day with 8 minutes. Talk time is where learning and money happen.

### Weekly
Applications · Locks · Fundings · Pull-through · Average loan amount · Pipeline value

### Monthly
Income · Cost per funded loan · Database growth · Referral count · **Source mix**

### The source mix — the health metric

| Source | Month 3 | Month 12 |
|---|---|---|
| Cold / aged | 70% | 30% |
| Database / referral | 20% | 50% |
| Inbound | 10% | 20% |

**If your mix hasn't shifted by month twelve, you're running a treadmill, not building a
business.** Cold calling is how you build a database; the database is what replaces cold
calling.

## Pipeline value

```
Pipeline value = Σ (loan amount × comp × probability)
```

| Stage | Rough probability |
|---|---|
| Conversation, no app | 10% |
| Application | 40% |
| Locked | 75% |
| Clear to close | 95% |

**Why it matters:** it tells you whether next month is already dead. If your weighted
pipeline in mid-month is thin, no amount of effort in week four fixes it — the damage was
done three weeks ago. Pipeline is a **leading** indicator; income is a lagging one.

---

## Cost per funded loan

```
Cost per funded loan = Total lead spend ÷ Fundings
```

$800/month on leads, 3 fundings = **$267/funded loan** against ~$3,750 in comp. Healthy.

**Post-trigger-lead-ban, lead costs are up ~45% year over year**
(`../4-COMPLIANCE/02-the-trigger-lead-ban.md`). Watch this number. If it climbs past
roughly 20% of your comp per file, the answer is usually to work leads harder — more
attempts, better dispositioning — rather than to buy more.

## Building the tracker

A spreadsheet is fine. Columns:

```
Date | Dials | Contacts | Convos | Appts | Apps | Talk(min) | Notes
```

Computed:
```
Contact% | Convo% | App% | Talk/dial
```

Monthly summary:
```
Month | Apps | Locks | Fundings | Pull-through | Avg loan | Income | Lead cost | Cost/funded | Source mix
```

**Update daily. Review Friday. Summarize monthly.**

## The three numbers to know cold

If someone stops you in the hallway, you should be able to answer instantly:

1. **My contact rate** — how well my list and my number are working
2. **My conversation rate** — how well my opening is working
3. **My dials per day** — whether I'm doing the job

An LO who can't answer those is guessing. An LO who can is managing a business.

---

## What you must be able to do

- Work backward from an income goal to a daily dial count
- Identify which conversion rate to improve instead of adding dials
- Diagnose a weak funnel stage from the numbers
- Calculate pipeline value and read it as a leading indicator
- Track source mix and know why it must shift

**Self-check:** You're at 70 dials/day, 15% contact, 40% conversation, 8% app, 60%
pull-through. You want 6 fundings/month. What's the highest-leverage move?

> *Current: 70 × 21 = 1,470 dials → 220 contacts → 88 conversations → 7 apps → ~4.2
> fundings. You need 6, so you need ~10 apps.*
>
> *The app rate is the outlier — 8% against a 10–25% range, while contact and conversation
> rates are healthy. You're reaching people and getting them talking, then losing them.
> That's discovery and closing, not volume. Getting app rate from 8% to 12% gets you to
> 6.3 fundings with no additional dials. Score your calls and look at the Drivers and
> Close categories specifically.*

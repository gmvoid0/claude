# Unit 6 — Product Cards

Every product as a spec card built for memorization. Same format each time, so your brain
files them in the same slots.

**Every card has:** what it is · who it's for · the numbers · the mortgage insurance
situation · the catch · when to use it.

`[All figures as of August 2026. Guidelines and fees are revised periodically — verify
current values with your lenders and the agency sites before quoting.]`

---

# CARD 1 — CONVENTIONAL

**What it is:** The default product. Not government-insured. Underwritten to Fannie Mae
or Freddie Mac rules and sold to them.

**Who it's for:** Anyone with decent credit. The benchmark everything else is measured
against.

### The numbers
| | |
|---|---|
| Min FICO | **620** |
| Min down — primary | **3%** (first-time buyer programs) / 5% standard |
| Min down — second home | 10% |
| Min down — investment | 15–25% |
| Max DTI | 45%, to **50%** with strong AUS |
| Max LTV — purchase | 97% |
| Max LTV — cash-out | **80%** |
| 2026 loan limit | **$832,750** (1-unit) · **$1,249,125** high-cost |
| Occupancy | Primary, second home, or investment |

### Mortgage insurance
**PMI** required under 20% down. **The key advantage: it goes away.**
- Automatic termination at **78% LTV** (by original amortization schedule)
- Cancellable on request at **80% LTV**
- **Cancellable on appreciation** with a new appraisal

### PMI structures
| Type | How it works |
|---|---|
| Borrower-paid monthly | Standard. Cancellable. |
| Single premium | Lump sum upfront. Not cancellable but no monthly cost. |
| **Lender-paid (LPMI)** | Built into a higher rate. **Never cancels** — the rate is permanent. |
| Split premium | Part upfront, lower monthly |

**The LPMI trap:** it looks great (no MI line item!) but the higher rate lasts forever,
even after they hit 20% equity. Usually the worst choice for a long-term holder.

### The catch
Strictest credit requirements of the major products. Below 620 you're out.

### Use it when
FICO 680+. Above that threshold conventional almost always beats FHA on total cost,
because the MI ends.

### The 3%-down programs
**HomeReady** (Fannie) and **Home Possible** (Freddie) — 3% down, income limits tied to
area median, **reduced MI coverage**, homebuyer education usually required. Often beat
FHA for a moderate-income first-time buyer with 660+ credit.

# CARD 2 — FHA

**What it is:** Insured by the Federal Housing Administration. FHA doesn't lend — it
insures the lender against loss, which is what lets lenders accept weaker credit.

**Who it's for:** Lower credit scores, thin savings, higher DTI, or a recent credit event.

### The numbers
| | |
|---|---|
| Min FICO — 3.5% down | **580** |
| Min FICO — 10% down | **500** |
| Max DTI | 43% baseline, higher with compensating factors + AUS |
| Max LTV — purchase | **96.5%** |
| Max LTV — cash-out | **85%** |
| Occupancy | **Primary residence ONLY** |
| Loan limits | County-specific, generally below conventional |
| Gift funds | **100% of the down payment may be gifted** |

### Mortgage insurance — the defining feature
**Two premiums:**
1. **UFMIP** — 1.75% of the base loan, paid upfront, almost always financed in
2. **Annual MIP** — charged monthly

**⚠️ The critical fact: on most FHA loans with minimum down payment, annual MIP lasts
the LIFE of the loan.** It does not fall off at 20% equity like conventional PMI.

**The only escape is to refinance out of FHA entirely.**

### The catch
Two catches, and both matter:
1. **Permanent MIP** — this is the real long-run cost, and it's why FHA can be more
   expensive than conventional even when it looks cheaper on day one.
2. **Minimum property standards** — FHA appraisals are stricter. Peeling paint on a
   pre-1978 home, missing handrails, a roof near end of life, broken systems — all can
   require repair before closing. This kills purchase deals on fixer-uppers.

### Use it when
FICO under 620, or a recent credit event, or DTI needs the flexibility.

### The honest framing on "500"
FHA's floor is technically 500 — but that requires **10% down**, and virtually no
wholesale lender will actually buy that paper. Most impose overlays at 580, 600, or 620.
Saying "we go to 500" without those caveats is misleading.

### FHA Streamline Refinance
| | |
|---|---|
| Appraisal | Usually **not required** |
| Income docs | Minimal |
| Credit | Reduced requirements |
| Requirement | Must show a **net tangible benefit** |
| **Cash out** | **Not permitted** |

Fast and cheap when it applies. In 2026 it applies to almost nobody, since existing FHA
borrowers hold below-market rates.

# CARD 3 — VA

**What it is:** Guaranteed by the Department of Veterans Affairs. The best loan product
in America for those who qualify — no contest.

**Who it's for:** Veterans, active duty, National Guard/Reserves with qualifying service,
and certain surviving spouses.

### The numbers
| | |
|---|---|
| Down payment | **$0** |
| Monthly mortgage insurance | **NONE. Ever.** |
| Min FICO | No VA minimum; lender overlays typically 580–620 |
| Max DTI | Flexible — **residual income** is the real test |
| Max LTV — purchase | **100%** |
| Max LTV — cash-out | Verify current limits with lender |
| Occupancy | Primary residence |
| Seller concessions | Up to 4% + closing costs |
| **Assumable** | **YES** |

### Mortgage insurance
**None.** Instead there's a one-time **funding fee**, financeable into the loan.

**⭐ MEMORIZE: borrowers receiving VA disability compensation are EXEMPT from the funding
fee entirely.** On a $300,000 loan that's thousands of dollars, and most originators
never check.

### Two VA-specific concepts

**1. Entitlement** — the amount VA guarantees to the lender; it's what enables zero down.
- **Full entitlement:** no loan limit; as high as the lender will approve with $0 down
- **Partial entitlement:** applies if there's an existing VA loan or a prior default —
  limits return and a down payment may be needed
- Pull the **COE** (Certificate of Eligibility) early — it's fast and free, and it tells
  you the entitlement position before you build on assumptions

**2. Residual income** — VA's signature rule. After taxes, all debts, the housing
payment, and a maintenance/utility allowance, the leftover must exceed a VA minimum that
varies by **family size** and **region**.

> **Consequence:** VA will approve DTIs far above conventional if residual income is
> strong — and can DECLINE a comfortable-looking DTI if a large family in a high-cost
> region fails residual. **Never assume DTI tells you the answer on a VA file.**

### Grossing up
VA disability is **tax-free** → underwriting grosses it up (commonly ×1.25). $2,000/mo of
disability counts as $2,500 for qualifying.

### Assumability — huge right now
A qualified buyer can take over an existing VA loan **at its existing rate**. A home
carrying an assumable 3% VA loan in a 6.8% market is a genuinely valuable asset.

**Honest caveats:** the buyer must qualify with the servicer (slow), must cover the
equity gap, and **if the buyer isn't a veteran, the seller's entitlement stays tied up**
until the loan is paid off.

### IRRRL (VA Streamline)
No appraisal, minimal docs, minimal credit, must produce a net tangible benefit, **no
cash out**. Dormant in 2026 for the same reason as FHA streamline.

### Use it when
The borrower is eligible. Almost always the right answer.

# CARD 4 — USDA

**What it is:** USDA Guaranteed Rural Housing loan. Zero down, government-backed.
**The most overlooked product in America.**

**Who it's for:** Low-to-moderate income buyers in eligible areas.

### The numbers
| | |
|---|---|
| Down payment | **$0** |
| Min FICO | 640 typical (lower with manual underwriting) |
| Max DTI | 41% typical |
| Occupancy | **Primary residence only** |
| Property | Must be in a **USDA-eligible area** |
| Income | Must be **UNDER** the area/household-size limit |

### Mortgage insurance
An upfront guarantee fee plus a smaller annual fee — **cheaper than FHA MIP**.

### The two eligibility tests — BOTH must pass
1. **Property eligibility** — check the address on USDA's map. "Rural" is far broader
   than people assume; many outer-ring suburbs and towns of 20,000+ qualify.
2. **Income eligibility** — a MAXIMUM, not a minimum. Household income includes
   non-borrower adults.

### The catch
The income cap. And the map check is binary — if the address fails, it fails.

### Use it when
Zero down needed, moderate income, and the address might qualify. **Check the map first
— it takes thirty seconds and most originators never do it.** A borrower with no down
payment and a 660 score in an eligible area is being steered into FHA with permanent MIP
for no reason.

# CARD 5 — JUMBO

**What it is:** Any loan above the conforming limit. Can't be sold to Fannie/Freddie, so
it's held on bank balance sheets or sold privately — which means **the investor writes
the rules**.

**Who it's for:** High-balance borrowers with strong profiles.

### The numbers
| | |
|---|---|
| Threshold | Above **$832,750** (or the high-cost county limit) |
| Min FICO | 700+, frequently 720–740+ |
| Down payment | 10–20%+ |
| Max DTI | Often capped ~43% |
| **Reserves** | **6–12+ months of PITI** ← the defining requirement |
| Documentation | Full and thorough |
| Appraisals | Sometimes two on very large loans |

### The catch
**Reserves.** This is where jumbo files die. A borrower with strong income and a good
score but no post-closing liquidity fails, even with a fine DTI.

**Ask early:** *"After the down payment and closing costs, roughly what's left in savings,
investments, and retirement?"*

### Why the broker channel matters most here
Conventional guidelines are identical everywhere — Fannie's rulebook is Fannie's rulebook.
**Jumbo guidelines vary enormously between investors.** One wants 12 months of reserves;
another wants 6. One caps DTI at 40%; another allows 45% with compensating factors. A
"no" at one bank says almost nothing about the rest of the market.

# CARD 6 — NON-QM

**What it is:** Loans outside the Qualified Mortgage safe harbor. They use **alternative
documentation**.

**⚠️ Non-QM is NOT subprime.** Post-2008 non-QM still requires a full ability-to-repay
determination — it just gets there differently. Most non-QM borrowers have strong credit
and real assets; they simply can't document income like a W-2 employee.

**The trade:** rates run meaningfully above conventional (roughly 1–1.5%+), down payments
are larger, reserves higher. In exchange, the loan actually closes.

## 6A — Bank Statement Loans
**The most important non-QM product.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Documentation | **12 or 24 months of bank statements** |
| Tax returns | **NOT REQUIRED** |
| Account | Business (typical) or personal |
| Expense factor | Commonly ~50%; a CPA P&L can justify lower |
| Min FICO | 620+ typical |
| Down | 10–20%+ |
| Self-employment | Usually 2+ years |

**The calculation:** `(total deposits ÷ months) × (1 − expense factor)`

**The question that unlocks it:** *"In a normal month, how much is DEPOSITED into the
business account? Gross deposits — not profit, not after write-offs."*

**Why it exists:** a business owner's tax return is engineered to minimize taxable income.
Great tax strategy, terrible mortgage qualification.

## 6B — DSCR
**The property qualifies, not the borrower.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Metric | **DSCR = monthly rent ÷ monthly PITIA** |
| Typical minimum | 1.00 (some go below with more down) |
| **Personal income** | **NOT verified** — no paystubs, no returns, no DTI |
| Employment | Not verified |
| Property | **Investment only** |
| Min FICO | 620–680+ |
| Down | 20–25% |

**Why it's powerful:** an investor with ten properties has a DTI that makes conventional
impossible. DSCR ignores that entirely.

## 6C — The rest of the menu
| Product | Qualifies on |
|---|---|
| **P&L only** | CPA-prepared profit & loss statement |
| **1099 only** | 1099 forms with an expense factor |
| **Asset depletion** | Liquid assets ÷ a set number of months = "income" — for retirees and post-liquidity-event borrowers |
| **ITIN** | Borrowers with an ITIN instead of an SSN; larger down payments |
| **Foreign national** | Non-residents, investment property, no US credit, 30%+ down |
| **Recent credit event** | Bankruptcy/foreclosure more recent than conventional allows — sometimes as little as one day out |

### Positioning it honestly
Non-QM isn't equivalent to conventional and shouldn't be presented that way. It's **the
loan that gets done**. And it's often temporary: two years of clean returns later, the
borrower refinances into conventional.

# CARD 7 — SECOND LIENS (HELOAN and HELOC)

**What it is:** A loan that sits *behind* the existing first mortgage, leaving it
completely untouched.

**Who it's for:** Anyone with a low-rate first mortgage who needs cash. **In 2026 that's
most of the country.**

## The problem it solves
Homeowner has a mortgage at 2.5–4%. Needs cash. Market is ~6.8%.

- **Cash-out refi** → pays off the first, creates a bigger loan at today's rate →
  **the low rate is destroyed on the ENTIRE balance**
- **Second lien** → first mortgage untouched → **market rate applies only to the new money**

## 7A — HELOAN (fixed second)
| | |
|---|---|
| Structure | Lump sum, fixed rate, fixed payment |
| Term | 10–30 years |
| Best for | A **known, one-time** amount |
| Advantage | Predictable |
| Disadvantage | Interest on the full balance from day one |

**Use for:** debt consolidation, a defined renovation, business capital.

## 7B — HELOC (line of credit)
| | |
|---|---|
| Structure | Revolving line, like a secured credit card |
| Rate | **Variable** (usually prime + margin) |
| Draw period | ~10 years, often interest-only |
| Repayment period | 10–20 years after |
| Best for | **Unknown or ongoing** needs |
| Advantage | Only pay interest on what's drawn; reusable |
| Disadvantage | **Payment moves with rates; payment shock when the draw period ends** |

**Use for:** phased renovations, working capital, emergency reserve.

### The mechanics
```
CLTV = (first mortgage + second) ÷ value
```
Typical maximums: 85–90% strong credit primary · 80–85% moderate · 70–75% investment.

**Seconds price above firsts** because they're subordinate — in a foreclosure the first
gets paid in full before the second sees a dollar. More risk, more rate. Even so, the
blended cost usually beats destroying a legacy first.

### The honest HELOC caveat
For the first ~10 years many HELOCs are interest-only, so the payment is comfortable.
Then it converts to principal-and-interest and **the payment can jump substantially.**
If the borrower won't pay it down inside the draw period, the fixed second is safer.

### When a cash-out refi IS better
- Current rate is near or above market → nothing to protect
- Amount needed is very large relative to the first → CLTV caps bind
- Current loan is **FHA with permanent MIP** → refinancing out can be worth real money
- Borrower genuinely wants one payment and understands the cost

**The rule: price both. Show both. Recommend one and say why.**

# CARD 8 — ARMs

**What it is:** A rate fixed for an initial period, then adjusting periodically.

**Notation: 5/6, 7/6, 10/6** — first number = **years** fixed, second = **months**
between adjustments after.

### The mechanics
```
New rate = INDEX + MARGIN   (subject to CAPS)
```
- **Index** — a published market rate; most current ARMs use **SOFR**
- **Margin** — a fixed add-on set at origination, **never changes**. This is the number
  to compare between lenders — it governs the rate for the rest of the loan's life.

### Caps — written as three numbers, e.g. **2/1/5**
| Cap | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Initial** (2) | Max change at the first adjustment |
| **Periodic** (1) | Max change at each adjustment after |
| **Lifetime** (5) | Max total increase over the start rate |

**Always compute and state the worst case.** Start at 6.25% with 2/1/5 caps → absolute
ceiling is 11.25%.

### Why ARMs price below fixed
The lender isn't carrying 30 years of rate risk. That discount is real money — **for the
right borrower.**

### Who it's right for
| Situation | ARM? |
|---|---|
| Selling/moving inside the fixed period | ✅ **Strongly** |
| Expects to refinance when rates fall | ✅ Often |
| Jumbo borrower with large reserves | ✅ Often |
| Fixed income, staying 30 years | ❌ No |
| Anxious about payment changes | ❌ No |
| **Barely qualifying at the start rate** | ❌ **Absolutely not** |

### The 2008 reputation — the honest answer
Those were different instruments: negative amortization, payment-option ARMs, teaser
rates, and **no ability-to-repay requirement**. Today's ARMs are underwritten to the
fully-indexed or maximum first-adjustment rate — the borrower must qualify at the
*higher* rate, not the teaser. Genuinely different product.

# CARD 9 — BUYDOWNS AND POINTS

## 9A — Temporary buydown
Prepaid money that subsidizes the payment for the first years. **The note rate never
changes** — a fund held in escrow covers the difference each month.

| Type | Yr 1 | Yr 2 | Yr 3 | After |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2-1** | −2% | −1% | note rate | note rate |
| **3-2-1** | −3% | −2% | −1% | note rate |
| **1-0** | −1% | note rate | — | — |

**Who pays:** usually the **seller** or builder as a concession.

**⚠️ Qualification is at the NOTE rate, not the bought-down rate.** The borrower must
afford the full payment when the subsidy ends. Say this out loud every time.

**Bonus fact:** if rates fall and they refinance before the buydown expires, the unused
buydown funds are typically credited back.

## 9B — Discount points (permanent)
**1 point = 1% of the loan.** Typically buys ~0.25% off the rate — but this varies daily
with the sheet, and sometimes points are poor value. Always check the actual sheet.

```
Break-even (months) = cost ÷ monthly savings
```
$300,000 · 1 point = $3,000 · saves $50/mo → **60 months**.

Longer than break-even → points win. Shorter → they lose.

## 9C — Negative points (lender credits)
The reverse: take a higher rate, receive a credit toward closing costs. See Unit 5.

# THE DECISION TABLE — memorize this

| Borrower situation | Product |
|---|---|
| Sub-4.5% first mortgage, needs cash | **Second lien** (almost always) |
| Moving/selling within 3 years | **ARM + lender credits**, no points |
| Self-employed, heavy write-offs | **Bank statement** |
| Tax returns not filed | **Bank statement** |
| Investor, DTI maxed out | **DSCR** |
| Veteran | **VA** — and check disability for the funding fee exemption |
| Retired, assets but little income | **Asset depletion** |
| FICO under 620 | **FHA** |
| FICO 620–680 | **Run BOTH FHA and conventional** |
| FICO 680+ | **Conventional** (PMI cancels) |
| Zero down, moderate income, rural-ish address | **Check the USDA map FIRST** |
| Above the conforming limit | **Jumbo** — ask about reserves immediately |
| Cards at 20%+ | **Consolidation** — run the blended rate |
| Seller won't drop price | **Seller-paid buydown** |

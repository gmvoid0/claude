1. What this is, and what it is not

S.A.M fills your LO Mortgage Application form the way a person fills it: it finds each field by the label you can see on screen and types into it. There is no API, no integration user, and nothing is sent to any server.

Concretely, when you press To Salesforce on the panel:

  1. The panel builds a plan — which values go in which fields.
  2. The extension's background worker finds your open Salesforce tab.
  3. The copy of S.A.M running in that tab types the values in.
  4. It reports back what actually landed, field by field.
Why not the REST API The API is the better integration and it is not available to you today. It needs a Connected App, an OAuth flow, and your org's custom field API names — all three require a Salesforce administrator. Filling the rendered form needs none of that and works this afternoon. Section 12 covers what changes if you ever get admin support.
This does not submit anything S.A.M fills the form. You review it and you press Submit. Nothing is created, saved or sent in Salesforce without you doing it.

2. Before you start

  • Chrome or Edge, with S.A.M loaded unpacked (see the README).
  • You are logged into Salesforce in the same browser profile.
  • Your org is on one of these hosts — this covers every standard Salesforce deployment:
    *.my.site.com · *.lightning.force.com · *.force.com · *.salesforce.com
  • The LO Mortgage Application is open in a tab. The Experience Cloud page and the App Sheet tab on a Lead render the same component, so either works.

S.A.M does not need to be "enabled" on the Salesforce site the way it does on your dialer. On a Salesforce host it runs automatically as a receiver: it draws no panel and does nothing at all until a handoff arrives.

3. One-time setup

Three names, entered once. These are the people who are the same on every application you send, so S.A.M should not be asking you for them on every call.

  1. Right-click the S.A.M toolbar icon → Options. (Or chrome://extensions → S.A.M → Extension options.)
  2. Find the Salesforce section.
  3. Fill in Loan Officer, Transfer Agent and Loan Officer Assistant.
  4. Press Save.
Type these names exactly as Salesforce shows them S.A.M searches the lookup with the text you type here and will only select a record when exactly one result matches every word of it. "Jane Smith" is right. "Jane" will match several people and be refused. "J. Smith" will match nobody. Copy the name straight out of the Salesforce record if you are not sure.

Leave a box blank and that lookup is not attempted at all — no search, no error, nothing typed. That is the correct setting if your shop does not use, say, a Loan Officer Assistant.

The Lead lookup needs no setting. It is searched using the borrower's name from the panel.

4. Using it on a call

  1. Take the call. S.A.M reads the dialer screen and fills the application panel.
  2. Fill in anything it could not know — income, employment, the cash-out the borrower actually wants.
  3. Open the LO Mortgage Application in another tab, on the Lead you are working.
  4. Press To Salesforce at the top of the application drawer.
  5. Read the report that appears under the form. Switch to the Salesforce tab, check it, submit.
The form does not have to be open first If you press To Salesforce before opening the form, the plan is held for ten minutes. Open the application within that window and it fills itself the moment the page loads. After ten minutes the plan is dropped and you press the button again.

How the name is split

You type one name. Salesforce wants two. The split happens on the way out:

RANDY D ROLLINS      → First "RANDY D"      Last "ROLLINS"
DOROTHY VANCE-HOLLIS → First "DOROTHY"      Last "VANCE-HOLLIS"
JAMES O'BRIEN JR     → First "JAMES"        Last "O'BRIEN JR"
RANDY VAN DYKE       → First "RANDY"        Last "VAN DYKE"
ROLLINS, RANDY D     → First "RANDY D"      Last "ROLLINS"

The rule is that the last word is the surname. Suffixes (JR, SR, II, III, IV, MD, PHD, ESQ) stay with the surname; particles (VAN, VON, DE, LA, MC, ST and the like) are pulled into it; and "Last, First" is recognised rather than reversed. This round-trips your dialer's own First "RANDY D" / Last "ROLLINS" unchanged.

Figures are sent as numbers

Salesforce currency and number fields reject formatted text. Anything that is a figure is stripped of $, ,, % and spaces before being typed. Names, addresses, phone numbers and states pass through exactly as shown.

6. The four lookups

A lookup is not a text box. It stores a record id; the text you see is only a label for whatever was picked. Typing a name into one and walking away leaves a field that looks complete and holds nothing — which is worse than leaving it blank, because nobody goes back to check a filled-looking field.

So S.A.M does what you do: types the name, waits for the result list, and clicks the matching row.

LookupSearched withSet where
LeadThe borrower's full name from the panelNothing to set
Loan OfficerYour standing defaultSettings → Salesforce
Transfer AgentYour standing defaultSettings → Salesforce
Loan Officer AssistantYour standing defaultSettings → Salesforce
The exactly-one rule Searching "Richard" on a real org returns Richard Howell, Richard Sasko, Richard Warren, Richard Brownell and Richard Mendoza. Taking the first is not matching — it is guessing, and a guess here attaches your application to somebody else's file.

S.A.M requires that exactly one result contains every word of the search term. Zero matches or more than one, and the field is left untouched with the reason reported. It will never pick for you.

Lookups are filled one at a time, after the plain fields, and each takes up to four seconds to search. Four lookups can take fifteen seconds in total — that is Salesforce's search speed, not the extension waiting around. Selecting a record re-renders the form, so S.A.M re-reads the page between each one.

7. What it will never fill

These fields exist on the form and S.A.M has no honest source for any of them. They are left blank and listed back to you in the report, so you find out before you submit rather than after:

left blank Email · Middle Name · Suffix · Employer · Length of Employment · Title · Borrower DOB · SSN · Marital Status · Disability Income · Other Income · SSI

Picklists and dropdowns on the form are also left alone unless a value matches an existing option exactly.

A blank is honest; a guess is not Every one of these is a field where a plausible-looking wrong answer would survive review and land in a file. That is the failure this tool is built to avoid, so it declines rather than infers.

8. Reading the result

After a send, a coloured strip appears under the application form:

ColourWhat it saysWhat it means
GreenFilled 9 fields in Salesforce. Linked Lead, Loan Officer.Everything planned went in and every configured lookup resolved.
AmberFilled 9 fields… plus a reason per lookup, plus a count of fields that could not be matchedSomething went in, something did not. Read the detail and finish it by hand.
RedNot sent.Nothing reached Salesforce. See the next section.

"Could not be matched" means S.A.M looked for a label and your form does not render one that matches. That is a mapping question, not a failure of the send — see section 11.

It filled the wrong box

Two fields on your form share a label and S.A.M matched the first one. Fix it by hand, then send a note about which labels collided — the patterns in section 11 can be tightened to distinguish them.

It looked filled and submitted empty

This should not happen and is worth reporting immediately if it does. Salesforce components keep their own copy of the field's value, and S.A.M writes through the native property setter specifically so the component sees the change (section 10). If a field ever submits empty after showing a value, that component is doing something the current approach does not handle.

10. How it works underneath

Useful if you are debugging, explaining it to IT, or handing this to another developer.

The path a value takes

Dialer tab                      Background worker            Salesforce tab
──────────                      ─────────────────            ──────────────
You press "To Salesforce"
  planFill() builds a plan
  → SAM_HANDOFF ───────────────► held (10 min)
                                 finds a Salesforce tab
                                 → SAM_FILL_FORM ───────────► fillForm(entries)
                                                              fillLookups(lookups)
  report shown on panel ◄─────── report ◄──────────────────── what actually landed

Why a plain assignment is not enough

Setting input.value = "..." on a Salesforce field does nothing useful. The component holds its own copy of the state, and a direct assignment leaves it unaware — the box shows the text and submits blank. S.A.M therefore:

  • writes through HTMLInputElement.prototype's native value setter, which is the one the framework has instrumented;
  • dispatches input and change afterwards, which is what it listens for;
  • marks both events composed: true, because these fields live inside shadow roots and an event that does not cross that boundary never reaches the component.

The test suite asserts against what the framework's own listeners saw, not against what the DOM holds, precisely because those two can disagree.

11. Changing the mapping

If your org labels a field differently, the fix is one line. The map lives in extension/src/lib/salesforce.js:

export const FIELD_MAP = [
  { key: 'fico', labels: [/^\s*loan fico\s*$/i, /^\s*fico\s*$/i, /\bfico\b/i] },
  ...
];

Each entry lists the label patterns to try, most specific first. To teach it a new label, add a pattern to the front of that field's list. To map a field S.A.M currently ignores, add a new entry whose key is the application field name.

Lookups work the same way in LOOKUP_MAP, and the never-filled list is UNSUPPLIED. All three are plain data with no DOM involved, so they can be checked against your form without an org in front of you.

Send the exact labels your form renders and these can be updated for you — a screenshot of the form with the labels visible is enough.

12. The REST API alternative

If you ever get administrator support, the API is the better integration: it does not depend on labels, it cannot fill the wrong box, and it works without the form being open. What it requires:

  • A Connected App in your org, created by an administrator.
  • An OAuth flow — most likely the PKCE web-server flow, since a browser extension cannot hold a client secret.
  • The API names of every custom field on the application object (the __c names, not the labels).
  • A decision about who the records belong to: the agent's own session, or a service user.

The mapping work you do in section 11 is not wasted if that happens — the field list is the same, the labels are simply replaced by API names.

13. Security and privacy

  • Nothing leaves your browser. There is no server, no telemetry and no third-party call anywhere in this path. The value goes from one tab, through the extension, into another tab.
  • No lead data is stored. Settings, field bindings, saved applications and the enabled-site list are kept locally. Borrower data is held only while the record is on screen.
  • No credentials are involved. S.A.M uses the Salesforce session you are already logged into. It never sees, stores or transmits a password or token.
  • It acts only when you press the button. On a Salesforce host the extension draws nothing and does nothing until a handoff arrives.
  • It submits nothing. Review and submit are always yours.
Worth confirming with your compliance team This automates keystrokes into your own authenticated Salesforce session — the same thing you would do by hand, faster. That is ordinary browser-extension behaviour, but if your shop has a policy on automation touching the CRM, it is a five-minute conversation worth having before the floor is using it.
